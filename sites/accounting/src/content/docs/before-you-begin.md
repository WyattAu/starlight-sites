Accounting's prerequisite is not mathematics; it is **discipline**: the habit of checking that
entries balance and numbers trace to sources. The arithmetic is primary-school level; the
standard is execution without error. This page states the three structural facts the
[Foundations track](/1-foundations/) builds on, shows the one calculation you must be fluent in,
and closes with a self-test.

### 1. The Three Structural Facts

Everything in accounting follows from these:

1. **The accounting equation**: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. What the entity controls equals
   the claims against it. An invariant; true after every properly recorded event, not just at
   the end.
2. **Double entry**: every transaction posts equal debits and credits across at least two
   accounts. This is the *mechanism* that preserves the invariant transaction by transaction.
3. **The accrual principle**: revenue when earned, expenses when incurred; cash timing is not
   recognition timing. This is why the books differ from the bank statement, and why adjusting
   entries exist.

If you can state those three in your own words, you understand the subject's skeleton. The
tracks teach the execution.

### 2. The One Calculation: Running Balances

Fluency required: maintain a running balance through additions and subtractions without losing
track. Example; a cash account through four events:

<div class="table-wrap">

| Event | In | Out | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner invests | 10,000 | | 10,000 |
| Buy equipment | | 2,400 | 7,600 |
| Collect fees | 5,000 | | 12,600 |
| Pay wages | | 3,000 | 9,600 |

</div>

Every column reconciles: total in 15,000, total out 5,400, net 9,600; the final balance. If a
row's balance doesn't equal the previous balance plus in minus out, stop and find the error
*before* continuing. This checking habit, applied everywhere, is eighty percent of the subject.

### 3. Reading a Ledger as a System

Accounts don't exist alone; every event touches at least two. Buying equipment with cash
touches Cash (down) and Equipment (up): one event, two accounts, equal amounts. The system's
consistency comes from this coupling: every "where did it go" has a "where did it come from".
When you can answer both for any number, you understand the entry.

### 4. Self-test

1. A company pays \$900 for inventory on 31 March, to be sold in April. Cash effect in March?
   Expense in March? (§1, fact 3; cash −900 in March; expense \$0: the inventory is an asset
   until sold. The two statements differ on purpose.)
2. An entry debits Cash \$500 and credits Revenue \$500. Which side of the equation does each
   touch? (§1, facts 1–2; an asset up, equity up: both sides grow by 500.)
3. A running balance shows 4,200 after a row whose In was 1,500 and Out was 2,300. What was the
   previous balance? (§2; 4,200 = previous + 1,500 − 2,300 → previous = 5,000.)
4. Why is the trial balance not proof the books are right? (§2's checking has limits; it
   verifies equal totals, not correct classification; a debit posted to the wrong account still
   balances.)

### 5. Vocabulary to Pre-Load

Three distinctions that prevent the first week's confusion: **receivable vs payable** (money
owed TO the entity vs owed BY it; receivables are assets, payables are liabilities);
**expense vs expenditure** (the recognition of a cost when incurred vs the cash payment -
they can happen in different periods); **profit vs cash** (net income is accrual-based and
never equals the change in the bank balance; the cash flow statement exists precisely because
of this).

The [Foundations track](/1-foundations/) defines each at its point of use; the
[glossary](/glossary/) collects them.

### 6. What You Can Skip

If the running-balance table in §2 took you under a minute and you answered all four self-test
items without looking back, go straight to [Foundations](/1-foundations/); the track starts
from zero and you are past it. The self-test exists so that "skip" is an assessment, not an
assumption.

### 7. The Standard, Restated

Every number in the tracks traces to a source entry, every entry balances, and every statement
interlocks. The self-test's role is to move that standard from the pages into your working
habits: post it, check it, then trust it.

### 8. Why No Mathematics Track Is Required

The [finance](https://finance.wyattau.com/before-you-begin/) and
[economics](https://economics.wyattau.com/before-you-begin/) siblings require probability and
calculus prerequisites; accounting requires none, and that is honest rather than diminishing -
the subject's rigour is structural (does it balance, does it trace, is it classified right), not
mathematical. Where an accounting standard embeds a calculation (present value in lease
accounting, for instance), the pages teach the calculation at the point of use.
