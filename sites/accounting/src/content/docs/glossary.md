- **Accrual principle** — revenue is recognised when earned and expenses when incurred, whether
  or not cash has moved. The gap between accrual and cash is why adjusting entries exist.
- **Accounting equation** — Assets = Liabilities + Equity. An invariant: every properly posted
  entry preserves it, because each entry's debits equal its credits.
- **Adjusting entries** — period-end entries recording earned-but-unrecorded revenue and
  incurred-but-unpaid expense (accruals), and allocated costs (deferrals, depreciation).
- **Closing entries** — period-end entries transferring revenue and expense balances to equity,
  resetting the temporary accounts so the next period starts clean.
- **Credit / Debit** — the two sides of an entry. Debits increase assets and expenses; credits
  increase liabilities, equity, and revenue. Memorising the rule is easier than deriving it: the
  sides are defined so that the equation's invariant is preserved.
- **Double entry** — every transaction posts equal debits and credits across at least two
  accounts. This is the mechanism that preserves the accounting equation transaction by
  transaction.
- **General ledger** — the collection of accounts to which journal entries are posted; the
  trial balance is struck from its balances.
- **Retained earnings** — the cumulative equity account absorbing each period's net income less
  distributions; the bridge from the income statement to the balance sheet.
- **Revenue recognition** — the rules deciding *when* performance has occurred enough to book
  revenue; the single most litigated area of the subject, and the reason the accrual principle
  needs standards rather than a slogan.
- **Trial balance** — the list of account balances with total debits and total credits. Equal
  totals verify the invariant held through posting; unequal totals prove an error.

Missing a term? The page using it defines it; the glossary collects the shared vocabulary.

## Conventions

Entries are shown in two-column journal form (debit account and amount, indented credit account
and amount). Statements appear in the standard order: income statement, statement of changes in
equity, balance sheet, cash flow statement — and the pages keep that order, because the
interlocks flow in it. Amounts are in a generic currency unit; nothing in the mechanics depends
on which.

## Statement terms

- **Balance sheet** — the statement of the accounting equation at a point in time: assets on one
  side, liabilities and equity on the other, equal by construction when the entries are right.
- **Income statement** — the period's revenues minus expenses, arriving at net income. Its
  bottom line flows into retained earnings, which is where it reaches the balance sheet.
- **Cash flow statement** — the period's cash movements, sorted into operating, investing, and
  financing. It is the reconciliation between accrual income and actual cash: net income plus
  the accrual reversals.

## How entries are ordered

Entries are alphabetical within the single list, because accounting's vocabulary is one shared
system rather than separate tracks. Each entry names the track that owns the term's deepest
treatment, so the glossary doubles as a subject map.

## The standard, restated

Every entry says what the thing is and what it does to the equation. That pairing — object and
effect — is the subject in miniature, and the glossary keeps the pairing visible even at
dictionary length.

- **Ledger account** — the running record of one asset, liability, equity, revenue, or expense
  category; its balance is what the trial balance reports and the statements consume.
- **Posting** — transferring a journal entry's amounts into the ledger accounts. Posting
  correctly preserves the invariant; posting to the wrong account preserves the totals but
  corrupts the statements — which is why the trial balance alone is not an audit.

- **Source document** — the invoice, receipt, or contract that authorises an entry; the evidence
  layer that keeps posting honest, and the reason "the books" are trusted in court.
- **Statement of changes in equity** — the bridge statement: opening equity, plus net income,
  minus distributions, plus contributions, equals closing equity. It is where the income
  statement's bottom line is delivered to the balance sheet.
- **Temporary account** — a revenue or expense account closed to zero at period end; its
  contents live on in equity, but its identity does not survive the closing entries.
- **Permanent account** — an asset, liability, or equity account whose balance carries forward.
  The distinction from temporary accounts is what the closing entries operate on.
- **Period** — the reporting interval the cycle runs on. The accrual principle allocates
  economic events to periods; without periods there would be no adjusting entries and no cycle.
- **Equity** — the owners' residual claim: assets minus liabilities. Not cash, not value — the
  accounting equation's balancing claim, and the statements' way of keeping score.
- **Contra account** — an account that subtracts from its parent (accumulated depreciation
  against equipment). Contras are how the statements show gross amounts and their offsets
  without losing either.
