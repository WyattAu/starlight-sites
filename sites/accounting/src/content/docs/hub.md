Accounting is not a theorem-proof subject, and this site does not pretend otherwise. It is a
mechanics-first subject: a small set of structural facts; the accounting equation, the
double-entry rule, the accrual principle; generate everything else, and mastery means executing
the mechanics without error and knowing *why* each step is forced.

## The tracks

- **[Foundations](/1-foundations/)**; the accounting equation stated as an invariant,
  double-entry as the rule that preserves it, debits and credits defined by their effect on the
  equation's sides, and the trial balance as the invariant's check.
- **[The Accounting Cycle](/2-the-accounting-cycle/)**; journalising, posting, adjusting
  entries (accruals and deferrals justified by the accrual principle), the adjusted trial
  balance, and the construction of the four statements; every step worked, every number
  traceable.

## The worked-example standard

Every page here carries a fully worked example in which every number is traceable: transaction
to journal entry to ledger to trial balance to statement. The examples are small enough to check
by hand and complete enough to interlock; the income statement feeds retained earnings, which
feeds the balance sheet, which must still balance, and the pages show it balancing.

## The through-line

The single structural fact of the subject is that the accounting equation is an invariant, and
double-entry is the operation that preserves it. Everything else; adjusting entries, closing
entries, statement construction; is bookkeeping around that core, and pages here keep the
invariant visible at each step rather than burying it under procedure.

## Prerequisites

Arithmetic and the patience to check that entries balance. The subject's difficulty is
discipline, not mathematics; and the discipline is easier to keep when the structure explains
the rules, which is the ordering these pages take.

## Corrections and error reporting

A worked example that does not balance is a defect of the worst kind here; it teaches the
mechanic broken. If you find one, it is fixed and the correction recorded. The same goes for
entries that balance but post to the wrong side of the equation: balancing is necessary, not
sufficient, and the corrections hold both standards.

## The exam-board connection

GCSE, A-Level, and IB business syllabi touch the accounting equation and simple statements; the
exam-board sites carry those treatments with their assessment framing. This site is the layer
underneath, where the invariants live, and the cross-references are maintained by tooling.

## The self-test convention

Each page closes with a self-test keyed to its sections. The items are execution-first; predict
the entry, produce the balance, trace the number; because the subject is learned by doing, and
the self-tests are where the doing is checked before it counts.

## Where the site is going

Foundations and the cycle open the site; management accounting (cost behaviour, budgeting,

## The reading order

[Foundations](/1-foundations/) first, [The Accounting Cycle](/2-the-accounting-cycle/) second -
and the cycle read with a pencil, producing each entry before reading it. The pages are written
so that every produced entry can be checked against the shown one, and the check is where the
learning happens.

## What the site is not

This is not a standards reference: it teaches the mechanics and names the frameworks, but the
authoritative texts are the standards themselves. Where a page simplifies a rule for teaching,
the simplification is flagged; the flag is part of the standard, not an apology for it.

## The one-paragraph version

Assets = Liabilities + Equity always. Every transaction posts equal debits and credits, which is
why the equation holds after every transaction, not just at the end. The trial balance checks
it, adjusting entries keep accrual honesty inside it, and the four statements are reports of it.
That is the subject; the tracks execute it.

## The cycle in one sentence

Journal, post, adjust, check, report, close; and the equation holds at every comma. The tracks
teach each comma, because students who can say that sentence and mean every part of it can run
the subject.

## The self-test order

Predict before you read the shown entry; balance before you read the shown total; trace before
you read the shown statement line. The self-tests are sequenced in exactly that order, and
working them in that order is the difference between reading accounting and doing it.
