## Admissions Systems

**Holistic Admissions**: Evaluating the whole applicant — academics, essays, extracurriculars, recommendations — not just grades.

**Common Application**: A platform used by over 900 universities for US undergraduate admissions.

**Coalition Application**: An alternative US admissions platform with different essay prompt structures.

**UCAS (Universities and Colleges Admissions Service)**: The UK-wide system for applying to university.

**JUPAS (Joint University Programmes Admissions System)**: The main admissions system for Hong Kong universities.

**OUAC (Ontario Universities Application Centre)**: The application centre for Ontario, Canada universities.

**Direct Application**: Applying directly to a university through its own portal rather than a shared platform.

**Early Decision (ED)**: A binding early application — if accepted, you must attend. Typically due November 1.

**Early Action (EA)**: A non-binding early application — you receive a decision early without commitment.

**Regular Decision (RD)**: The standard application deadline, typically January 1–15.

**Rolling Admissions**: Universities review applications as they arrive until all places are filled.

## Application Components

**Personal Essay**: A written personal statement revealing who you are beyond grades and test scores.

**Supplemental Essay**: Additional essays required by specific universities ("Why Us?", "Why Major?", etc.).

**Activities List**: A summary of extracurricular activities, leadership roles, and achievements.

**Resume/CV**: A summary of academic and extracurricular achievements (some universities require it).

**Application Fee**: A fee paid when submitting an application (fee waivers are available for financial need).

**Application Portal**: The online system where you submit and track your application.

**Decision Release**: The date when universities announce their admissions decisions.

**Waitlist**: A list of qualified applicants who may be offered admission if places become available.

**Deferral**: Postponing a decision on your application to a later round.

**Rejection**: A university's decision not to offer admission.

## Personal Statements and Essays

**Hook**: A compelling opening that draws the reader in — a specific moment, image, or question.

**Show, Don't Tell**: Using concrete examples and experiences rather than abstract claims.

**Voice**: The authentic tone and personality that comes through in your writing.

**Vulnerability**: Showing genuine reflection and honesty about experiences and challenges.

**Specificity**: Using particular details rather than general statements.

**Cliché**: Overused phrases or topics that admissions officers see repeatedly.

**Resume Repetition**: Describing activities already listed in the activities section.

**Thesis**: The central idea or argument of your essay.

**Revision**: Multiple rounds of editing to improve content, structure, and voice.

**UCAS Personal Statement**: A 4,000-character statement for UK applications focusing on academic motivation.

## Standardised Testing

**SAT**: A standardised test for US university admissions with Math and Evidence-Based Reading and Writing sections.

**ACT**: An alternative to the SAT, including English, Math, Reading, Science, and an optional Writing section.

**AP (Advanced Placement)**: College-level courses and exams taken in high school.

**Test-Optional**: A policy where submitting standardised test scores is optional.

**Test-Blind**: A policy where standardised test scores are not considered even if submitted.

**Score Choice**: The ability to choose which SAT scores to send to universities.

**Superscoring**: Combining the highest section scores from multiple test sittings.

**Percentile**: The percentage of test-takers who scored below you.

**SAT Subject Tests**: Discontinued exams in specific subjects (some universities still consider AP scores).

**MAT (Mathematics Admissions Test)**: Oxford's mathematics admissions test.

**TMUA (Test of Mathematics for University Admission)**: A mathematics admissions test used by several UK universities.

**STEP (Sixth Term Examination Paper)**: Cambridge and Warwick's mathematics admissions test.

**UCAT (University Clinical Aptitude Test)**: A medical admissions test used by UK universities.

**LNAT (National Admissions Test for Law)**: A law admissions test used by UK universities.

## Extracurricular Activities

**Depth Over Breadth**: Deep involvement in fewer activities is more impressive than superficial involvement in many.

**Leadership**: Taking on roles that shape the direction of an organisation or project.

**Initiative**: Starting something new rather than only joining existing activities.

**Impact**: Measurable outcomes — funds raised, people served, projects completed.

**Sustained Commitment**: Long-term involvement over years, not a flurry of senior-year activity.

**Awards and Honours**: Recognition for achievement in competitions, academics, or activities.

**Research**: Independent or university-affiliated research projects.

**Community Service**: Volunteering and service work, especially with ongoing commitment.

**Work Experience**: Employment or internships, especially demonstrating responsibility.

**Competitions**: Academic contests (Math Olympiad, Science Olympiad, Debate, etc.).

## Recommendation Letters

**Academic Reference**: A letter from a teacher or professor evaluating your academic abilities.

**Counsellor Letter**: A report from your school counsellor providing context about your character and circumstances.

**Supplemental Recommender**: Additional letters from coaches, employers, or mentors.

**Brag Sheet**: A summary of your achievements and qualities provided to recommenders.

**Waiver**: Giving up your right to read the recommendation letter, making it more credible.

**Confidentiality**: The expectation that recommendation letters remain private.

**Relationship Over Rank**: Choosing teachers who know you well rather than those who gave you the highest grade.

## Financial Aid

**FAFSA (Free Application for Federal Student Aid)**: The form required for US federal financial aid.

**CSS Profile**: A form required by many private US universities for institutional financial aid.

**Need-Based Aid**: Financial aid based on your family's financial situation.

**Merit Scholarship**: Financial aid awarded based on academic or extracurricular achievement.

**Full-Ride Scholarship**: A scholarship covering tuition, room, board, and fees.

**Student Loan**: Borrowed money that must be repaid with interest.

**Grant**: Financial aid that does not need to be repaid.

**Work-Study**: A programme providing part-time employment to help pay for education.

**International Financial Aid**: Financial aid available to international students (varies significantly by university).

**Net Price**: The actual cost after all financial aid and scholarships are applied.

**EFC (Expected Family Contribution)**: An estimate of what your family can afford to pay (replaced by SAI in recent FAFSA changes).

## Decision-Making

**Fit**: How well a university matches your academic, social, and personal needs.

**Reach School**: A university where your credentials are below the average admitted student.

**Match School**: A university where your credentials match the average admitted student.

**Safety School**: A university where your credentials exceed the average admitted student.

**Yield**: The percentage of admitted students who actually enroll.

**National Decision Day**: May 1 — the deadline to confirm your enrolment at a US university.

**Deferral from Waitlist**: Being offered admission after the initial decision round.

**Gap Year**: Taking a year between high school and university.

**Transfer**: Applying to a different university after completing some college coursework.

**Visiting Campus**: Attending an open day or tour to experience university life firsthand.
