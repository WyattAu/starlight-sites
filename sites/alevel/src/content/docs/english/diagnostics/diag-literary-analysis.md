## Intuition

**Literary analysis is like being a detective — looking for clues in the text to uncover deeper meanings.**

# Diagnostic Test: Literary Analysis

Answer each question. Check your answers against the key at the end.

## Questions

**1.** In literary analysis, "focalisation" refers to:
(A) The physical location of the author at the time of writing
(B) The perspective through which events and information are filtered
(C) The use of formal language in dialogue
(D) The chronological arrangement of events in a narrative

**2.** Which technique involves giving human characteristics to abstract ideas or inanimate objects?
(A) Synecdoche
(B) Pathetic fallacy
(C) Allusion
(D) Anaphora

**3.** The term "diegesis" in narrative theory refers to:
(A) The emotional tone of a passage
(B) The world of the story and its narrative content
(C) The use of repetition for emphasis
(D) The physical setting of a literary work

**4.** What is the primary purpose of contextual analysis in A-Level English?
(A) To provide a biographical summary of the author's life
(B) To situate the text within its historical, social, and literary context
(C) To determine the author's personal political views
(D) To replace close reading of the text itself

**5.** Which of the following best describes "unreliable narration"?
(A) A narrator who is physically absent from the story
(B) A narrator whose account is contradicted or undermined by the text
(C) A narrator who speaks in the third person
(D) A narrator who is identified by name

**6.** When analysing symbolism in a novel, the most effective approach involves:
(A) Identifying every instance of a symbol without interpreting its significance
(B) Exploring how symbols develop and interact with the text's broader themes
(C) Assuming that symbols always have a single, fixed meaning
(D) Focusing exclusively on symbols mentioned in the title

**7.** The concept of "intertextuality" suggests that:
(A) A text can only be understood in isolation
(B) Texts are influenced by and reference other texts, creating layers of meaning
(C) The author's biography is the sole key to interpretation
(D) Literary analysis should avoid comparisons between texts

**8.** In comparative literary analysis, a "convergence" approach:
(A) Examines texts in complete isolation from one another
(B) Identifies areas where texts share common concerns despite differing contexts
(C) Argues that all texts convey identical messages
(D) Rejects the possibility of meaningful literary comparison

**9.** Which of the following is an example of "free indirect discourse"?
(A) Direct quotation of a character's speech
(B) A third-person narrative that incorporates a character's thoughts and language without direct attribution
(C) A first-person account by the author
(D) A chorus commenting on the action in a play

**10.** The effectiveness of a literary analysis essay primarily depends on:
(A) The quantity of quotations included
(B) The ability to construct a sustained, evidence-based argument
(C) The use of complex vocabulary unrelated to the text
(D) The personal opinions of the examiner

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing denotation with connotation:** Denotation is the literal meaning of a word; connotation is the associated or implied meaning. When analysing language, the power usually lies in connotation. Saying "the word 'dark' means it is nighttime" is denotation; saying "dark connotes danger, ignorance, and moral corruption" is analysis. Always explore the associative meanings.

**Using literary terminology without understanding it:** Terms like "unreliable narrator," "pathetic fallacy," and "free indirect discourse" have specific meanings. Using them incorrectly (e.g., calling any first-person narrator "unreliable" without evidence) weakens your analysis. Only use terminology you can define and apply precisely.

**Accumulating quotations instead of building an argument:** A string of quotations with brief comments is not analysis. Each quotation should serve a specific point in a sustained argument. Plan your essay as a series of claims, each supported by close reading of specific evidence. Quality of engagement matters more than quantity of quotations.
