## Probability Generating Functions

> **Info:** Board Coverage Edexcel FS1 (Section 5) | AQA Optional Topic | OCR (A) FS1
>
A **probability generating function (PGF)** packs the whole probability distribution of a discrete
random variable taking non-negative integer values into one expression:

$$
G_X(t) = \mathrm{E}(t^X) = \sum_{x=0}^{\infty} \mathrm{P}(X = x)\, t^x
$$

The coefficient of $t^x$ in the expansion of $G_X(t)$ is exactly $\mathrm{P}(X = x)$. PGFs are
examinable for three reasons: they give expectation and variance quickly, they classify standard
distributions, and they handle **sums of independent random variables** with almost no work.

## 1. Basic Properties

- $G_X(1) = \sum \mathrm{P}(X = x) = 1$: every PGF equals 1 at $t = 1$. This is the standard check
  for unknown constants.
- $G_X(0) = \mathrm{P}(X = 0)$.
- The PGF fully determines the distribution of $X$.

## 2. Expectation and Variance from $G(t)$

Differentiate and evaluate at $t = 1$:

$$
\mathrm{E}(X) = G_X'(1), \qquad
\mathrm{E}(X(X-1)) = G_X''(1)
$$

so

$$
\mathrm{Var}(X) = G_X''(1) + G_X'(1) - \bigl(G_X'(1)\bigr)^2
$$

**Worked example.** $G_X(t) = \frac{1}{2}(1 + t)^2$. Then

$$
G'(t) = (1+t), \quad G'(1) = 2; \qquad G''(t) = 1, \quad G''(1) = 1
$$

$$
\mathrm{E}(X) = 2, \qquad \mathrm{Var}(X) = 1 + 2 - 2^2 = 1
$$

(Expand to check: this is $X \sim \mathrm{B}(2, \tfrac12)$ -- see the standard forms below.)

## 3. Standard PGFs to Memorise

| Distribution | $G_X(t)$ | $\mathrm{E}(X)$ | $\mathrm{Var}(X)$ |
| ------------ | -------- | --------------- | ----------------- |
| $\mathrm{B}(n, p)$ | $(q + pt)^n$, $q = 1-p$ | $np$ | $npq$ |
| $\mathrm{Po}(\lambda)$ | $e^{\lambda(t-1)}$ | $\lambda$ | $\lambda$ |
| Geometric$(p)$ on $\{1, 2, \ldots\}$ | $\dfrac{pt}{1 - q t}$ | $\dfrac{1}{p}$ | $\dfrac{q}{p^2}$ |

Learn the binomial and Poisson forms derivationally: expand $(q+pt)^n$ by the binomial theorem and
read off coefficients; expand $e^{\lambda(t-1)}$ as a Maclaurin series and compare with the Poisson
pmf.

## 4. Sums of Independent Random Variables

For independent $X$ and $Y$:

$$
G_{X+Y}(t) = G_X(t)\, G_Y(t)
$$

This is the result the exam is built around.

**Worked example.** $X \sim \mathrm{B}(4, 0.3)$, $Y \sim \mathrm{B}(6, 0.3)$, independent. Then

$$
G_{X+Y}(t) = (0.7 + 0.3t)^4 (0.7+0.3t)^6 = (0.7 + 0.3t)^{10}
$$

which is the PGF of $\mathrm{B}(10, 0.3)$: the sum of independent binomials with the same $p$ is
binomial. The same argument for independent Poissons gives
$\mathrm{Po}(\lambda_1) + \mathrm{Po}(\lambda_2) = \mathrm{Po}(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)$.

A related form: if $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ are i.i.d. then
$G_{S_n}(t) = \bigl(G_X(t)\bigr)^n$. For example, the sum of 12 independent geometric variables has
PGF $\left(\dfrac{pt}{1-qt}\right)^{12}$ -- a negative binomial distribution.

## 5. Finding the Distribution from a PGF

Given an unfamiliar PGF, either match it to a standard form or expand enough coefficients to
identify the probabilities, then name the distribution and state its parameters.

**Worked example.** $G(t) = e^{2(t - 1)} \cdot (0.6 + 0.4t)^5$ for independent
$X \sim \mathrm{Po}(2)$ and $Y \sim \mathrm{B}(5, 0.4)$. Then $X + Y$ has PGF equal to the product,
which has no simple closed form -- but you can still compute $\mathrm{E}(X+Y) = 2 + 2 = 3$ and
$\mathrm{Var}(X+Y) = 2 + 5(0.4)(0.6) = 3.2$ from the product rule applied to derivatives at $t=1$.

## 6. Common Pitfalls

- **Forgetting $G''(1)$ is $\mathrm{E}(X(X-1))$, not $\mathrm{Var}(X)$**: the variance formula has
  three terms.
- **Valid PGFs satisfy $G(1) = 1$**: if an unknown constant $k$ appears, set $G(1) = 1$ *first*,
  then read off probabilities.
- **Sums of independent variables only.** If $X$ and $Y$ are dependent, the PGF product is wrong;
  state independence in your working.
- **Geometric support**: some specifications define the geometric distribution on
  $\{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$ (number of failures); its PGF is then $\dfrac{p}{1-qt}$. Check which
  convention your board uses -- the exam paper will match the formula booklet.
- Mixed distributions (constant + random parts) do not have PGFs on their own: write
  $X = c + Y$ and use $G_Y$.

## 7. Summary

| Task | Method |
| ---- | ------ |
| $\mathrm{P}(X = x)$ | Coefficient of $t^x$ |
| $\mathrm{E}(X)$ | $G'(1)$ |
| $\mathrm{Var}(X)$ | $G''(1) + G'(1) - (G'(1))^2$ |
| Sum of independent RVs | Multiply the PGFs |
| Unknown constant | Use $G(1) = 1$ |
