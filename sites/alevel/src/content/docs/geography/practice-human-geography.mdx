## A-Level Geography -- Human Geography Practice

18 MCQ practice problems on human geography. Select an answer, submit, and review the explanation.
Questions follow A-Level examination style and cover urbanisation, globalisation, migration,
development, population dynamics, cultural diversity, and inequality.

### What These Questions Test

These problems test your ability to explain spatial patterns, evaluate development strategies, and analyse the social, economic, and environmental dimensions of human geographical issues.

**Typical question types:**

- **Globalisation:** Explain the drivers and consequences of globalisation. Analyse the role of TNCs, trade agreements, and technology. Evaluate whether globalisation reduces or increases inequality.
- **Urbanisation:** Describe and explain urban models. Analyse the challenges of rapid urbanisation in the Global South. Evaluate urban planning strategies (e.g. master planning, slum upgrading, new towns).
- **Migration:** Explain the push and pull factors for different types of migration. Analyse the impacts of migration on source and destination countries. Evaluate migration policies (e.g. points-based systems, quotas, amnesty).
- **Development:** Compare different measures of development (GDP, HDI, PPP). Explain why some countries remain poor. Evaluate development strategies (aid, trade, debt relief, microfinance).
- **Population dynamics:** Explain the demographic transition model. Analyse ageing populations and dependency ratios. Evaluate population policies (pro-natalist, anti-natalist, immigration).

### Approach Strategy

1. **Use the PEEL structure.** Point (state your argument), Evidence (give a specific example), Explain (link evidence to the argument), Link (connect to the question). This ensures each paragraph is focused and supported.
2. **Be specific.** Instead of "many people migrate," say "in 2020, there were approximately 281 million international migrants, representing 3.6% of the global population."
3. **Consider multiple perspectives.** For any issue, consider the economic, social, environmental, and political dimensions. A complete answer addresses all four.
4. **Evaluate, don't just describe.** For any strategy or policy, discuss its effectiveness, costs, benefits, and limitations.

### Intuition

Human geography is about understanding why people live where they do, how they organise their societies, and what happens when populations and economies change. It is fundamentally about the relationship between people and place.

Think of the world as a network of flows. People, goods, money, information, and ideas all move across the surface of the Earth. Human geography studies where these flows go, why they go there, and what happens when they arrive.

---

### Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing correlation with causation.** "Countries with high literacy rates have high GDP" does not mean literacy causes wealth. Both may be caused by a third factor (e.g. good governance). Always look for the mechanism.
2. **Using outdated data.** Development statistics change over time. Ensure you are using recent data and cite your source. Using 20-year-old statistics weakens your argument.
3. **Assuming all urbanisation is the same.** Urbanisation in the Global North (e.g. UK, USA) is mature and often involves suburbanisation or counter-urbanisation. Urbanisation in the Global South (e.g. Lagos, Dhaka) is rapid and often unplanned, leading to informal settlements.
4. **Forgetting the environmental dimension.** Human geography is not just about people and economies. Environmental factors (climate, natural resources, natural hazards) shape human activity and are affected by it.
