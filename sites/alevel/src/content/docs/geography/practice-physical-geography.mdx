## A-Level Geography -- Physical Geography Practice

18 MCQ practice problems. Select answer, submit, review explanation.

### What These Questions Test

These problems test your understanding of physical processes, your ability to apply geographical models, and your skill in analysing data and case studies.

**Typical question types:**

- **Water and carbon cycles:** Explain the stores and flows in the water cycle and carbon cycle. Analyse how human activities disrupt these cycles. Interpret data on river discharge, CO$_{2}$ levels, or carbon fluxes.
- **Coastal processes:** Identify and explain the formation of coastal landforms. Evaluate coastal management strategies. Interpret maps, photographs, or fieldwork data showing coastal change.
- **Glacial processes:** Explain the formation of glacial landforms (corries, arêtes, U-shaped valleys). Distinguish between glacial erosion and deposition features. Apply knowledge to unfamiliar glacial landscapes.
- **Tectonic hazards:** Explain the causes of earthquakes and volcanic eruptions. Compare the impacts of tectonic hazards on different areas. Evaluate strategies for hazard management (prediction, protection, preparation).
- **Atmospheric hazards:** Explain the formation and structure of tropical storms. Analyse the impacts of storms on different areas. Evaluate management strategies.

### Approach Strategy

1. **Use the CEDAR framework.** For physical geography essays: Causes (why does it happen?), Effects (what are the consequences?), Distribution (where does it happen?), Responses (how do humans respond?). This ensures a complete answer.
2. **Refer to specific case studies.** Mentioning real examples (e.g. the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake, Hurricane Katrina, the retreat of the Mer de Glace) strengthens your answer.
3. **Link processes to outcomes.** Don't just say "hydraulic action erodes the coast." Explain how: "Hydraulic action forces water into cracks, compresses the air, and the pressure shatters the rock, forming a wave-cut notch."
4. **Evaluate, don't just describe.** For management strategies, discuss advantages, disadvantages, costs, and environmental impacts.

### Intuition

Physical geography is about understanding the forces that shape the Earth's surface. Water, ice, wind, and tectonic plates are all agents of change. The key is understanding the scale: some processes (like erosion) are slow and gradual, while others (like earthquakes) are sudden and catastrophic.

Think of the Earth's surface as a balance sheet. Erosion and deposition are debits and credits. The water cycle and carbon cycle are transfer accounts moving resources between different stores. Human activities are like a new account that has been opened, disrupting the balance.

### Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing weathering and erosion.** Weathering is the breakdown of rock in situ (in place). Erosion is the removal and transportation of weathered material. Weathering happens without movement; erosion requires transport.
2. **Misidentifying landforms from descriptions.** A corrie is a armchair-shaped hollow (erosion). A drumlin is an elongated hill (deposition). A spit is a narrow projection of land (deposition). Mixing these up loses marks.
3. **Assuming all volcanoes are the same.** Shield volcanoes (e.g. Mauna Loa) have gentle slopes and effusive eruptions. Composite volcanoes (e.g. Mount Fuji) have steep slopes and explosive eruptions. The type depends on the magma composition.
4. **Forgetting that the Carbon Cycle involves the oceans.** The ocean is the largest carbon sink. Ocean absorption of CO$_{2}$ causes ocean acidification, which threatens marine ecosystems. This is often overlooked.
