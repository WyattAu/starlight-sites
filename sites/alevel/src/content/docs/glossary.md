## General Examination Terms

**Assessment Objective (AO)**: The skills and knowledge being assessed. A-Level subjects typically have three AOs: AO1 (demonstrate knowledge and understanding), AO2 (apply knowledge and understanding), AO3 (analyse and evaluate).

**UCAS Tariff**: The points system used for university admissions in the UK. An A* is worth 56 points, A is 48, B is 40, and so on.

**Linear Qualification**: A qualification where all exams are taken at the end of the two-year course, rather than in modular units throughout.

**Synoptic Assessment**: Questions that require drawing together knowledge from multiple areas of the specification, typically appearing at the end of the A2 papers.

**Grade Boundary**: The minimum raw marks required to achieve each grade (A*-E) for a given paper or overall qualification.

**Cash-In**: The process of claiming the full A-Level qualification once all units have been completed.

## Mathematics

**Discriminant**: The expression $b^2 - 4ac$ in a quadratic equation, indicating the nature of the roots. Positive means two real roots, zero means one repeated root, negative means no real roots.

**Integration by Parts**: A technique for integrating products of functions, based on the rule $\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du$.

**Parametric Equations**: Equations that express coordinates as separate functions of a parameter, typically time or angle.

## Physics

**Electromotive Force (EMF)**: The energy supplied per unit charge by a cell or power supply, measured in volts.

**Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)**: Oscillatory motion where acceleration is directly proportional to displacement and directed towards a fixed equilibrium point.

**Half-Life**: The time taken for half the unstable nuclei in a sample to decay, or for half a quantity of a substance to be eliminated.

## Chemistry

**Rate-Determining Step**: The slowest step in a reaction mechanism, which controls the overall rate of reaction.

**Standard Conditions**: 298 K temperature, 100 kPa pressure, and 1 mol dm⁻³ concentration for all aqueous solutions.

**Enthalpy Change of Formation**: The enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed from its elements under standard conditions.

## Biology

**Homeostasis**: The maintenance of a stable internal environment despite changes in external conditions.

**Semi-Conservative Replication**: The mechanism of DNA replication where each new DNA molecule contains one original strand and one newly synthesised strand.

## Economics

**Elasticity**: A measure of how responsive quantity demanded or supplied is to a change in price or income.

**Market Failure**: A situation where the free market fails to allocate resources efficiently, justifying government intervention.
