## Intuition

**History is like a vast library of human experience — learning from the past to understand the present.**

# Diagnostic Test: Source Analysis

Answer each question. Check your answers against the key at the end.

## Questions

**1.** In the OPVL framework, what does the letter "P" stand for?
(A) Perspective
(B) Purpose
(C) Period
(D) Provenance

**2.** When evaluating the provenance of a historical source, which factor is most relevant?
(A) The physical appearance of the document
(B) The author's identity, position, and potential biases
(C) The font used in the printed text
(D) The colour of the paper or ink

**3.** A primary source is best defined as:
(A) Any source published during the period under study
(B) A source created at the time of the events or by a direct participant
(C) A source written by a professional historian
(D) A source that has been verified by academic institutions

**4.** What is the key difference between "interpretation" and "original source material"?
(A) Interpretations are always more reliable than original sources
(B) Interpretations are secondary analyses that select, organise, and explain evidence from primary sources
(C) Original sources are always more accurate than interpretations
(D) There is no meaningful difference between the two

**5.** When analysing a historian's argument, which question is most important?
(A) How many footnotes does the argument contain?
(B) What evidence supports the claim, and how convincingly does it address counter-arguments?
(C) Is the historian affiliated with a prestigious university?
(D) Was the argument published recently?

**6.** Historiography refers to:
(A) The study of historical events themselves
(B) The study of how history has been written and interpreted over time
(C) The collection of primary sources for a given period
(D) The process of dating archaeological artefacts

**7.** Which of the following is the strongest indication that a source may be biased?
(A) It was written in the language of the period
(B) The author had a direct personal or political interest in the events described
(C) It contains numerical data
(D) It was published in a peer-reviewed journal

**8.** When comparing two conflicting historical interpretations, the most effective approach is to:
(A) Choose the interpretation that aligns with your personal views
(B) Evaluate the evidence and reasoning each interpretation uses and assess their relative strengths
(C) Reject both interpretations as unreliable
(D) Summarise each interpretation without analysis

**9.** What does "corroboration" mean in source analysis?
(A) Confirming that a source agrees with the historian's thesis
(B) Cross-referencing information from multiple independent sources to assess reliability
(C) Translating a source from its original language
(D) Dating a source to determine its authenticity

**10.** Which statement about contested historical views is most accurate?
(A) Historians who disagree are necessarily motivated by personal prejudice
(B) Contestation reflects genuine differences in evidence, interpretation, and analytical framework
(C) All historical debates have been resolved by current scholarship
(D) Students should avoid engaging with contested interpretations

## Answer Key

| Q | Answer | Explanation |
|---|--------|-------------|
| 1 | (B) | OPVL stands for Origin, Purpose, Value, and Limitation; "P" is Purpose, asking why the source was created. |
| 2 | (B) | Provenance concerns the source's origins, including author identity, circumstances of creation, and potential biases. |
| 3 | (B) | A primary source is created contemporaneously with events or by someone with direct experience of them. |
| 4 | (B) | Interpretations are scholarly analyses that synthesise and explain primary evidence rather than being the evidence itself. |
| 5 | (B) | Evaluating the strength of evidence and the handling of counter-arguments is central to assessing historical argument. |
| 6 | (B) | Historiography is the study of historical writing itself, examining how interpretations have evolved over time. |
| 7 | (B) | Personal or political interest creates potential for bias, requiring careful evaluation of the source's claims. |
| 8 | (B) | Effective comparison evaluates the evidential basis and analytical reasoning of each interpretation. |
| 9 | (B) | Corroboration involves checking a source's claims against other independent sources to test reliability. |
| 10 | (B) | Historians contest interpretations due to differing evidence, methodology, and analytical frameworks, not merely bias. |
