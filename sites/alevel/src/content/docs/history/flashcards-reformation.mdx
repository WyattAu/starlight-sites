## A-Level History -- The Reformation Flashcards

### What These Flashcards Cover

These flashcards test your knowledge of the Reformation, both as a European movement and specifically as it unfolded in England. You should be able to explain the causes of the Reformation, describe the key events, and evaluate its long-term consequences.

**Key areas:**

- **Causes of the Reformation:** Corruption in the Catholic Church (indulgences, simony, clerical abuses). The printing press enabling the spread of Protestant ideas. Humanism and the return to original biblical texts. Political grievances: national monarchs resenting papal authority. Martin Luther's 95 Theses (1517) challenged indulgences and papal authority.
- **Luther and the Spread of Protestantism:** Luther's doctrine of sola scriptura (scripture alone) and sola fide (faith alone). The Diet of Worms (1521) and Luther's excommunication. The Peace of Augsburg (1555) established cuius regio, eius religio (the ruler determines the religion).
- **The English Reformation:** Henry VIII's break with Rome (1534) driven by the annulment crisis and the desire for a male heir. The Act of Supremacy and the dissolution of the monasteries. The theological pendulum under Edward VI (Protestant), Mary I (Catholic), and Elizabeth I (moderate Protestant settlement).
- **Key Theological Debates:** Transubstantiation vs consubstantiation vs memorial view of the Eucharist. The role of faith vs works in salvation. The authority of the Pope vs the authority of the monarch. Predestination vs free will.
- **Consequences of the Reformation:** The permanent establishment of Protestantism in England. The creation of the Church of England as a national church. The loss of monastic lands and the rise of a new landowning class. The lasting sectarian divisions that persisted for centuries.
- **The Counter-Reformation:** The Council of Trent (1545-1563) reformed Catholic practices. The Society of Jesus (Jesuits) promoted education and missionary work. The Inquisition enforced Catholic orthodoxy. The Catholic Church reformed itself in response to Protestant criticism.

---

### Intuition

The Reformation was a perfect storm. You had genuine religious grievances (corruption, doctrinal questions) combined with political ambitions (monarchs wanting independence from Rome) and technological change (the printing press spreading ideas). No single cause was sufficient on its own -- it was the combination that made the Reformation unstoppable.

In England specifically, the Reformation started as a political manoeuvre (Henry wanted a divorce) but took on a life of its own. Once the break with Rome happened, Protestant reformers seized the opportunity to reshape English religion. The result was a church that was Protestant in theology but retained many Catholic traditions in practice.

### Key Dates to Remember

- **1517:** Martin Luther publishes his 95 Theses.
- **1521:** Diet of Worms; Luther excommunicated.
- **1534:** Act of Supremacy establishes Henry VIII as Head of the Church of England.
- **1536-1541:** Dissolution of the Monasteries.
- **1547:** Edward VI ascends the throne; Protestant reforms accelerated.
- **1553:** Mary I becomes queen; Catholic restoration.
- **1558:** Elizabeth I ascends; religious settlement (1559).
- **1559:** Act of Uniformity and Act of Supremacy re-establish Protestantism.
- **1568-1603:** Ridolfi, Throckmorton, and Babington plots against Elizabeth.

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Reducing the Reformation to a single cause.** It was not just about indulgences, or just about politics, or just about technology. All of these factors interacted. An essay that focuses on only one cause will be too narrow.
2. **Assuming the Reformation was universally popular.** Many people in England (especially in the north and west) remained Catholic or were indifferent. The Pilgrimage of Grace (1536) showed significant opposition to Henry's religious changes.
3. **Confusing the Church of England with Puritanism.** The Church of England (Anglican Church) was a moderate Protestant church that retained some Catholic ceremonies. Puritans wanted to "purify" the church further by removing all traces of Catholicism. These were different movements within Protestantism.
4. **Forgetting the European context.** The English Reformation did not happen in isolation. It was part of a wider European movement, and international events (like the Peace of Augsburg) influenced English policy.
