## A-Level History -- Tudor England Practice

18 MCQ practice problems. Select answer, submit, review explanation.

### What These Questions Test

These problems test your knowledge of the political, religious, and social developments in Tudor England, as well as your ability to evaluate historical interpretations and construct arguments.

**Typical question types:**

- **Knowledge and understanding:** Identify key dates, events, and figures. Explain the causes and consequences of specific events (e.g. the dissolution of the monasteries, the Spanish Armada).
- **Analysis and interpretation:** Evaluate the significance of different factors (political, religious, economic, personal) in causing change. Assess the reliability of historical sources. Compare different historians' interpretations.
- **Historiography:** Know the key historians and their arguments. For example: G.R. Elton emphasised the role of Thomas Cromwell in the Reformation; Geoffrey Dickens stressed popular piety and anti-clericalism; Christopher Haigh argued the Reformation was imposed from above rather than demanded from below.
- **Essay technique:** Structure arguments with a clear thesis, supporting evidence, and counter-arguments. Use specific examples rather than vague generalisations.

---

### Approach Strategy

1. **Read the question carefully.** Identify the command word (explain, evaluate, compare, to what extent). This determines the structure of your answer.
2. **Use specific evidence.** Instead of saying "Henry VIII was desperate for a male heir," say "Henry's marriage to Catherine of Aragon produced only one surviving child, Mary, and six pregnancies ended in stillbirth or miscarriage."
3. **Consider multiple factors.** Avoid monocausal explanations. The break with Rome was driven by personal (dynastic), political (national sovereignty), religious (reformation ideas), and economic (monastic wealth) factors.
4. **Engage with historiography.** Reference at least two historians in an extended response. Show that you understand the debate, not just the facts.

---

### Intuition

Tudor history is about the interplay between personality and structure. The Tudors were charismatic rulers who understood power, but they also operated within constraints (Parliament, the Church, the nobility, public opinion). The most successful Tudor policies were those that aligned the monarch's personal goals with broader structural trends.

The Reformation in England was not a sudden rupture but a gradual process. Each monarch shifted the religious settlement, and ordinary people had to adapt. Some embraced Protestantism enthusiastically; others clung to Catholicism; many were indifferent and just wanted to be left alone.

---

### Common Mistakes

1. **Anachronism.** Judging Tudor actions by modern standards. Henry VIII's break with Rome made sense in the context of 16th-century politics and religion, even if it seems selfish by modern standards.
2. **Oversimplifying complex events.** The Spanish Armada was not just about religion. It involved English support for Dutch rebels, English privateering against Spanish ships, and Philip II's geopolitical ambitions. A full explanation requires multiple factors.
3. **Confusing correlation with causation.** Just because two events happened around the same time does not mean one caused the other. The dissolution of the monasteries and the Pilgrimage of Grace happened together, but the Grace was caused by broader grievances, not just the dissolution.
4. **Forgetting that "Tudor" covers a long period.** 1485 to 1603 is over a century. Conditions, policies, and attitudes changed significantly over this period. What was true under Henry VII was not necessarily true under Elizabeth I.
