### FAQ

**How long should I study for each A-Level?**
Most students spend 5-7 hours per week per subject outside of class time. This includes reading, practice questions, and revision.

**What's the best order to study topics?**
Follow the suggested learning path in each subject hub. Foundation topics build on each other, so don't skip ahead.

**How many practice questions should I do?**
Aim for at least 20-30 practice questions per topic, mixing worked examples with independent practice.

## How to Use This Hub

1. Pick your subject from the index above and skim the topic list to locate weak areas.
2. Read the notes for one topic, then immediately attempt its practice questions — testing effect beats re-reading.
3. Keep a mistake log: every wrong answer gets a one-line cause (knowledge gap, misread, arithmetic slip).
4. Two weeks before papers, switch to timed whole-paper practice under exam conditions.

## Revision Strategy

**Spaced repetition** beats cramming: revisit each topic at expanding intervals (1 day, 3 days, 1 week). **Interleaving** — mixing topics within a session — builds discrimination between problem types, which is exactly what synoptic papers demand. **Past papers are the curriculum**: examiner reports show the same command words and mark-scheme patterns recur every year; studying them is the highest-yield hour you can spend.


## Assessment Objectives Across Subjects

Every A-Level spec tests three objectives in different proportions: AO1 knowledge, AO2 application, AO3 analysis and evaluation. Sciences weight AO2 (applying knowledge to unfamiliar contexts) most heavily — which is why past-paper practice outperforms note re-reading. Essay subjects weight AO3: examiners reward argued judgement, not balanced summaries that refuse to conclude.

## Practical Endorsement (Sciences)

AQA, Edexcel, OCR, and Eduqas sciences include the CPAC practical endorsement: twelve required techniques and procedures assessed by teachers across the two years. It is reported separately (pass/fail) from the exam grade but is required by most universities for science courses. Our biology, chemistry, and physics sections include the required practicals with method, variables, error analysis, and typical exam questions drawn from them.

## Choosing Subjects

Keep doors open: two facilitating subjects (maths, sciences, history, geography, English literature, languages) preserve the widest degree choice. Check specific-entry requirements on university course pages early — medicine wants chemistry plus biology or maths; engineering wants maths plus physics; economics increasingly wants maths. Enjoyment matters: two years of a subject you dislike shows in attendance, retention, and grades alike.

## Study Timeline

**September-December (Year 1)**: build notes per topic as taught; end each topic with its exam-board past questions. **January-May (Year 1)**: first mock cycle; diagnose gaps from mock papers, not vibes. **June-September**: consolidate Year 1 content (Year 2 papers assume it); read ahead into Year 2 topics. **September-December (Year 2)**: NEA/coursework milestone for subjects that have it; begin weekly timed essays. **January-May (Year 2)**: two full mock cycles plus the final past-paper sprint — every recent paper, timed, marked against the scheme, mistakes logged.
