## Intuition

**A-Levels are deep dives into specific subjects:** Unlike GCSEs which cover breadth, A-Levels go deep. You'll develop expert-level understanding in your chosen subjects, learning not just facts but how to think like a scientist, mathematician, or humanist. This depth is what universities value — it shows you can handle rigorous, specialised study.

**Why it matters:** A-Level results determine university admissions, but more importantly, they develop the analytical and critical thinking skills that will serve you throughout your career. The ability to construct logical arguments, analyse evidence, and solve complex problems is valued in every profession.

**The key insight:** Success in A-Levels comes from understanding, not memorisation. You need to grasp the underlying principles so well that you can apply them to unfamiliar problems. Worked examples and practice questions are your best tools — they show you how experts think through problems.

## How to Use These Notes

1. **Start with the fundamentals** -- build a solid foundation before moving to advanced topics
2. **Work through examples** -- follow along with the worked examples to build intuition
3. **Test yourself** -- use the practice problems and flashcards to check your understanding
4. **Review regularly** -- spaced repetition helps retain what you have learned



## Common Mistakes

**Studying passively instead of actively:** Reading notes and highlighting text creates an illusion of understanding without building recall ability. Active recall — closing the book and trying to write or explain the topic from memory — is significantly more effective for exam preparation. Use flashcards and practice problems to test yourself regularly.

**Skipping worked examples:** A-Level exams reward method, not just answers. Students who skip worked examples often lose marks on method even when their final answer is correct. Follow through every worked example step-by-step, then attempt similar problems independently.

**Neglecting exam board specifications:** AQA, OCR, and Edexcel have different syllabuses for the same subject. Studying content that is not on your exam board wastes time, while missing board-specific topics loses marks. Always cross-reference your notes against the relevant specification.
