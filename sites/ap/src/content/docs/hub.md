## Complete AP Course Guide — All Subjects

The Advanced Placement program, administered by the College Board, offers college-level coursework across a wide range of disciplines. Earning a high AP exam score can earn you college credit, strengthen your university application, and give you a head start on university-level study. This hub brings together every AP study resource on Wyatt's Notes into a single page, covering Biology, Chemistry, Computer Science A, Computer Science Principles, English Language and Composition, English Literature, History (US, European, and World), Mathematics (Calculus AB, Calculus BC, and Statistics), and Physics (1, 2, and C).

Whether you are preparing for your first AP exam or refining your technique for a full slate of tests, the guides below give you the content mastery and strategic advantage you need.

## AP Biology

AP Biology explores the living world at every scale, from molecular processes to ecosystem dynamics. The course is organised around four Big Ideas: evolution, energetics, information transfer, and systems interactions. You will study biochemistry, cell structure and function, genetics and gene expression, evolution and phylogeny, ecology, and animal physiology.

Key topics include the chemistry of life, enzyme kinetics, cellular respiration and photosynthesis, Mendelian and molecular genetics, population genetics, and biological systems. The exam tests both conceptual understanding and data analysis, so practise interpreting graphs, designing experiments, and applying the scientific method.

Read the full guide: [AP Biology Study Guide](biology).

## AP Chemistry

AP Chemistry gives you a deep understanding of matter and its interactions. The course covers atomic structure and periodicity, chemical bonding and molecular geometry, intermolecular forces, chemical reactions, stoichiometry, thermodynamics, kinetics, equilibrium, acids and bases, electrochemistry, and nuclear chemistry.

You will work with Lewis structures, VSEPR theory, ideal gas laws, and equilibrium calculations. The exam rewards both conceptual clarity and quantitative problem-solving. Practise balancing half-reactions, constructing Born-Haber cycles, and interpreting titration curves.

Read the full guide: [AP Chemistry Study Guide](chemistry).

## AP Computer Science A

AP Computer Science A teaches object-oriented programming in Java. Core topics include classes and objects, inheritance and polymorphism, arrays and ArrayLists, sorting and searching algorithms, recursion, and basic data structures. The course emphasises algorithmic thinking and the ability to read, write, and analyse code.

You will practise designing classes, implementing methods, tracing code execution, and analysing time complexity. The exam tests your ability to write correct Java code and reason about program behaviour.

Read the full guide: [AP Computer Science A Study Guide](ap-computer-science-a).

## AP Computer Science Principles

AP Computer Science Principles introduces the foundational ideas of computing without requiring a specific programming language. Topics include the internet, data, cybersecurity, algorithms, programming, abstraction, and the social impact of computing.

The course builds computational thinking through creative problem-solving and the exploration of real-world computing applications. You will complete a Create Performance Task as part of the course.

Read the full guide: [AP Computer Science Principles Study Guide](ap-computer-science-principles).

## AP English Language and Composition

AP English Language and Composition focuses on rhetorical analysis, argumentation, and synthesis. You will analyse non-fiction texts, construct evidence-based arguments, and write persuasive essays. The course develops your ability to evaluate how authors use language to persuade and inform.

The exam includes multiple-choice questions on prose passages and three free-response essays: synthesis, rhetorical analysis, and argumentation.

## AP English Literature and Composition

AP English Literature and Composition centres on close reading and literary analysis. You will study fiction, poetry, and drama, analysing how authors use literary elements such as imagery, symbolism, characterisation, and narrative structure. The course trains you to construct interpretive arguments grounded in textual evidence.

The exam includes multiple-choice questions on prose and poetry passages and three free-response essays: literary analysis, prose analysis, and poetry analysis.

## AP History

The AP History programme includes three distinct courses, each with its own thematic focus:

### AP United States History

AP US History covers American history from 1491 to the present. Topics include colonial societies, the American Revolution, the Constitution, westward expansion, the Civil War and Reconstruction, industrialisation, the Progressive Era, the World Wars, the Cold War, and contemporary America. You will practise document-based questions (DBQs), long essay questions, and short answer questions.

Read the full guide: [AP History Study Guide](history).

### AP European History

AP European History traces European civilisation from 1450 to the present. Key themes include the Renaissance, the Reformation, absolutism, the Enlightenment, the French Revolution, industrialisation, nationalism, the World Wars, the Cold War, and contemporary Europe. The exam tests your ability to analyse primary sources and construct historical arguments.

### AP World History: Modern

AP World History covers global history from 1200 CE to the present. The course emphasises comparison, causation, and continuity and change over time across Africa, the Americas, Asia, Europe, and Oceania. You will analyse trade networks, empires, revolutions, and globalisation.

## AP Mathematics

### AP Calculus AB

AP Calculus AB covers differential and integral calculus. Topics include limits, continuity, derivatives, applications of derivatives, integrals, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, and applications of integration. The course emphasises conceptual understanding and the ability to solve problems analytically, graphically, and numerically.

Read the full guide: [AP Calculus AB Study Guide](calculus-ab).

Practice materials: [AP Calculus AB Practice](practice-calculus-ab).

### AP Calculus BC

AP Calculus BC includes all of the Calculus AB content plus additional topics: sequences, series, parametric equations, polar coordinates, and the Taylor and Maclaurin series. The course provides a more rigorous treatment of integration techniques and infinite processes.

Practice materials: [AP Calculus BC Practice](practice-calculus).

### AP Statistics

AP Statistics introduces statistical reasoning and data analysis. Topics include exploratory data analysis, sampling and experimental design, probability distributions, sampling distributions, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, and regression. You will practise interpreting real data sets, constructing statistical arguments, and using technology for calculations.

Read the full guide: [AP Statistics Study Guide](statistics).

## AP Physics

AP Physics offers several levels of study:

### AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based

AP Physics 1 covers Newtonian mechanics, work, energy, power, mechanical waves, sound, and introductory circuits. The course uses algebra and trigonometry rather than calculus. You will analyse physical systems, design experiments, and make predictions based on physical laws.

Read the full guide: [AP Physics 1 Study Guide](physics-1).

Practice materials: [AP Physics Mechanics Practice](practice-physics-mechanics).

### AP Physics 2: Algebra-Based

AP Physics 2 extends into fluid mechanics, thermodynamics, electricity and magnetism, optics, and modern physics including atomic and nuclear physics. The course builds on the conceptual framework of Physics 1 with more advanced topics.

### AP Physics C: Mechanics

AP Physics C: Mechanics is a calculus-based course covering kinematics, Newton's laws, work and energy, systems of particles and linear momentum, rotation, oscillations, and gravitation. The course provides a rigorous treatment of classical mechanics.

### AP Physics C: Electricity and Magnetism

AP Physics C: E&M covers electrostatics, conductors and capacitors, electric circuits, magnetic fields, and electromagnetism. The course uses calculus to derive and apply physical laws.

Read the full guide: [AP Physics Study Guide](physics).

## AP Exam Strategy Tips

Success on AP exams requires more than content knowledge. Here are proven strategies:

### Before the Exam

1. **Start early.** Begin reviewing at least six to eight weeks before the exam. Spaced repetition and consistent practice outperform last-minute cramming.
2. **Use the College Board's course description.** The Course and Exam Description (CED) is the definitive source for what content appears on the exam. Study it carefully and identify any gaps.
3. **Practise with released exams.** The College Board publishes past exams with scoring guidelines. These are the closest thing to the real test and help you calibrate your timing and depth of response.
4. **Make a formula and equation sheet.** Even if the exam provides a formula sheet, writing your own helps you memorise and understand the relationships between quantities.

### During the Exam

1. **Read every question carefully.** Many mistakes come from misreading what the question is asking. Underline key terms and identify the specific skill being tested.
2. **Manage your time.** Allocate time proportionally to the number of points each question is worth. If you get stuck, move on and return later.
3. **Show your work.** On free-response questions, partial credit is awarded for correct reasoning even if the final answer is wrong. Write down every step.
4. **Use process of elimination.** On multiple-choice questions, eliminate obviously wrong answers before guessing. This increases your probability of selecting the correct option.
5. **Answer every question.** There is no penalty for wrong answers on AP exams. Never leave a question blank.

### After the Exam

1. **Review your performance.** If you have access to your score report, identify weak areas and use them to guide future study.
2. **Consider score reports for universities.** Send your AP scores to universities that grant credit. A score of 4 or 5 typically earns credit, though policies vary.
3. **Build on your AP foundation.** AP courses prepare you for university-level work. Continue building on the skills and knowledge you have developed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many AP exams should I take?

There is no single right number. Focus on quality over quantity. Take AP courses in subjects you are genuinely interested in and where you can perform well. One or two well-prepared AP exams are more valuable than five rushed ones. Consider your overall workload, extracurricular commitments, and university goals.

### What score do I need to earn college credit?

Most universities award credit for scores of 4 or 5, though some accept scores of 3. Policies vary widely by institution and department. Check the specific university's AP credit policy before registering for exams. Elite universities may grant credit only for scores of 5 or may place you into advanced courses rather than awarding credit.

### When should I start preparing for AP exams?

Ideally, begin preparing when you enrol in the AP course. Consistent study throughout the year is far more effective than cramming in the spring. If you are self-studying for an AP exam, start at least four to six months before the exam date.

### Can I self-study for AP exams?

Yes. The College Board allows anyone to register for AP exams, regardless of whether they are enrolled in an AP course. Self-study requires discipline and a clear study plan. Use the course description as your roadmap, obtain a good textbook, and practise with released exams.

### How do AP exams compare to IB exams?

AP exams are subject-specific and test content knowledge and analytical skills within a single discipline. IB exams are part of a broader diploma programme that includes internal assessments, an extended essay, and Theory of Knowledge. Both are rigorous, but they differ in structure and philosophy. Many students take both depending on their school's offerings.

### Are AP courses worth it for university admissions?

AP courses demonstrate academic rigour and college readiness. Admissions committees view AP coursework favourably, especially when you perform well. However, AP courses are only one component of your application. Strong grades, test scores, extracurricular activities, and essays all matter. Take AP courses where you can excel, not just to fill a transcript.

### What is the difference between AP Calculus AB and BC?

Calculus AB covers differential and integral calculus at a foundational level. Calculus BC includes all AB content plus sequences, series, parametric equations, polar coordinates, and more advanced integration techniques. Calculus BC is more demanding and covers about 60% more material. If you have a strong数学 background, BC may be the better choice, but AB provides a solid foundation for most STEM majors.

### How do I handle AP exam stress?

Start preparing early to reduce last-minute pressure. Break your study plan into manageable daily tasks. Practise under timed conditions to build confidence. Get adequate sleep before the exam. Remember that a single exam score does not define your academic potential. Use study groups, office hours, and online resources when you need support.
