## Common Mistakes

**Treating AP courses as easy alternatives to university courses:** AP exams cover college-level material and require serious preparation. The pass rate for AP Calculus BC is roughly 40% for scores of 5 — nearly 60% of students score 3 or below. Underestimating the difficulty leads to inadequate preparation.

**Focusing on test-taking strategies without understanding concepts:** AP exams increasingly test conceptual understanding and application, not just rote memorisation. Knowing the chain rule formula is insufficient — you must understand when and why to apply it in novel contexts. Conceptual gaps become exposed on the free-response sections.

**Neglecting to practise with official College Board materials:** Third-party practice tests may not match the style, difficulty, or content emphasis of actual AP exams. Always use official released exams and scoring guidelines to calibrate your preparation and understand what graders expect.

## Intuition

AP courses are designed to be the equivalent of first-year university courses — they are not lighter versions of college classes but condensed versions that demand the same depth of understanding in less time. The key to succeeding in AP is recognising that the exam tests **conceptual understanding and application**, not rote memorisation. You can memorise every formula for AP Calculus, but if you cannot explain *why* the chain rule works or apply it to a novel problem, you will struggle on the free-response questions.

The subjects covered here — Calculus, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Statistics, History, English, and Computer Science — share a common structure: foundational concepts build on each other sequentially, and gaps in early material compound later. This means you cannot afford to skip topics you find difficult. The practice problems and flashcards in these notes are designed to build the kind of understanding that lets you transfer knowledge between contexts — which is exactly what AP exams reward.

Approach your AP study with active recall (attempt problems before reading explanations), spaced repetition (revisit difficult material after increasing intervals), and interleaving (mix topics rather than studying one to completion). These evidence-based strategies are far more effective than re-reading notes or highlighting. The goal is not just to pass the AP exam but to build a foundation of understanding that will serve you in university and beyond.
