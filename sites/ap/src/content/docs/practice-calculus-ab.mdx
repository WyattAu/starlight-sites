### Limits and Continuity

Limits are the foundation of calculus. Before you can differentiate or integrate, you need to understand what happens to a function as the input approaches a value — even if the function is undefined there. The key intuition is that limits describe **behaviour near a point**, not behaviour at the point.

**Common limit patterns to recognise:**

- $\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a}$ is the definition of $f'(a)$
- $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1$ (the most important trigonometric limit)
- $\lim_{x \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{1}{x}\right)^x = e$ (the definition of $e$)

**When L'Hopital's rule applies:** Only when the limit produces $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$. Always verify the indeterminate form first.

**Common Mistakes:**

- Applying L'Hopital's rule without verifying the indeterminate form first
- Forgetting that limits describe behaviour *near* a point, not *at* the point
- Confusing $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ with $f(a) = L$

### Derivatives

The derivative measures the **instantaneous rate of change** of a function. Geometrically, it is the slope of the tangent line. The power rule, product rule, quotient rule, and chain rule are your computational tools — but understanding *why* the chain rule works (composition of functions) matters more than memorising the formula.

**The chain rule in plain English:** If $y$ depends on $u$, and $u$ depends on $x$, then the rate of change of $y$ with respect to $x$ is the product of the individual rates: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}$.

**Common Mistakes:**

- Forgetting the chain rule when differentiating composite functions
- Confusing the product rule $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$ with the quotient rule
- Applying the power rule to functions like $e^x$ or $\sin x$

### Integrals

Integration is the reverse of differentiation, but its deeper meaning is **accumulation**. The definite integral $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ sums up infinitesimal contributions of $f(x)$ over the interval $[a, b]$. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects the two branches: differentiation and integration are inverse operations.

**Common integration strategies:**

- **u-substitution:** Use when you see a function and its derivative together
- **Integration by parts:** Use for products of different types of functions (polynomial × exponential, polynomial × trig)
- **Recognise special forms:** $\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \ln|x| + C$, $\int e^x\,dx = e^x + C$

**Common Mistakes:**

- Forgetting the constant of integration $+C$ on indefinite integrals
- Incorrectly applying u-substitution (wrong $du$ or wrong limits)
- Confusing definite and indefinite integrals

---

### Differential Equations

Differential equations describe how quantities change in relation to each other. The most common type on the AP exam is **separation of variables**: rearrange the equation so all $y$ terms are on one side and all $x$ terms on the other, then integrate both sides. Exponential growth and decay ($\frac{dy}{dt} = ky$) and logistic growth ($\frac{dy}{dt} = ky(1 - y/L)$) are the models you must know.

**Common Mistakes:**

- Forgetting to separate variables before integrating
- Mixing up exponential growth ($k > 0$) and decay ($k < 0$)
- Incorrectly applying initial conditions to find the constant $C$
