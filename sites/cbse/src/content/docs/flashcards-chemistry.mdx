## Intuition

Chemistry at the Class 12 level connects the microscopic world of atoms and electrons to macroscopic observations like colour changes, energy release, and reaction rates. Think of coordination compounds as molecular architecture — central metal ions surrounded by ligand "building blocks" that determine the compound's colour, magnetism, and reactivity. Electrochemistry is the bridge between chemical energy and electrical energy, like a battery converting stored reactions into usable power.

## How to Use These Flashcards

**First pass:** Go through all 20 cards without rating — just flip and read. This familiarises you with the content and identifies weak areas.

**Rating system:** 1 = could not recall, 2 = recalled with difficulty, 3 = recalled correctly, 4 = instant recall. The spaced repetition algorithm shows cards rated 1-2 more frequently.

**Session timing:** 10-15 minutes per session is optimal. Multiple short sessions beat one long session for retention. Review before sleep for better consolidation.

**Common Pitfalls**

- **Electrochemistry**: Confusing anode/cathode signs in galvanic vs electrolytic cells — in galvanic cells the anode is negative, but in electrolytic cells it is positive.
- **Kinetics**: Forgetting that catalysts lower activation energy without changing the overall enthalpy change of reaction — ΔH remains the same.
- **Coordination compounds**: Mixing up the CFT (Crystal Field Theory) splitting diagrams for octahedral and tetrahedral geometries, leading to wrong d-orbital energy level predictions.
- **Surface chemistry**: Confusing physisorption with chemisorption — physisorption uses weak van der Waals forces (reversible, low energy), while chemisorption involves chemical bond formation (often irreversible, higher energy).
- **Biomolecules**: Mixing up the structures of DNA and RNA bases — both have purines and pyrimidines, but the sugar (deoxyribose vs ribose) and strand count (double vs single) differ fundamentally.

## Study Tips

- **Draw diagrams for coordination compounds:** Sketch the d-orbital splitting for octahedral, tetrahedral, and square planar geometries. Visual recall is more reliable than memorising energy level orderings.
- **Write the Nernst equation from memory:** Practice deriving the relationship between cell potential and concentration without looking at notes. This builds the intuition needed for non-standard conditions.
- **Connect kinetics to real reactions:** Think about why rusting is slow (high activation energy, no catalyst) while combustion is fast (lower activation energy, available oxygen). Real-world examples anchor abstract concepts.
