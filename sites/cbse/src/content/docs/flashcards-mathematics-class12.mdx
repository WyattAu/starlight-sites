## Intuition

Calculus is the mathematics of change — derivatives measure instantaneous rates while integrals accumulate quantities. Think of a derivative as the slope of a tangent line at any point on a curve, and an integral as the total area under that curve. Matrices are rectangular arrays of numbers that encode linear transformations, like stretching, rotating, or reflecting shapes in space. The determinant tells you how much a transformation scales area (in 2D) or volume (in 3D).

## How to Use These Flashcards

**First pass:** Go through all 20 cards without rating — just flip and read. This familiarises you with the content and identifies weak areas.

**Rating system:** 1 = could not recall, 2 = recalled with difficulty, 3 = recalled correctly, 4 = instant recall. The spaced repetition algorithm shows cards rated 1-2 more frequently.

**Active recall:** Before flipping, try to work through the derivation or proof from memory. Passive reading does not build mathematical intuition.

**Common Pitfalls**

- **Calculus**: Confusing the chain rule application — forgetting to multiply by the derivative of the inner function, e.g. d/dx[sin(2x)] = cos(2x)·2, not just cos(2x).
- **Matrices**: Assuming AB = BA — matrix multiplication is not commutative, so the order of multiplication matters enormously.
- **Probability**: Mixing up P(A∩B) with P(A|B) — the joint probability of A and B is different from the probability of A given that B has occurred.
