## CBSE Structure

**CBSE (Central Board of Secondary Education)**: India's national board of education, one of the largest and most influential school education boards.

**NCERT (National Council of Educational Research and Training)**: The organisation that publishes textbooks and sets the curriculum that CBSE follows.

**Class 11**: The first year of senior secondary school, building foundational knowledge in Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics.

**Class 12**: The final year of senior secondary school, when board examinations are taken.

**Board Examination**: The end-of-year exams set and marked by CBSE, taken at the end of Class 12.

**Senior School Certificate (Class XII)**: The qualification awarded upon passing Class 12 board examinations.

**Secondary School Examination (Class X)**: The qualification awarded upon passing Class 10 board examinations.

**Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (CCE)**: The assessment system combining formative and summative evaluation.

**Internal Assessment**: Marks awarded by schools based on practical work, projects, and periodic tests.

**Practical Examination**: Hands-on assessment in laboratory-based subjects (Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Computer Science).

**Theory Examination**: The written examination component, typically 70–80% of the total marks.

## Subjects and Streams

**Science Stream**: Includes Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics, and Biology (PCM or PCB).

**Commerce Stream**: Includes Accountancy, Business Studies, Economics, and Mathematics.

**Humanities/Arts Stream**: Includes History, Political Science, Geography, Psychology, and other subjects.

**PCM**: Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics — the standard combination for engineering aspirants.

**PCB**: Physics, Chemistry, Biology — the standard combination for medical aspirants.

**Elective Subjects**: Optional subjects students can choose alongside compulsory subjects.

**Work Experience**: Practical subjects including Computer Science, Informatics Practices, and Engineering Graphics.

## Assessment and Grading

**Grading System**: CBSE uses a letter grading system (A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2, D, E) based on marks.

**A1**: Top 1/8th of passing candidates, or marks ≥ 91%.

**A2**: Next 1/8th, or marks 81–90%.

**B1**: Next 1/8th, or marks 71–80%.

**B2**: Next 1/8th, or marks 61–70%.

**C1**: Next 1/8th, or marks 51–60%.

**C2**: Next 1/8th, or marks 41–50%.

**D**: Next 1/8th, or marks 33–40%.

**E**: Below 33% (fail).

**Passing Marks**: 33% in each subject (theory + internal assessment combined).

**Marking Scheme**: The official CBSE guide showing how marks are awarded for each question.

**Normalization**: Process of adjusting marks across different exam sessions to ensure fairness.

## JEE (Joint Entrance Examination)

**JEE Main**: The qualifying examination for JEE Advanced, conducted by NTA.

**JEE Advanced**: The examination for admission to IITs (Indian Institutes of Technology).

**NTA (National Testing Agency)**: The body that conducts JEE Main and NEET examinations.

**IIT (Indian Institute of Technology)**: India's premier engineering institutions, accessible through JEE Advanced.

**NIT (National Institute of Technology)**: Government-funded engineering institutions, accessible through JEE Main.

**Percentile Score**: The score indicating the percentage of candidates who scored below you.

**All India Rank (AIR)**: Your national ranking based on JEE Main or JEE Advanced score.

**Cutoff Score**: The minimum score required to qualify for JEE Advanced or specific college admissions.

**counselling**: The process of selecting and confirming college seats based on JEE rank.

**JoSAA (Joint Seat Allocation Authority)**: The body managing seat allocation for IITs, NITs, and other institutions.

## NEET (National Eligibility cum Entrance Test)

**NEET**: The sole entrance examination for medical admissions in India (MBBS, BDS, AYUSH).

**MBBS (Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery)**: The undergraduate medical degree.

**BDS (Bachelor of Dental Surgery)**: The undergraduate dental degree.

**AYUSH**: Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy medical systems.

**Medical Counselling Committee (MCC)**: Manages counselling for AIIMS, JIPMER, and other medical institutions.

**All India Quota**: 15% of seats in government medical colleges allocated through central counselling.

**State Quota**: 85% of seats in government medical colleges allocated through state counselling.

**Bond**: A commitment to serve in rural areas or a government institution after completing medical education.

## Study Strategies

**NCERT Textbook**: The foundation of both board exams and competitive exams. Read every chapter thoroughly.

**Exemplar Problems**: Advanced problems published by NCERT, useful for JEE/NEET preparation.

**Previous Year Questions**: Past board and competitive exam papers, essential for understanding exam patterns.

**Mock Test**: A practice exam simulating real exam conditions.

**Time Management**: Allocating time efficiently during exams — roughly 1–2 minutes per MCQ in JEE.

**Revision Notes**: Concise summaries of key concepts for quick review.

**Formula Sheet**: A compilation of important formulas for each subject.

**Flashcards**: Study cards for memorizing facts, especially useful for inorganic chemistry.

**Test Series**: Regular practice tests simulating competitive exam conditions.

**Doubt Clearing**: Sessions dedicated to resolving confusion on specific topics.
