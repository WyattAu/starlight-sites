## Intuition

**Chemistry is the science of substances and their transformations:** From everyday reactions like cooking to industrial processes like steel-making, chemistry explains what things are made of and how they change. The mole concept connects atomic-scale reactions to laboratory-scale quantities.

**Why it matters:** Chemical understanding is essential for healthcare, environmental science, food technology, and material development.

**The key insight:** Balancing chemical equations is not just arithmetic — it reflects the physical law of conservation of mass that atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed.

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing intermolecular and intramolecular forces:** Hydrogen bonding, dipole-dipole interactions, and van der Waals forces are intermolecular (between molecules) and affect physical properties like boiling point. Covalent and ionic bonds are intramolecular (within molecules) and determine chemical identity. Mixing these up leads to incorrect predictions about boiling points and solubility.

**Using the wrong value of the gas constant $R$:** The value of $R$ depends on the units used: $8.314$ J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$ for energy calculations, $0.0821$ L atm K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$ for gas law problems with pressure in atm. Using $8.314$ when the problem uses litres and atmospheres gives wrong numerical answers.

**Forgetting to balance redox equations before applying electrochemistry formulas:** The Nernst equation and EMF calculations require balanced half-reactions with correct electron counts. Unbalanced equations give incorrect cell potentials and stoichiometric ratios. Always balance atoms first, then charges, before substituting into formulas.
