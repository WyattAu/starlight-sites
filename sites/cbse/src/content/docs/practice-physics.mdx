## Electromagnetism and Optics

**Q1. A circular coil of 100 turns, radius 10 cm, carries a current of 1 A. What is the magnetic field at the centre of the coil? ($\\\\mu_0 = 4\\\\pi \\\\times 10^\{-7\}$ T m A$^\{-1\}$)**

A. $6.28 \\\\times 10^\{-4\}$ T
B. $3.14 \\\\times 10^\{-4\}$ T
C. $1.26 \\\\times 10^\{-3\}$ T
D. $2.00 \\\\times 10^\{-5\}$ T',

**Answer: A**

**Answer: A** — $B = \\\\dfrac\{\\\\mu_0 n I\}\{2R\} = \\\\dfrac\{4\\\\pi \\\\times 10^\{-7\} \\\\times 100 \\\\times 1\}\{2 \\\\times 0.10\} = \\\\dfrac\{4\\\\pi \\\\times 10^\{-5\}\}\{0.2\} = 2\\\\pi \\\\times 10^\{-4\} \\\\approx 6.28 \\\\times 10^\{-4\}$ T.


**Q2. In Young's double-slit experiment using light of wavelength $\\\\lambda = 600$ nm, the fringe width is 2 mm. If the distance between the slits is doubled, the new fringe width will be:**

A. 4 mm
B. 2 mm
C. 0.5 mm
D. 1 mm

**Answer: D**

**Answer: D** — Fringe width $\\\\beta = \\\\dfrac\{\\\\lambda D\}\{d\}$, where $D$ is the distance to the screen and $d$ is the slit separation. If $d$ is doubled, $\\\\beta$ is halved. New fringe width $= \\\\dfrac\{2\}\{2\} = 1$ mm.


**Q3. A convex lens of focal length 20 cm is placed in contact with a concave lens of focal length 30 cm. What is the power of the combination?**

A. $-1.67$ D
B. $+5.00$ D
C. $+0.60$ D
D. $+1.67$ D

**Answer: D**

**Answer: D** — $P_1 = \\\\dfrac\{1\}\{f_1\} = \\\\dfrac\{1\}\{0.20\} = +5$ D (convex). $P_2 = \\\\dfrac\{1\}\{f_2\} = \\\\dfrac\{1\}\{-0.30\} = -3.33$ D (concave). $P_\{\\\\text\{net\}\} = P_1 + P_2 = 5 - 3.33 = +1.67$ D. The equivalent focal length is $f = 1/P = 1/1.67 \\\\approx 60$ cm (convex).

## Wave Optics and Photoelectric Effect

**Q5. In a single-slit diffraction experiment, the width of the central maximum is found to be 4 mm. If the slit width is halved, the width of the central maximum becomes:**

A. 2 mm
B. 16 mm
C. 8 mm
D. 4 mm

**Answer: C**

**Answer: C** — The width of the central maximum in single-slit diffraction is $2y = \\\\dfrac\{2\\\\lambda D\}\{a\}$, where $a$ is the slit width. If $a$ is halved, $2y$ doubles. New width $= 2 \\\\times 4 = 8$ mm. Narrower slit produces broader diffraction pattern.


**Q6. The work function of a metal surface is 2 eV. If light of wavelength 400 nm is incident on it, what is the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons? ($h = 6.63 \\\\times 10^\{-34\}$ J s, $c = 3 \\\\times 10^8$ m/s, $1$ eV $= 1.6 \\\\times 10^\{-19\}$ J)**

A. $3.105$ eV
B. $1.105$ eV
C. $0.895$ eV
D. $2.000$ eV

**Answer: B**

**Answer: B** — Energy of photon: $E = \\\\dfrac\{hc\}\{\\\\lambda\} = \\\\dfrac\{6.63 \\\\times 10^\{-34\} \\\\times 3 \\\\times 10^8\}\{400 \\\\times 10^\{-9\}\} = 4.97 \\\\times 10^\{-19\}$ J $= \\\\dfrac\{4.97 \\\\times 10^\{-19\}\}\{1.6 \\\\times 10^\{-19\}\} = 3.105$ eV. By Einstein's equation: $K_\{\\\\max\} = E - \\\\phi = 3.105 - 2.0 = 1.105$ eV.


**Q7. The de Broglie wavelength associated with an electron accelerated through a potential difference of 100 V is approximately: ($m_e = 9.1 \\\\times 10^\{-31\}$ kg, $e = 1.6 \\\\times 10^\{-19\}$ C, $h = 6.63 \\\\times 10^\{-34\}$ J s)**

A. 0.123 nm
B. 1.23 nm
C. 0.0123 nm
D. 12.3 nm

**Answer: A**

**Answer: A** — $\\\\lambda = \\\\dfrac\{h\}\{\\\\sqrt\{2m_e eV\}\} = \\\\dfrac\{6.63 \\\\times 10^\{-34\}\}\{\\\\sqrt\{2 \\\\times 9.1 \\\\times 10^\{-31\} \\\\times 1.6 \\\\times 10^\{-19\} \\\\times 100\}\} = \\\\dfrac\{6.63 \\\\times 10^\{-34\}\}\{\\\\sqrt\{2.912 \\\\times 10^\{-47\}\}\} = \\\\dfrac\{6.63 \\\\times 10^\{-34\}\}\{5.396 \\\\times 10^\{-24\}\} = 1.228 \\\\times 10^\{-10\}$ m $= 0.1228$ nm $\\\\approx 0.123$ nm.


## Semiconductor Electronics

**Q8. In a p-n junction diode, the potential barrier is approximately 0.3 V for Ge and 0.7 V for Si. If a Si diode is forward biased with a voltage of 0.5 V applied, what is the current through the diode?**

A. Moderately high
B. Very high
C. Cannot be determined
D. Nearly zero

**Answer: D**

**Answer: D** — For a forward-biased Si diode, the applied voltage must exceed the knee voltage (barrier potential) of approximately 0.7 V before significant current flows. Since 0.5 V < 0.7 V, the current is negligibly small (nearly zero). The diode is technically forward biased but below the threshold for conduction.


**Q9. In a transistor, the current gain $\\\\beta = 99$. If the base current $I_B = 20$ $\\\\mu$A, the collector current $I_C$ is:**

A. 2.00 mA
B. 0.20 mA
C. 1.98 mA
D. 0.99 mA

**Answer: C**

**Answer: C** — $\\\\beta = \\\\dfrac\{I_C\}\{I_B\}$, so $I_C = \\\\beta \\\\cdot I_B = 99 \\\\times 20 \\\\times 10^\{-6\} = 1980 \\\\times 10^\{-6\}$ A $= 1.98$ mA. The emitter current $I_E = I_C + I_B = 1.98 + 0.02 = 2.00$ mA.


**Q10. In an LED (Light Emitting Diode), the colour of emitted light depends on:**

A. The band gap energy of the semiconductor material
B. The intensity of forward current
C. The applied reverse voltage
D. The physical size of the diode',

**Answer: A**

**Answer: A** — The colour of light emitted by an LED is determined by the energy of photons released during electron-hole recombination across the band gap: $E = hc/\\\\lambda$. A wider band gap produces shorter wavelength (higher energy) light. For example, GaAs ($E_g \\\\approx 1.43$ eV) emits infrared; GaAsP ($\\\\approx 1.9$ eV) emits red; GaN ($\\\\approx 3.4$ eV) emits blue/violet.


## Intuition

**Physics describes the fundamental rules governing the natural world:** From motion to electricity to optics, physics provides mathematical models that predict physical behaviour. It is the most quantitative of the sciences.

**Why it matters:** Physics understanding enables technological innovation and explains phenomena from everyday events to cosmic processes.

**The key insight:** Units are your built-in error detection system — if units do not balance on both sides of an equation, an error has occurred.

## Common Mistakes

**Using the wrong value of gravitational acceleration:** CBSE physics problems use $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$ (or sometimes $g = 10$ m/s$^2$ for approximation). Using $g = 32.2$ ft/s$^2$ (imperial) or mixing units gives incorrect numerical answers. Always check whether the problem specifies $g = 9.8$ or $g = 10$.

**Confusing the lens formula sign convention:** The lens formula $1/f = 1/v - 1/u$ uses sign conventions where distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive. A common error is using $1/f = 1/v + 1/u$ (mirror formula) for lenses. Convex lenses have positive focal lengths; concave lenses have negative focal lengths.

**Confusing drift velocity with thermal velocity of electrons:** Drift velocity ($v_d \approx 10^{-4}$ m/s) is the average velocity of electrons in a conductor under an applied field. Thermal velocity ($\approx 10^5$ m/s) is the random velocity due to thermal agitation. The drift velocity is orders of magnitude smaller, but it is what determines current flow.
