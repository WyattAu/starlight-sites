## Electromagnetism and Optics

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"A circular coil of 100 turns, radius 10 cm, carries a current of 1 A. What is the magnetic field at the centre of the coil? ($\\mu_0 = 4\\pi \\times 10^{-7}$ T m A$^{-1}$)&quot; options={[ '$6.28 \\times 10^{-4}$ T', '$3.14 \\times 10^{-4}$ T', '$1.26 \\times 10^{-3}$ T', '$2.00 \\times 10^{-5}$ T', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;$B = \\dfrac{\\mu_0 n I}{2R} = \\dfrac{4\\pi \\times 10^{-7} \\times 100 \\times 1}{2 \\times 0.10} = \\dfrac{4\\pi \\times 10^{-5}}{0.2} = 2\\pi \\times 10^{-4} \\approx 6.28 \\times 10^{-4}$ T."} difficulty="easy"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"In Young\'s double-slit experiment using light of wavelength $\\lambda = 600$ nm, the fringe width is 2 mm. If the distance between the slits is doubled, the new fringe width will be:&quot; options={['1 mm', '4 mm', '2 mm', '0.5 mm']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;Fringe width $\\beta = \\dfrac{\\lambda D}{d}$, where $D$ is the distance to the screen and $d$ is the slit separation. If $d$ is doubled, $\\beta$ is halved. New fringe width $= \\dfrac{2}{2} = 1$ mm."} difficulty="easy"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"A convex lens of focal length 20 cm is placed in contact with a concave lens of focal length 30 cm. What is the power of the combination?&quot; options={['$+1.67$ D', '$-1.67$ D', '$+5.00$ D', '$+0.60$ D']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;$P_1 = \\dfrac{1}{f_1} = \\dfrac{1}{0.20} = +5$ D (convex). $P_2 = \\dfrac{1}{f_2} = \\dfrac{1}{-0.30} = -3.33$ D (concave). $P_{\\text{net}} = P_1 + P_2 = 5 - 3.33 = +1.67$ D. The equivalent focal length is $f = 1/P = 1/1.67 \\approx 60$ cm (convex)."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"A ray of light travelling in a denser medium strikes a rarer medium at an angle of incidence $i$. The reflected ray is completely polarised. The angle between the reflected and refracted rays is:&quot; options={['$90^\\circ$', '$0^\\circ$', '$180^\\circ$', '$i$']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;When the reflected ray is completely polarised, the angle of incidence equals Brewster\'s angle $i_B$. At Brewster\'s angle, the reflected and refracted rays are perpendicular to each other: $i_B + r = 90^\\circ$, where $r$ is the angle of refraction. Hence the angle between the reflected and refracted rays is always $90^\\circ$."} difficulty="medium"  />

## Wave Optics and Photoelectric Effect

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"In a single-slit diffraction experiment, the width of the central maximum is found to be 4 mm. If the slit width is halved, the width of the central maximum becomes:&quot; options={['8 mm', '2 mm', '16 mm', '4 mm']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;The width of the central maximum in single-slit diffraction is $2y = \\dfrac{2\\lambda D}{a}$, where $a$ is the slit width. If $a$ is halved, $2y$ doubles. New width $= 2 \\times 4 = 8$ mm. Narrower slit produces broader diffraction pattern."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"The work function of a metal surface is 2 eV. If light of wavelength 400 nm is incident on it, what is the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons? ($h = 6.63 \\times 10^{-34}$ J s, $c = 3 \\times 10^8$ m/s, $1$ eV $= 1.6 \\times 10^{-19}$ J)&quot; options={['$1.105$ eV', '$3.105$ eV', '$0.895$ eV', '$2.000$ eV']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;Energy of photon: $E = \\dfrac{hc}{\\lambda} = \\dfrac{6.63 \\times 10^{-34} \\times 3 \\times 10^8}{400 \\times 10^{-9}} = 4.97 \\times 10^{-19}$ J $= \\dfrac{4.97 \\times 10^{-19}}{1.6 \\times 10^{-19}} = 3.105$ eV. By Einstein\'s equation: $K_{\\max} = E - \\phi = 3.105 - 2.0 = 1.105$ eV."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"The de Broglie wavelength associated with an electron accelerated through a potential difference of 100 V is approximately: ($m_e = 9.1 \\times 10^{-31}$ kg, $e = 1.6 \\times 10^{-19}$ C, $h = 6.63 \\times 10^{-34}$ J s)&quot; options={['0.123 nm', '1.23 nm', '0.0123 nm', '12.3 nm']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;$\\lambda = \\dfrac{h}{\\sqrt{2m_e eV}} = \\dfrac{6.63 \\times 10^{-34}}{\\sqrt{2 \\times 9.1 \\times 10^{-31} \\times 1.6 \\times 10^{-19} \\times 100}} = \\dfrac{6.63 \\times 10^{-34}}{\\sqrt{2.912 \\times 10^{-47}}} = \\dfrac{6.63 \\times 10^{-34}}{5.396 \\times 10^{-24}} = 1.228 \\times 10^{-10}$ m $= 0.1228$ nm $\\approx 0.123$ nm."} difficulty="hard"  />

## Semiconductor Electronics

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"In a p-n junction diode, the potential barrier is approximately 0.3 V for Ge and 0.7 V for Si. If a Si diode is forward biased with a voltage of 0.5 V applied, what is the current through the diode?&quot; options={['Nearly zero', 'Moderately high', 'Very high', 'Cannot be determined']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;For a forward-biased Si diode, the applied voltage must exceed the knee voltage (barrier potential) of approximately 0.7 V before significant current flows. Since 0.5 V < 0.7 V, the current is negligibly small (nearly zero). The diode is technically forward biased but below the threshold for conduction."} difficulty="easy"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"In a transistor, the current gain $\\beta = 99$. If the base current $I_B = 20$ $\\mu$A, the collector current $I_C$ is:&quot; options={['1.98 mA', '2.00 mA', '0.20 mA', '0.99 mA']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;$\\beta = \\dfrac{I_C}{I_B}$, so $I_C = \\beta \\cdot I_B = 99 \\times 20 \\times 10^{-6} = 1980 \\times 10^{-6}$ A $= 1.98$ mA. The emitter current $I_E = I_C + I_B = 1.98 + 0.02 = 2.00$ mA."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"In an LED (Light Emitting Diode), the colour of emitted light depends on:&quot; options={[ 'The band gap energy of the semiconductor material', 'The intensity of forward current', 'The applied reverse voltage', 'The physical size of the diode', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;The colour of light emitted by an LED is determined by the energy of photons released during electron-hole recombination across the band gap: $E = hc/\\lambda$. ## Intuition

**Physics describes the fundamental rules governing the natural world:** From motion to electricity to optics, physics provides mathematical models that predict physical behaviour. It is the most quantitative of the sciences.

**Why it matters:** Physics understanding enables technological innovation and explains phenomena from everyday events to cosmic processes.

**The key insight:** Units are your built-in error detection system — if units do not balance on both sides of an equation, an error has occurred.

## Common Mistakes

**Using the wrong value of gravitational acceleration:** CBSE physics problems use $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$ (or sometimes $g = 10$ m/s$^2$ for approximation). Using $g = 32.2$ ft/s$^2$ (imperial) or mixing units gives incorrect numerical answers. Always check whether the problem specifies $g = 9.8$ or $g = 10$.

**Confusing the lens formula sign convention:** The lens formula $1/f = 1/v - 1/u$ uses sign conventions where distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive. A common error is using $1/f = 1/v + 1/u$ (mirror formula) for lenses. Convex lenses have positive focal lengths; concave lenses have negative focal lengths.

**Confusing drift velocity with thermal velocity of electrons:** Drift velocity ($v_d \approx 10^{-4}$ m/s) is the average velocity of electrons in a conductor under an applied field. Thermal velocity ($\approx 10^5$ m/s) is the random velocity due to thermal agitation. The drift velocity is orders of magnitude smaller, but it is what determines current flow.
