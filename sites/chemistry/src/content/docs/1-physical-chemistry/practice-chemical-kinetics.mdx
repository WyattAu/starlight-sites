**Answer: A**

**Answer: A**

**Q4. For a reaction mechanism with a slow rate-determining step followed by fast steps, what is the relationship between the rate law and the rate-determining step?**

B. The rate law always matches the overall stoichiometry
C. The rate law has no relationship to any individual step
D. The rate law is determined by the fastest step',

**Answer: A**
— For an elementary (single molecular event) rate-determining step, the rate law directly reflects its molecularity. For example, if the RDS is $\text{A} + \text{B} \to \text{products}$ (bimolecular), the rate law is $v = k[A][B]$. If the RDS involves intermediates, the steady-state approximation or pre-equilibrium approximation may be needed to express the rate law in terms of reactants.

**Q6. The Arrhenius equation $k = A e^{-E_a/RT}$ relates the rate constant to temperature. What happens to the rate constant if the temperature increases from 300 K to 310 K for a reaction with $E_a = 50$ kJ/mol?**

A. The rate constant approximately doubles ($k_{310}

**Answer: A**
— Using the two-point Arrhenius form: $\ln(k_2/k_1) = (E_a/R)(1/T_1 - 1/T_2) = (50000/8.314)(1/300 - 1/310) = 6014 \times 0.0001074 = 0.645$. **Q8. A catalyst increases the rate of a chemical reaction. Which of the following is true about the catalyzed vs. uncatalyzed reaction?**

A. The catalyst lowers the activation energy but does not change $\Delta H$ or $\Delta G^\circ$; it provides an alternative pathway
B. The catalyst changes the equilibrium constant
C. The catalyst increases the activation energy to slow the reverse reaction
D. The catalyst is consumed in the reaction',

**Answer: A**
— A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy $E_a$. It accelerates both forward and reverse reactions equally, so the equilibrium constant $K$ (and thus $\Delta G^\circ$) is unchanged. $\Delta H$ is also unchanged since the answer varies based on only on initial and final states. A true catalyst is not consumed in the reaction.

**Q9. For a consecutive reaction $\text{A} \xrightarrow{k_1} \text{B} \xrightarrow{k_2} \text{C}$, under what condition does the concentration of intermediate B reach a maximum?**

A. When $k_1[A] = k_2[B]$, i.e., the rate of formation of B equals the rate of consumption of B
B. When $[A] = [B] = [C]$
C. When $k_1 = k_2$
D. When $[A] = 0$',

**Answer: A**
— B is formed from A ($d[B]/dt = k_1[A] - k_2[B]$) and consumed to give C. B reaches maximum concentration when $d[B]/dt = 0$, i.e., $k_1[A] = k_2[B]$. Before this point, B accumulates ($k_1[A] > k_2[B]$); after, B is consumed faster than formed ($k_2[B] > k_1[A]$). The time of maximum B depends on both rate constants.
