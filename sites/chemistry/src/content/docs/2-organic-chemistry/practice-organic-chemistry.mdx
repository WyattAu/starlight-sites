## Organic Chemistry -- Practice Problems

## Intuition

**Organic chemistry is the chemistry of carbon's infinite variety:** Carbon's ability to form four bonds, chains, rings, and double bonds with itself creates millions of compounds. Understanding functional groups — the reactive parts of molecules — lets you predict how any organic molecule will behave.

**Why it matters:** Organic chemistry is the foundation of pharmaceuticals, plastics, fuels, food chemistry, and biochemistry.

**The key insight:** Functional groups are the "alphabet" of organic chemistry — once you recognise an -OH, -COOH, or -NH₂, you can predict reactivity without memorising every individual compound.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Predicting SN1 vs SN2

**Problem:** For each substrate-nucleophile pair, determine whether SN1 or SN2 is favored and predict the product:

(a) $(R)$-2-bromobutane + $\text{NaOCH}_3$ in $\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$
(b) $(R)$-3-bromo-3-methylhexane + $\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$

**Solution:**

**(a)** Substrate: secondary alkyl halide. Nucleophile: methoxide (strong). Solvent: methanol (polar protic).

**Analysis:** Strong nucleophile + secondary substrate → **SN2 favored**

**Product:** $(S)$-2-methoxybutane (inversion of configuration via Walden inversion)

**(b)** Substrate: tertiary alkyl halide. Nucleophile: methanol (weak). Solvent: methanol (polar protic).

**Analysis:** Weak nucleophile + tertiary substrate → **SN1 favored**

**Product:** Racemic 3-methoxy-3-methylhexane (carbocation intermediate → planar → attack from both faces)

**Decision rules:**
| Factor | SN1 | SN2 |
|--------|-----|-----|
| Substrate | $3^\circ > 2^\circ$ | $1^\circ > 2^\circ$ |
| Nucleophile | Weak (H₂O, ROH) | Strong (⁻OH, ⁻OR) |
| Solvent | Polar protic | Polar aprotic |
| Stereochemistry | Racemization | Inversion |

### Example 2: Retrosynthetic Analysis

**Problem:** Design a synthesis of 2-methyl-2-pentanol from 1-bromopropane and acetone.

**Solution:**

**Target:** 2-methyl-2-pentanol (tertiary alcohol)

**Retrosynthesis:**
$$\text{2-methyl-2-pentanol} \Rightarrow \text{acetone} + \text{propyl Grignard}$$

**Forward synthesis:**

**Step 1:** Prepare Grignard reagent
$$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Br} + \text{Mg} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{MgBr}$$

**Step 2:** Grignard addition to acetone
$$\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{MgBr} \rightarrow \text{alkoxide intermediate}$$

**Step 3:** Aqueous workup
$$\text{alkoxide} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} \text{(CH}_3)_2\text{C(OH)CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$$

**Key insight:** Grignard reagents are powerful because they create new C-C bonds. The nucleophilic carbon attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon, extending the carbon skeleton.

### Example 3: IR Spectrum Interpretation

**Problem:** A compound has the following IR absorptions: 3300 cm⁻¹ (broad), 2950 cm⁻¹, 1710 cm⁻¹ (strong), 1220 cm⁻¹. Identify the functional group.

**Solution:**

| Absorption (cm⁻¹) | Assignment |
|-------------------|------------|
| 3300 (broad) | O-H stretch (carboxylic acid) |
| 2950 | C-H stretch (sp³) |
| 1710 (strong) | C=O stretch |
| 1220 | C-O stretch |

**Diagnostic pattern:** Broad O-H (2500-3300) + C=O (~1710) = **carboxylic acid**

**Confirmation:** The broad O-H absorption spanning 2500-3300 cm⁻¹ is unique to carboxylic acids. Alcohols show O-H at 3200-3600 cm⁻¹ but lack the C=O. Ketones show C=O but lack the broad O-H.

**Key insight:** IR spectroscopy identifies functional groups by their characteristic vibrations. Always look for diagnostic pairs: O-H + C=O (acid), N-H + C=O (amide), C=O alone (ketone/aldehyde/ester).

The anti-periplanar geometry requirement means the H-C-C-LG dihedral angle must be approximately $180^\\circ$ for optimal orbital overlap. <PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"In electrophilic aromatic substitution (EAS) of benzene with a nitro group already present, where does the second electrophile preferentially add?&quot; options={[ 'Meta position, because the nitro group is a meta-directing deactivator', 'Ortho and para positions, because the nitro group is an ortho-para director', 'All positions equally, because the nitro group has no directing effect', 'Meta position, because the nitro group is a meta-directing activator', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;The nitro group ($-\\text{NO}_2$) is strongly electron-withdrawing via both resonance and inductive effects. <PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"What is the major product when 2-methylbut-2-ene undergoes acid-catalyzed hydration with $\\text{H}_3\\text{O}^+$?&quot; options={[ '2-methylbutan-2-ol (tertiary alcohol, Markovnikov product)', '2-methylbutan-1-ol (primary alcohol, anti-Markovnikov product)', 'A mixture of 2-methylbutan-2-ol and 3-methylbutan-2-ol', '2-methylbutan-2-ol with rearrangement to a more stable carbocation', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;Acid-catalyzed hydration follows Markovnikov\'s rule: the proton adds to the less substituted carbon, forming the more stable tertiary carbocation at C2. Water then attacks the carbocation, and deprotonation yields 2-methylbutan-2-ol (tertiary alcohol). No rearrangement occurs because the initial carbocation is already tertiary, the most stable possible. ## Stereochemistry

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"A molecule with two chiral centers is described as meso. Which of the following is true?&quot; options={[ 'The molecule has a plane of symmetry and is achiral despite having stereocenters', 'The molecule has no plane of symmetry and exists as a pair of enantiomers', 'The molecule has two chiral centers that are both $R$ configuration', 'The molecule cannot be optically active under any conditions', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;A meso compound contains chiral centers but also has an internal plane of symmetry, making it achiral overall. The mirror images are superimposable. For example, $(2R,3S)$-tartaric acid is meso because it has a plane of symmetry between C2 and C3. A molecule with two different chiral centers and no symmetry element can have up to $2^2 = 4$ stereoisomers (2 pairs of enantiomers), or fewer if some are meso."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"For the reaction of $(R)$-2-bromobutane with sodium methoxide ($\\text{NaOCH}_3$) in methanol, what is the stereochemical outcome of the major product?&quot; options={[ '$(S)$-2-methoxybutane, formed by inversion of configuration via $S_N2$', '$(R)$-2-methoxybutane, formed by retention of configuration via $S_N2$', 'Racemic 2-methoxybutane, formed via $S_N1$ with carbocation intermediate', '$(S)$-2-methoxybutane, formed via $S_N1$ with carbocation rearrangement', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;Sodium methoxide is a strong nucleophile and methanol is a polar protic solvent favoring $S_N2$ for a primary substrate. 2-bromobutane is secondary, so both $S_N1$ and $S_N2$ are possible, but with a strong nucleophile like methoxide, $S_N2$ dominates. The $S_N2$ mechanism gives inversion of configuration (Walden inversion), converting $(R)$ to $(S)$. If a weak nucleophile were used in a polar protic solvent, $S_N1$ with racemization would dominate."} difficulty="hard"  />

## Spectroscopy and Synthesis

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"In proton NMR spectroscopy, what does a triplet with integration of 3H at $\\delta \\approx 1.0$ ppm indicate?&quot; options={[ 'A methyl group ($\\text{CH}_3$) adjacent to a $\\text{CH}_2$ group (coupling to 2 equivalent protons, $n+1=3$)', 'A methyl group adjacent to a $\\text{CH}$ group', 'Three equivalent protons on an aromatic ring', 'A methyl group that is not coupled to any neighbors', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;A triplet arises from the $n+1$ rule: a methyl group ($\\text{CH}_3$, 3 protons) coupled to 2 equivalent neighboring protons ($n=2$) splits into $2+1=3$ peaks. The integration of 3 confirms it is a methyl group. A methyl next to a $\\text{CH}$ would appear as a doublet ($n=1$), and an uncoupled methyl would be a singlet. The chemical shift of ~1.0 ppm is typical for aliphatic methyl groups."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"What is the correct order of reactivity for nucleophilic acyl substitution: acid chloride, anhydride, ester, amide?&quot; options={[ 'Acid chloride > anhydride > ester > amide', 'Amide > ester > anhydride > acid chloride', 'Ester > acid chloride > amide > anhydride', 'Anhydride > acid chloride > amide > ester', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;Reactivity in nucleophilic acyl substitution follows the leaving group ability: $\\text{Cl}^- > \\text{RCOO}^- > \\text{RO}^- > \\text{NH}_2^-$. Acid chlorides are the most reactive because chloride is the best leaving group and the carbonyl carbon is the most electrophilic (strong inductive withdrawal by Cl). Amides are the least reactive because $\\text{NH}_2$ is a poor leaving group and nitrogen donates electron density via resonance. This reactivity trend is central to designing synthetic sequences involving carbonyl transformations."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"Which reagent combination achieves a Grignard reaction to convert a ketone into a tertiary alcohol?&quot; options={[ '$\\text{RMgBr}$ followed by aqueous workup', '$\\text{LiAlH}_4$ followed by aqueous workup', '$\\text{NaBH}_4$ in methanol', '$\\text{H}_2$ with Pd/C catalyst', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;Grignard reagents ($\\text{RMgBr}$) add to the carbonyl carbon of ketones, forming a tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate. Aqueous workup protonates the alkoxide to give a tertiary alcohol (since the ketone already has two carbon substituents on the carbonyl). $\\text{LiAlH}_4$ reduces ketones to secondary alcohols, not tertiary. $\\text{NaBH}_4$ similarly gives secondary alcohols. The Grignard reaction is a powerful C-C bond-forming reaction."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"In infrared spectroscopy, a strong broad absorption around 2500--3300 $\\text{cm}^{-1}$ and a strong absorption near 1700 $\\text{cm}^{-1}$ together indicate the presence of which functional group?&quot; options={[ 'A carboxylic acid ($\\text{COOH}$)', 'An aldehyde ($\\text{CHO}$)', 'A ketone ($\\text{C=O}$)', 'An alcohol ($\\text{OH}$) and an ester', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;The broad absorption at 2500--3300 $\\text{cm}^{-1}$ is characteristic of the O-H stretch in carboxylic acids, broadened by hydrogen bonding that creates a range of O-H bond strengths.
