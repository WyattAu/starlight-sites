## Intuition

**Spectroscopy is molecular fingerprinting:** Different bonds absorb different frequencies of light. IR spectroscopy identifies functional groups, NMR reveals the carbon-hydrogen skeleton, and mass spectrometry gives molecular weight. Together, they let you determine an unknown molecule's structure without destroying it.

**Why it matters:** Spectroscopy is used in drug development, forensics, environmental monitoring, and quality control. It is the primary tool for confirming that a synthesis produced the intended molecule.

**The key insight:** In ¹H NMR, the integration tells you *how many* protons, the splitting tells you *what neighbours* they have, and the chemical shift tells you *what environment* they are in.

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing NMR chemical shifts with coupling constants:** Chemical shift ($\delta$, in ppm) indicates the electronic environment of a proton. Coupling constant ($J$, in Hz) indicates the interaction between nearby non-equivalent protons. They are different quantities with different units — a peak at $\delta = 2.5$ ppm with $J = 7$ Hz means the chemical shift is 2.5 ppm and the splitting pattern has 7 Hz spacing.

**Misidentifying IR absorption bands:** The O-H stretch (broad, 2500-3300 cm$^{-1}$) and N-H stretch (sharp, 3300-3500 cm$^{-1}$) can overlap. The C=O stretch (strong, 1700 cm$^{-1}$) is one of the most reliable bands. Do not confuse C-H stretches (just below 3000 cm$^{-1}$) with O-H stretches — the shape (broad vs sharp) is the key distinguishing feature.

**Forgetting protecting group compatibility in multi-step synthesis:** Each protecting group has specific conditions for installation and removal. A THP ether is stable to base but labile to acid — if your next step requires acid, the protecting group will be removed prematurely. Always check that protecting group conditions are compatible with all reaction steps.
