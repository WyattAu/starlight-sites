## What This Site Covers

Comprehensive undergraduate chemistry across three major branches: physical chemistry (thermodynamics, kinetics, quantum mechanics, spectroscopy), organic chemistry (reaction mechanisms, synthesis, stereochemistry), and inorganic chemistry (coordination compounds, organometallics, solid-state structures). Each topic includes clear explanations, worked examples, and common exam pitfalls.

## Exam Strategies

| Question Type | Approach | Marks Allocation |
| --------------- | ---------- | ----------------- |
| Mechanism | Draw arrow pushing from nucleophile to electrophile | 1 per correct arrow, 1 for product |

## Intuition

**Chemistry is the central science:** It connects physics (energy and forces), biology (molecular mechanisms of life), geology (mineral formation), and engineering (materials design). Every material, drug, food, and fuel exists because of chemistry.

**Why it matters:** Chemical literacy lets you understand medicine labels, evaluate environmental claims, and appreciate the molecular basis of everyday phenomena from cooking to corrosion.

**The key insight:** Chemistry is fundamentally about electrons — how they are shared, transferred, or redistributed during chemical reactions determines everything about what substances form and how they behave.

## Common Mistakes

**Skipping foundational topics before advancing:** Chemistry is cumulative — you cannot understand reaction kinetics without thermodynamics, or organic mechanisms without understanding orbital theory. Skipping general chemistry to jump into specialised topics creates gaps that compound.

**Confusing thermodynamic stability with kinetic stability:** A reaction may be thermodynamically favourable ($\Delta G < 0$) but kinetically inert (high activation energy). Diamonds converting to graphite is thermodynamically favourable but kinetically negligible at room temperature. Always consider both thermodynamics AND kinetics.

**Ignoring significant figures and rounding errors:** Chemistry calculations require proper significant figure rules. Reporting an answer with 6 significant figures when your input data has 2 gives a false sense of precision. Track significant figures through multi-step calculations.
