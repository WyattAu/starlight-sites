## 学习方法

化学是一门累积性学科——每个概念都建立在之前的知识之上。建议先学习物理化学（热力学、动力学、量子力学），然后学习有机化学（反应机理、合成），最后学习无机化学（配位化合物、固态化学）。

**解题方法：** 化学问题既需要概念理解，也需要计算能力。对于反应机理问题，始终画出电子推动箭头。对于计算问题，跟踪每一步的单位，并验证答案在物理上是否合理。

**实验联系：** 将理论概念与你做过的实际实验联系起来。理解为什么某个反应会产生特定的颜色、沉淀或气体，可以加强你的概念理解。

## Intuition

**Chemistry transcends language barriers:** The fundamental laws of chemistry — conservation of mass, periodic trends, reaction mechanisms — are universal regardless of the language used to describe them. IUPAC nomenclature provides a shared chemical vocabulary across all languages.

**Why it matters:** Scientific communication requires precise, unambiguous terminology. Understanding how chemical concepts translate between languages helps international collaboration and ensures accurate exchange of scientific knowledge.

**The key insight:** Chemical equations must balance in any language — the atoms do not change their behaviour based on which script you write them in.

## Common Mistakes

**Assuming chemical nomenclature translates directly between languages:** While IUPAC names are universal, common names and colloquial terms differ between English and Chinese. For example, "苯" (benzene) is straightforward, but common reagent names may not have direct translations. Always verify IUPAC names when working across languages.

**Confusing Chinese and international convention for writing chemical formulas:** Chinese chemical writing conventions sometimes differ in ordering (e.g., salt formulas). The chemical meaning is identical, but the notation may look unfamiliar. Focus on the underlying chemistry rather than surface-level formatting differences.

**Neglecting that chemical equations must balance in any language:** The law of conservation of mass is universal. Whether you write the equation in English or Chinese, the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides. Language does not change the stoichiometry.
