## The Constitution

**US Constitution**: The supreme law of the United States, establishing the framework of government and protecting the rights of citizens. It was written in 1787 and ratified in 1788.

**Preamble**: The opening statement of the Constitution, beginning with "We the People," explaining the purpose of the document.

**We the People**: The first three words of the Constitution, establishing the concept of popular sovereignty — that government derives its power from the citizens.

**Article I**: Establishes the Legislative Branch (Congress), defining its powers, structure, and the process for making laws.

**Article II**: Establishes the Executive Branch, defining the powers and responsibilities of the President.

**Article III**: Establishes the Judicial Branch, defining the role of the Supreme Court and the federal court system.

**Supremacy Clause**: Article VI, Clause 2, establishing that the Constitution and federal laws are the supreme law of the land, taking precedence over state laws.

**Separation of Powers**: The division of government responsibilities among the three branches (legislative, executive, judicial) to prevent concentration of power.

**Checks and Balances**: The system allowing each branch of government to limit the powers of the other branches, preventing any one branch from becoming too powerful.

**Federalism**: The division of power between the national (federal) government and the state governments.

**Amendment**: A formal change or addition to the Constitution. There have been 27 amendments since ratification.

**Bill of Rights**: The first 10 amendments to the Constitution, ratified in 1791, guaranteeing individual rights and liberties.

## The Bill of Rights (Amendments 1–10)

**First Amendment**: Protects the freedoms of religion, speech, press, assembly, and petition. Guarantees both freedom of religion (no establishment clause, free exercise clause) and freedom of expression.

**Second Amendment**: Protects the right to keep and bear arms.

**Third Amendment**: Prohibits the forced quartering of soldiers in private homes during peacetime.

**Fourth Amendment**: Protects against unreasonable searches and seizures, requiring warrants based on probable cause.

**Fifth Amendment**: Protects against double jeopardy, self-incrimination, and deprivation of life, liberty, or property without due process. Includes the takings clause.

**Sixth Amendment**: Guarantees the rights of criminal defendants, including the right to a speedy and public trial, an impartial jury, to be informed of charges, to confront witnesses, and to have counsel.

**Seventh Amendment**: Guarantees the right to a jury trial in civil cases exceeding $20.

**Eighth Amendment**: Prohibits excessive bail, excessive fines, and cruel and unusual punishment.

**Ninth Amendment**: States that the enumeration of certain rights in the Constitution does not mean other rights are not retained by the people.

**Tenth Amendment**: Reserves powers not delegated to the federal government to the states or the people.

## The Legislative Branch

**Congress**: The national legislature, consisting of the Senate and the House of Representatives. Congress makes federal laws.

**Senate**: The upper chamber of Congress, with 100 senators (two per state). Senators serve six-year terms.

**House of Representatives**: The lower chamber of Congress, with 435 members apportioned by state population. Representatives serve two-year terms.

**Speaker of the House**: The presiding officer of the House of Representatives, elected by its members. Second in the presidential line of succession.

**President of the Senate**: The Vice President of the United States, who presides over the Senate and casts tie-breaking votes.

**Majority Leader**: The leader of the majority party in either chamber of Congress, responsible for scheduling legislation.

**Minority Leader**: The leader of the minority party in either chamber, responsible for representing their party's interests.

**Committee**: A subgroup of members of Congress that specialises in specific policy areas, reviewing and amending legislation.

**Veto**: The President's power to reject a bill passed by Congress. Congress can override a veto with a two-thirds vote in both chambers.

**Override**: The process by which Congress can pass a law despite a presidential veto, requiring a two-thirds vote in both the Senate and House.

**Quorum**: The minimum number of members (a majority) required to be present for a legislative body to conduct business.

**Filibuster**: A Senate procedure allowing extended debate to delay or block a vote on legislation.

**Cloture**: A Senate procedure to end a filibuster, requiring 60 votes.

## The Executive Branch

**President**: The head of state and head of government, elected to a four-year term. The President enforces federal laws and commands the armed forces.

**Vice President**: The second-highest elected official, who presides over the Senate and is first in the line of presidential succession.

**Executive Order**: A directive issued by the President that has the force of law, used to manage the operations of the federal government.

**Cabinet**: The group of senior advisors to the President, consisting of the heads of the 15 executive departments.

**Department of State**: The executive department responsible for foreign affairs and diplomacy.

**Department of the Treasury**: The executive department responsible for managing government revenue, printing currency, and collecting taxes.

**Department of Defence**: The executive department responsible for the nation's military and defence policies.

**Attorney General**: The head of the Department of Justice and the chief law enforcement officer of the federal government.

**Executive Privilege**: The President's right to withhold information from Congress and the courts, subject to judicial review.

**Impeachment**: The process by which the House of Representatives formally accuses a federal official of misconduct. It is not removal from office.

**Conviction and Removal**: Conviction by two-thirds of the Senate following impeachment by the House results in removal from office.

**Line of Succession**: The order in which officials assume the presidency if the President is unable to serve, starting with the Vice President.

## The Judicial Branch

**Supreme Court**: The highest court in the United States, consisting of nine justices appointed for life. It interprets the Constitution and federal law.

**Judicial Review**: The power of courts to examine laws and government actions and declare them unconstitutional, established in Marbury v. Madison (1803).

**Chief Justice**: The presiding justice of the Supreme Court, who administers the presidential oath of office.

**Associate Justice**: The eight other members of the Supreme Court who hear and decide cases.

**Life Tenure**: Supreme Court justices serve "during good behaviour," effectively for life, unless they resign, retire, or are impeached and removed.

**Appellate Court**: A court that hears appeals from lower courts, reviewing whether the law was applied correctly.

**District Court**: The trial courts of the federal court system, where cases are first heard and decided.

**Class Action Lawsuit**: A lawsuit filed by a group of people who have suffered similar harm from the same cause.

**Habeas Corpus**: A legal protection requiring that a person detained by the government be brought before a court to determine if the detention is lawful.

## Rights and Liberties

**Civil Rights**: The rights of individuals to receive equal treatment and protection under the law, regardless of race, gender, religion, or other characteristics.

**Civil Liberties**: The fundamental individual freedoms protected by the Bill of Rights, such as freedom of speech, religion, and due process.

**Due Process**: The constitutional guarantee that the government must respect all legal rights owed to a person, ensuring fair treatment through the judicial system.

**Equal Protection**: The Fourteenth Amendment's guarantee that no state shall deny any person within its jurisdiction the equal protection of the laws.

**Voting Rights**: The right of citizens to vote in elections, protected by the Constitution and expanded through amendments and legislation.

**Freedom of Religion**: The First Amendment's guarantee that Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion or prohibiting the free exercise thereof.

**Freedom of Speech**: The First Amendment's protection of the right to express opinions without government censorship or punishment.

**Right to Assemble**: The First Amendment's protection of the right of people to gather peacefully for protests, rallies, and other purposes.

**Right to Petition**: The First Amendment's protection of the right to appeal to the government for redress of grievances.

## American History

**Declaration of Independence**: The 1776 document declaring the 13 colonies independent from Britain, written primarily by Thomas Jefferson.

**Founding Fathers**: The group of leaders who signed the Declaration of Independence and/or participated in creating the Constitution.

**George Washington**: The first President of the United States (1789–1797), often called the "Father of His Country."

**Thomas Jefferson**: The principal author of the Declaration of Independence and the third President (1801–1809).

**Benjamin Franklin**: A Founding Father, diplomat, and signer of both the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution.

**Abraham Lincoln**: The 16th President (1861–1865), who led the nation through the Civil War and issued the Emancipation Proclamation.

**Emancipation Proclamation**: President Lincoln's 1863 executive order freeing enslaved people in Confederate states.

**Civil War**: The war (1861–1865) between the Union (Northern states) and Confederacy (Southern states) over issues including slavery and states' rights.

**Thirteenth Amendment**: Abolished slavery and involuntary servitude, ratified in 1865.

**Fourteenth Amendment**: Granted citizenship to all persons born or naturalised in the United States, including former slaves, and guaranteed equal protection and due process.

**Fifteenth Amendment**: Prohibited denying the right to vote based on race, colour, or previous condition of servitude.

**Nineteenth Amendment**: Prohibited denying the right to vote based on sex, granting women the right to vote in 1920.

## Symbols and Holidays

**Stars and Stripes (US Flag)**: The national flag with 50 stars representing the 50 states and 13 stripes representing the original 13 colonies.

**The Star-Spangled Banner**: The national anthem of the United States, written by Francis Scott Key during the War of 1812.

**Liberty Bell**: A symbol of American independence located in Philadelphia, cracked in the 18th century.

**Statue of Liberty**: A gift from France in 1886, located in New York Harbour, symbolising freedom and democracy.

**Independence Day (Fourth of July)**: The national holiday celebrating the adoption of the Declaration of Independence on July 4, 1776.

**Thanksgiving Day**: A national holiday on the fourth Thursday of November, celebrating the 1621 harvest feast shared by Pilgrims and Native Americans.

**Memorial Day**: A federal holiday on the last Monday of May, honouring military personnel who have died in service.

**Veterans Day**: A federal holiday on November 11, honouring all military veterans who have served in the United States Armed Forces.
