## Intuition

**The language of computers:** Discrete mathematics is the native language of computers — logic is how circuits work, sets are how databases organise data, and graph theory is how networks connect. Without it, you're reading a foreign language without knowing the alphabet.

**Why it matters:** Discrete math underpins every area of computer science — algorithms use graph theory, databases use relations, compilers use formal logic, and cryptography uses number theory. It's the foundation that makes everything else possible.

**The key insight:** Proof by induction is like a domino chain — if the first domino falls (base case) and each domino knocks over the next (inductive step), then all dominoes fall. This simple idea powers some of the deepest results in mathematics.

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing Euler circuits with Hamiltonian circuits:** Euler circuits visit every EDGE exactly once. Hamiltonian circuits visit every VERTEX exactly once. They're different graph problems.

**Forgetting the handshaking lemma:** The sum of all vertex degrees equals twice the number of edges. This is useful for counting and verification.

**Mixing up planar with planar-embedded:** A planar graph can be drawn without edge crossings. A planar embedding is a specific drawing. Don't assume all drawings of a planar graph are planar embeddings.
