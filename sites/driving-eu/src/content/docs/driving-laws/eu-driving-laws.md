## Overview

EU driving laws are harmonised through the Vienna Convention and EU directives,
but individual countries retain some variation. This guide covers the key
rules you need to know when driving in the European Union.

## Speed Limits

Standard EU speed limits (unless otherwise signed):

| Road Type | Built-Up Areas | Outside Built-Up | Motorways |
|-----------|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| Cars | 50 km/h | 90 km/h | 120-130 km/h |
| Trucks (>3.5t) | 50 km/h | 80 km/h | 80-90 km/h |

### Country Variations

- **Germany**: No general speed limit on some autobahn sections
- **France**: 110 km/h on motorways in rain
- **Spain**: 90 km/h on single carriageways (increased to 100 km/h in 2021)
- **Italy**: 130 km/h on autostrade

## Alcohol Limits

EU countries have strict alcohol limits for drivers:

- **Most EU countries**: 0.05% BAC (50mg/100ml)
- **Germany**: 0.05% BAC (0.00% for new drivers)
- **France**: 0.05% BAC (0.02% for new drivers)
- **Sweden**: 0.02% BAC
- **Czech Republic**: 0.00% BAC (zero tolerance)

### Penalties

- Fines vary by country (50-5000 EUR)
- License suspension (30 days to 6 months)
- Vehicle confiscation in some countries
- Criminal charges for high BAC levels

## Required Documents

When driving in the EU, you must carry:

- Valid driving licence (EU format or International Driving Permit)
- Vehicle registration document (V5C or equivalent)
- Proof of insurance (Green Card or EU insurance card)
- Passport or national ID card
- European Health Insurance Card (EHIC)

## Child Safety

- Children under 12 years or shorter than 150cm must use appropriate child
  restraints
- Rear-facing child seats for children under 15 months
- Forward-facing child seats for children 15 months to 4 years
- Booster seats for children 4-12 years

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Assuming speed limits are the same everywhere**: EU speed limits vary
   by country and road type. Research the specific limits for each country
   you plan to visit.

2. **Not carrying required documents**: Some countries require you to carry
   specific documents. Failing to produce them can result in fines.

3. **Ignoring country-specific rules**: Some countries have unique rules
   (e.g., France requires warning triangles and reflective vests in cars).

## Summary

EU driving laws are largely harmonised through the Vienna Convention, with
standard speed limits (50/90/120-130 km/h), strict alcohol limits (0.05%
BAC in most countries), and required documents (licence, registration,
insurance, ID). Child safety rules require appropriate restraints for
children under 12 or shorter than 150cm. Individual countries may have
additional rules, so research before driving abroad.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Speed Limit Conversion

You are driving in France on a motorway in heavy rain.

**Rule**: France reduces motorway speed limits to 110 km/h in rain.

**Conversion**: 110 km/h = approximately 68 mph.

**Action**: Reduce speed to 110 km/h or below.

### Example 2: Document Check

You are driving from the UK to France via the Channel Tunnel.

**Required documents**:
- Valid UK driving licence (EU format)
- Vehicle registration (V5C)
- Green Card or EU insurance card
- Passport
- European Health Insurance Card (EHIC)
- Warning triangle and reflective vests (required in France)
