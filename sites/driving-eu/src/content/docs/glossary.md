## EU Driving Regulations

**EU Driving Licence Directive**: The European Union legislation standardising driving licence formats, categories, and minimum ages across member states.

**European Driving Licence**: A credit-card format licence valid across all EU/EEA countries, replacing the older paper and booklet-style licences.

**B1 Driving Category**: The licence category for light quadricycles and small vehicles up to 400 kg unladen weight.

**B Driving Category**: The standard car licence allowing drivers to operate vehicles up to 3,500 kg with up to 8 passenger seats.

**BE Driving Category**: Required for driving a car with a trailer exceeding 750 kg, for articulated vehicle combinations.

**C1 Driving Category**: Allows driving medium-sized trucks between 3,500 kg and 7,500 kg.

**C Driving Category**: Required for driving large goods vehicles over 3,500 kg.

**D1 Driving Category**: Allows driving minibuses with up to 16 passenger seats.

**D Driving Category**: Required for driving buses with more than 8 passenger seats.

**AM Driving Category**: The moped licence for light two-wheeled vehicles up to 45 km/h.

**A1 Driving Category**: For small motorcycles up to 125 cc and 11 kW.

**A2 Driving Category**: For medium motorcycles up to 35 kW.

**A Driving Category**: For all motorcycles without power restrictions.

## EU Road Signs

**Vienna Convention on Road Signs and Signals**: The international treaty establishing standard road sign shapes, colours, and meanings adopted by most European countries.

**Mandatory Sign**: A circular sign with a blue background indicating an obligation, such as a mandatory direction of travel or cycle path.

**Prohibitory Sign**: A circular sign with a red border indicating a restriction, such as speed limits, no overtaking, or weight limits.

**Danger Warning Sign**: A triangular sign with a red border alerting drivers to hazards ahead, such as bends, slippery roads, or roadworks.

**Information Sign**: Signs providing guidance about directions, services, and facilities, typically rectangular and blue or green.

**Priority Road Sign**: A yellow diamond-shaped sign indicating the driver has right-of-way at intersections along that road.

**No Overtaking Sign**: A circular sign with a red border showing two cars side by side, prohibiting overtaking.

**End of Restriction Sign**: A white circular sign with diagonal black lines indicating the end of a previously imposed restriction.

**Speed Limit Sign**: A circular sign with a red border and a number indicating the maximum speed in km/h.

**Motorway Sign**: A blue rectangular sign indicating the start of a motorway, with specific motorway rules applying.

**Tunnel Sign**: A sign indicating an approaching tunnel with specific requirements for lights, spacing, and overtaking.

## European Driving Rules

**Kilometre Per Hour (km/h)**: The standard speed unit used across Europe. Speed limits are posted in km/h, not mph.

**Right-Hand Traffic**: The standard in all EU countries except the UK, Ireland, Cyprus, and Malta, where vehicles drive on the left.

**Drunk Driving Limit**: The maximum blood alcohol concentration (BAC) for drivers, typically 0.05% (0.02% for new drivers in many countries).

**Winter Tyres (Winter Tires)**: Tyres designed for cold and icy conditions, required by law in several EU countries during winter months.

**Snow Chains**: Metal chains fitted to tyres for traction on snow and ice, required or recommended in mountainous areas during winter.

**High-Visibility Vest (Reflective Vest)**: A safety garment that must be carried in the vehicle and worn when stopped on the roadside in many EU countries.

**Warning Triangle**: A reflective triangle placed behind a broken-down vehicle to warn approaching traffic, mandatory in most EU countries.

**Headlight Usage**: Many EU countries require headlights at all times, day and night, even in good weather conditions.

**Emergency Corridor (Rettungsgasse)**: In countries like Germany and Austria, drivers must form an emergency corridor by pulling to the edges of the road when traffic is stationary.

**Minimum Age to Drive**: Varies by country, typically 18 for a full car licence, with some countries offering supervised driving from 17.

## Vehicle Categories and Inspection

**MOT (Contrôle Technique)**: The mandatory periodic vehicle safety inspection required in EU countries, equivalent to the UK MOT test.

**TPV (Technischer Überprüfung)**: The German term for the technical vehicle inspection, required every two years.

**Contrôle Technique**: The French vehicle inspection, required every two years for vehicles under four years old and annually thereafter.

**TÜV (Technischer Überwachungsverein)**: German technical inspection associations authorised to carry out vehicle safety inspections.

**Euro Emission Standards**: EU regulations limiting exhaust emissions from vehicles, with progressively stricter standards (Euro 1 through Euro 7).

**ULEZ (Ultra Low Emission Zone)**: Designated zones in European cities where only vehicles meeting specific emission standards may enter.

**Environmental Zone (Umweltzone)**: A zone in German cities where vehicles need a green emission sticker to enter.

**Low Emission Zone (LEZ)**: An area where access of some polluting vehicles is restricted or deterred with a view to improving air quality.

## Cross-Border Driving

**Green Card (Insurance)**: An international certificate of motor vehicle insurance, required when driving in some non-EU countries.

**European Health Insurance Card (EHIC)**: A card providing access to state-provided healthcare during temporary stays in EU/EEA countries.

**International Driving Permit (IDP)**: A document translating a driving licence, useful when driving outside the EU.

**European Breakdown Cover**: Insurance providing roadside assistance and recovery when driving in European countries.

**Carte Verte (Green Card)**: The French term for the international motor insurance certificate, required in some non-EU countries.

**Border Control**: While the Schengen Agreement allows passport-free travel within the Schengen Area, some EU countries maintain border checks.

**Schengen Area**: A zone of 27 European countries that have abolished passport controls at their mutual borders.

## Drink-Driving and Safety

**Breathalyser**: A device used to measure blood alcohol content (BAC) from a breath sample. Some countries require drivers to carry one.

**Alcohol Interlock Device**: An ignition interlock connected to a vehicle's ignition that prevents the engine from starting if the driver's BAC exceeds a set limit.

**Zero Tolerance**: Some EU countries have a zero BAC limit for new or young drivers, meaning any detectable alcohol is illegal.

**Mobile Phone Usage**: Using a handheld mobile phone while driving is illegal across the EU. Hands-free use is permitted but may still attract penalties if it causes distraction.

**Seat Belt Law**: Wearing a seat belt is compulsory for drivers and all passengers in all EU countries, with limited exceptions.

**Child Restraint**: Children under a certain age or height must use appropriate child safety seats, booster seats, or booster cushions.
