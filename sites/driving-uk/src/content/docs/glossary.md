## Highway Code and Law

**Highway Code**: The official rules and guidelines for all road users in the UK, covering pedestrians, cyclists, motorcyclists, and drivers. It is not law itself but many rules are backed by legislation.

**Road Traffic Act 1988**: The primary legislation governing road use in England, Wales, and Scotland, covering offences, penalties, and vehicle requirements.

**DVSA (Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency)**: The government agency responsible for maintaining driving standards, conducting driving tests, and monitoring vehicle safety.

**DVLA (Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency)**: The government body that manages driving licences and vehicle registration records in the UK.

**Contravention**: An offence of breaching traffic laws, such as parking illegally or running a red light, resulting in fines or penalty points.

**Penalty Points**: Endorsements added to a driving licence for motoring offences. Accumulating 12 or more points within three years leads to a disqualification.

**Fixed Penalty Notice (FPN)**: A fine issued by police or civil enforcement officers for minor motoring offences without going to court.

**Conditional Offer**: A fixed penalty offer requiring payment of a fine and acceptance of penalty points within a set period.

** totting-Up**: The process of disqualifying a driver who has accumulated 12 or more penalty points within a three-year period.

**Driving Ban (Disqualification)**: A court-ordered prohibition from driving for a specified period, imposed for serious offences or totting-up.

## Road Signs and Markings

**Regulatory Signs**: Signs that impose a legal obligation, such as speed limits, no-entry signs, and one-way signs. Red circles indicate prohibitions.

**Warning Signs**: Signs alerting drivers to hazards ahead, typically triangular with a red border. Examples include bends, roadworks, and animal crossings.

**Information Signs**: Signs providing guidance about routes, services, or facilities. Rectangular in shape and typically blue or green on motorways.

 **Speed Limit Sign**: A circular sign with a red border indicating the maximum permitted speed in miles per hour. National speed limit signs show a white circle with a black diagonal stripe.

**Motorway Sign**: Blue rectangular signs on motorways indicating junction numbers, destinations, and services. Only displayed on motorways.

**Direction Sign**: Signs showing destinations and distances, colour-coded: green for primary routes, blue for motorways, white for local routes.

 **Road Markings**: White and yellow lines painted on the road surface indicating lanes, stopping points, parking restrictions, and directional guidance.

**Double Yellow Lines**: Continuous yellow lines along the kerb indicating no waiting or parking at any time unless signs indicate otherwise.

**Double White Lines**: Two continuous white lines down the centre of the road where overtaking is prohibited for the driver on the side of the solid line.

**Box Junction**: A criss-cross yellow marking at junctions where you must not enter unless your exit is clear, except when turning right.

** zig-Zag Lines**: White zig-zag lines near pedestrian crossings indicating no overtaking or parking. Approaching zig-zags also prohibit U-turns.

**Hatched Road Markings**: Diagonal white lines on the road surface that separate traffic streams or protect turning traffic. Solid borders should not be crossed.

## Road Layouts and Junctions

**Roundabout**: A circular intersection where traffic flows clockwise around a central island. Give way to traffic already on the roundabout from the right.

**Mini-Roundabout**: A small roundabout marked by a painted circle and often a raised dome. Follow the same rules as standard roundabouts.

 **Dual Carriageway**: A road with a central reservation separating traffic flowing in opposite directions, typically with a higher speed limit than single carriageways.

**One-Way System**: A network of streets where traffic is only permitted to travel in one direction, indicated by blue rectangular signs with a white arrow.

 **T-Junction**: A junction where a minor road meets a major road, requiring traffic on the minor road to give way.

**Crossroads**: An intersection where two roads meet at right angles, controlled by traffic lights, give-way markings, or unmarked.

 **Slip Road**: A road allowing drivers to join or leave a motorway or dual carriageway, including acceleration and deceleration lanes.

**Box Junction**: A yellow criss-cross marking at junctions designed to prevent blocking. You must not enter unless your exit is clear.

## Speed and Distance

**Stopping Distance**: The total distance needed to bring a vehicle to a halt, consisting of thinking distance plus braking distance.

**Thinking Distance**: The distance a vehicle travels during the driver's reaction time. At 30 mph this is approximately 12 metres.

**Braking Distance**: The distance a vehicle travels after the brakes are applied until it comes to a complete stop. It increases with speed and poor road conditions.

**Two-Second Rule**: A safe following distance rule — at least two seconds behind the vehicle in front in normal conditions, doubled in wet weather and increased further in ice or fog.

**Safe Following Distance**: The recommended gap between your vehicle and the one ahead, allowing adequate time to stop safely.

**Speed Limit**: The maximum legal speed on a road, set by local authorities and indicated by signs. National speed limits vary by road type and vehicle.

## Vehicle Safety and Systems

**MOT Test**: The annual Ministry of Transport test checking vehicle safety, roadworthiness, and exhaust emissions. Required for vehicles three years and older.

**Vehicle Excise Duty (VED)**: The tax required to legally use a vehicle on public roads, commonly known as road tax.

**M1 Test**: The category of driving licence required to drive a car or light van.

**ABS (Anti-lock Braking System)**: A safety system that prevents wheels from locking during heavy braking, allowing the driver to maintain steering control.

**Electronic Stability Control (ESC)**: A system that helps prevent skidding by automatically applying brakes to individual wheels when it detects loss of traction.

**Seat Belt**: A restraint system worn by vehicle occupants. Front and rear seat belts must be worn where available. Failure to wear one is an offence.

**Head Restraint**: A device designed to support the head and reduce the risk of whiplash injury in a rear-end collision.

**Daytime Running Lights**: Lights that automatically illuminate when the engine is started, improving visibility to other road users during daylight.

**Hazard Lights**: Flashing indicators used simultaneously to warn other drivers of a hazard or a broken-down vehicle. Do not use them to indicate a lane change.

## Driving Conditions

**Adverse Weather Conditions**: Driving in rain, snow, ice, fog, or high winds, requiring reduced speed and increased caution.

**Aquaplaning**: A phenomenon where a layer of water builds between the tyres and the road surface, causing loss of traction and steering control.

**Black Ice**: A thin, transparent layer of ice on the road surface that is difficult to see, creating extremely hazardous driving conditions.

**Fog**: Dense water vapour reducing visibility below 100 metres. Use fog lights when visibility is seriously reduced but remember to switch them off when conditions improve.

**Reduced Visibility**: Conditions such as rain, fog, darkness, or sun glare that limit how far ahead you can see, requiring reduced speed.

**Windscreen Glare**: Bright sunlight reflecting off the windscreen or wet road surface, temporarily reducing visibility. Use sun visors and reduce speed.

## Vulnerable Road Users

**Pedestrian**: A person travelling on foot, given priority at crossings and in many road situations. Drivers must take special care near schools and residential areas.

**Cyclist**: A person riding a bicycle. Cyclists may use cycle lanes but are not required to. Drivers must leave at least 1.5 metres when overtaking at speeds up to 30 mph.

**Motorcyclist**: A person riding a motorcycle. Motorcycles are vulnerable due to their smaller size and lack of protective bodywork compared to cars.

**Horse Rider**: A person riding a horse on the road. Slow down to walking pace when passing, do not sound the horn, and give plenty of room.

**Learner Driver**: A person learning to drive under supervision. L-plates must be displayed on the front and rear of the vehicle.

**Blue Badge Holder**: A person with a disability parking permit allowing them to park in designated bays and certain restricted areas.

## Theory Test Components

**Multiple-Choice Questions**: The first part of the theory test, consisting of 50 questions on the Highway Code, road signs, and driving safety. Pass mark is 43 out of 50.

**Hazard Perception Test**: The second part of the theory test, assessing the ability to identify developing hazards on screen. Pass mark is 44 out of 75.

**Developing Hazard**: A hazard that requires you to take action, such as changing speed, direction, or stopping. You score higher for spotting it earlier.

**Potential Hazard**: Something that could become a developing hazard if you do not react appropriately, such as a pedestrian standing near the kerb.

**Video Clips**: The hazard perception test uses 14 video clips, each approximately 60 seconds long, filmed from a driver's perspective.
