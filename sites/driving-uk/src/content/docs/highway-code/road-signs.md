## Overview

UK road signs are classified by shape and colour, making them instantly
recognisable. The Highway Code defines three main categories: regulatory signs
(red circles), warning signs (triangles), and information signs (rectangles).
Understanding these signs is essential for passing the UK driving theory test.

## Regulatory Signs (Red Circles)

Red circular signs indicate instructions that must be obeyed. They are legally
enforceable and disobeying them is a motoring offence.

### Prohibition Signs

- **No Entry**: Red circle with white horizontal bar -- vehicles prohibited
- **No Right Turn**: Red circle with black right arrow crossed out in red
- **No Left Turn**: Red circle with black left arrow crossed out in red
- **No U-Turn**: Red circle with black U-turn arrow crossed out in red
- **No Overtaking**: Red circle with two car silhouettes (red and black)
- **Speed Limit**: Red circle with black number -- maximum speed in mph

### Mandatory Signs

Blue circular signs indicate actions that must be taken.

- **Turn Left**: Blue circle with white arrow pointing left
- **Turn Right**: Blue circle with white arrow pointing right
- **Ahead Only**: Blue circle with white arrow pointing up
- **Roundabout**: Blue circle with three white arrows in circular pattern
- **Cycle Route**: Blue circle with white bicycle symbol

## Warning Signs (Triangles)

Yellow or white triangular signs with red borders warn of hazards ahead.

- **Curve Ahead**: Triangle with curved arrow indicating direction
- **Road Narrowing**: Triangle with narrowing road symbol
- **Traffic Signals**: Triangle with traffic light symbol
- **Pedestrian Crossing**: Triangle with walking figure
- **School Crossing**: Triangle with two walking figures (children)
- **Road Works**: Triangle with person digging symbol
- **Slippery Road**: Triangle with car and wavy skid marks
- **Double Bend**: Triangle with S-shaped curve

## Information Signs (Rectangles)

Rectangular signs provide information about routes, services, and directions.

### Direction Signs

- **Green background**: Primary routes (motorways and A-roads)
- **White background**: Non-primary routes (B-roads and local roads)
- **Blue background**: Motorway-specific information
- **Brown background**: Tourist attractions and points of interest

### Motorway Signs

- **Blue background with white text**: Motorway junction numbers
- **Motorway symbol**: M-prefix number (e.g., M25, M1)
- **Junction number**: White number on blue background

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Confusing regulatory and warning signs**: Red circles are mandatory
   (must obey). Triangles are warnings (be prepared). Rectangles are
   information (helpful to know).

2. **Misreading speed limit signs**: UK speed limits are in mph, not km/h.
   A red circle with "30" means 30 mph maximum.

3. **Ignoring motorway signs**: Blue signs on motorways have specific
   meanings. Junction numbers, lane designations, and exit information are
   critical for safe motorway driving.

## Summary

UK road signs follow a consistent colour and shape system: red circles for
regulatory signs (must obey), triangles for warnings (be prepared), and
rectangles for information. The colour indicates the type of road: green for
primary routes, white for non-primary, blue for motorways, and brown for
tourist attractions. Understanding this system is essential for the theory
test and safe driving.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Sign Identification

You see a red circle with "40" in black text.

**Identification**: This is a mandatory maximum speed limit sign. The red
circle indicates it is a regulatory sign that must be obeyed. The number 40
means 40 mph maximum.

**Action**: Reduce speed to 40 mph or below.

### Example 2: Motorway Navigation

You are on the M25 approaching junction 15. You see a blue sign with "M4
West 1/2 mile".

**Identification**: This is a motorway direction sign. The blue background
indicates motorway information. The "1/2 mile" tells you the distance to
the junction.

**Action**: Prepare to change lanes to the left if you need to exit for the M4.
