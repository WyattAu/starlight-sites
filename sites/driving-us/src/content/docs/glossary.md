## Licensing and Registration

**DMV (Department of Motor Vehicles)**: The state agency responsible for issuing driver's licences, vehicle registrations, and identification cards.

**Driver's Licence**: An official document authorising a person to operate a motor vehicle on public roads, issued by the state DMV.

**Learner's Permit (Instruction Permit)**: A restricted licence allowing a new driver to practise driving under the supervision of a licensed adult.

**Provisional Licence**: A licence issued to drivers under 18 with restrictions such as nighttime driving limits and passenger limitations.

**Full (Unrestricted) Licence**: A standard driver's licence with no restrictions, typically issued after holding a provisional licence for a specified period.

**CDL (Commercial Driver's Licence)**: A licence required to operate commercial vehicles such as trucks, buses, and tankers.

**International Driving Permit (IDP)**: A document translating a foreign driver's licence, required in some states for visiting drivers.

**Vehicle Registration**: The process of recording a vehicle with the state, requiring proof of ownership, insurance, and payment of fees.

**Title (Vehicle Title)**: A legal document proving ownership of a vehicle, also known as a pink slip in some states.

**Licence Renewal**: The process of extending the validity of a driver's licence, typically required every four to eight years depending on the state.

## Road Signs and Signals

**Regulatory Signs**: Signs that indicate traffic laws and regulations that must be obeyed, such as speed limits, stop signs, and yield signs.

**Warning Signs**: Diamond-shaped signs alerting drivers to potential hazards ahead, such as curves, merges, and school zones.

**Guide Signs**: Signs providing information about directions, distances, and services, typically rectangular and green or blue.

**Speed Limit Sign**: A white rectangular sign indicating the maximum legal speed in miles per hour for that road section.

**Stop Sign**: An octagonal red sign requiring drivers to come to a complete stop and yield to cross traffic and pedestrians before proceeding.

**Yield Sign**: A triangular red and white sign requiring drivers to slow down and give way to traffic and pedestrians in the intersection.

**Merge Sign**: A warning sign indicating that traffic lanes are ending and drivers must merge into the continuing lane.

**Divided Highway Sign**: A sign indicating the road ahead splits into two one-way roadways separated by a median.

**One-Way Sign**: A rectangular sign indicating traffic flows only in the direction shown by the arrow.

**Pedestrian Crossing Sign**: A yellow diamond-shaped sign warning drivers of an upcoming pedestrian crossing area.

## Right-of-Way Rules

**Right-of-Way**: The legal right of a driver or pedestrian to proceed first in a traffic situation, determined by signs, signals, and rules.

**Yielding**: The act of giving the right-of-way to another vehicle or pedestrian when required by law or courtesy.

**Uncontrolled Intersection**: An intersection with no traffic signals or signs, where the first vehicle to arrive and enter has the right-of-way.

**Four-Way Stop (All-Way Stop)**: An intersection where all approaching vehicles must stop. The first vehicle to stop proceeds first; if simultaneous, yield to the right.

**Roundabout**: A circular intersection where traffic flows counterclockwise. Drivers must yield to traffic already in the roundabout.

**Pedestrian Right-of-Way**: Pedestrians have the right-of-way at marked crosswalks and intersections, and drivers must stop completely.

**Emergency Vehicle Right-of-Way**: Drivers must yield to emergency vehicles with activated sirens and lights by pulling to the right and stopping.

## Driving Maneuvres

**Parallel Parking**: Parking a vehicle parallel to the kerb between two vehicles, requiring precise steering and positioning.

**Reverse Parking**: Backing a vehicle into a parking space, often required in driving tests to demonstrate spatial awareness.

**Three-Point Turn (K-Turn)**: A manoeuvre to turn a vehicle around in a narrow road using forward and reverse gears.

**Lane Change**: Moving a vehicle from one lane to another, requiring signalling, mirror checks, and a blind spot check.

**Merging**: Combining two lanes of traffic into one, requiring drivers to adjust speed and yield as appropriate.

**Overtaking (Passing)**: Moving past a slower vehicle in the same direction, permitted only when safe and legal.

**U-Turn**: A 180-degree turn to reverse direction, permitted only where legal and safe, indicated by signs.

**Backing Up**: Reversing a vehicle, requiring the driver to check mirrors and look over the shoulder for obstacles and pedestrians.

## Vehicle Systems and Safety

**Braking System**: The mechanism for slowing or stopping a vehicle, including disc brakes, drum brakes, and anti-lock systems.

**ABS (Anti-lock Braking System)**: A system that prevents wheels from locking during emergency braking, allowing steering control to be maintained.

**Airbags**: Inflatable safety devices that deploy in a collision to cushion occupants from impact with the steering wheel, dashboard, or windows.

**Seat Belt**: A restraint system that must be worn by all front-seat passengers and rear passengers where available. Required by law.

**Headlights**: Front-facing lights required from sunset to sunrise and in poor visibility. Low beams for traffic; high beams for dark roads without oncoming traffic.

**Turn Signals (Indicators)**: Flashing lights indicating the driver's intention to turn or change lanes, required to be activated at least 100 feet before turning.

**Side Mirrors (Wing Mirrors)**: Mirrors mounted on the sides of the vehicle, allowing the driver to see traffic behind and to the sides.

**Rear-View Mirror**: A mirror mounted inside the vehicle on the windscreen, allowing the driver to see traffic directly behind.

**Hood (Bonnet)**: The hinged cover over the engine compartment, which should be opened to check oil, coolant, and other fluids.

**Tyre (Tire)**: Rubber coverings on wheels that provide traction. Tyre pressure and tread depth must meet legal minimums.

## Road Rules and Regulations

**Speed Limit**: The maximum legal speed on a road, varying by road type, area, and conditions. Posted on signs.

**Right on Red**: In most states, drivers may turn right at a red light after a complete stop and yielding to pedestrians and cross traffic.

**Left Turn on Red**: Permitted only when turning from a one-way street onto another one-way street, after a complete stop.

**School Zone**: An area near a school with reduced speed limits during school hours, enforced with signs and sometimes cameras.

**Construction Zone (Work Zone)**: An area of roadwork with reduced speed limits and fines, often doubled for violations.

**HOV Lane (High-Occupancy Vehicle Lane)**: A lane reserved for vehicles with two or more occupants, used to reduce congestion.

**Rumble Strips**: Raised or grooved patterns on road edges or centres that alert drowsy or distracted drivers through vibration and noise.

**Clearway**: A road where stopping or parking is prohibited during specified times to maintain traffic flow.

## Types of Roads and Interstates

**Interstate Highway**: A network of controlled-access highways connecting states, with speed limits typically 55–75 mph depending on the state.

**Freeway (Expressway)**: A high-speed road with controlled access, no traffic signals, and grade-separated interchanges.

**State Highway**: A road maintained by the state government, connecting cities and towns within the state.

**County Road**: A road maintained by the county, often rural or suburban in nature.

**Residential Street**: A road in a residential area with lower speed limits (typically 25 mph) and higher pedestrian activity.

**One-Way Street**: A road where traffic flows in a single direction only, indicated by signs.
