## Overview

Defensive driving is the practice of anticipating dangerous situations and
making safe, well-informed decisions to protect yourself and others. It goes
beyond following traffic laws -- it means being prepared for the mistakes of
other drivers and environmental hazards.

## Space Management

### The 3-Second Rule

Maintain at least a 3-second following distance behind the vehicle in front
of you. This gives you time to react and stop safely.

### How to Measure

1. Pick a fixed object (sign, tree, bridge)
2. When the vehicle ahead passes it, start counting
3. If you reach the object before counting to 3, you are too close

### Increased Following Distance

Increase to 4-6 seconds in these conditions:

- Rain, snow, or ice
- Heavy traffic
- Following a large truck
- Visibility is reduced
- You are tired or distracted

## Hazard Perception

### Common Hazards

- **Intersection**: Most accidents occur here; scan left-right-left before
  entering
- **Pedestrians**: Watch for pedestrians stepping off curbs, especially children
- **Cyclists**: Give at least 3 feet of space when passing
- **Construction zones**: Expect sudden lane shifts and reduced speeds
- **Parked cars**: Watch for doors opening or pedestrians emerging

### Scanning Techniques

- Look 12-15 seconds ahead (about 1 city block or 400 feet)
- Check mirrors every 5-8 seconds
- Scan intersections before entering, even on green
- Check blind spots before changing lanes

## Fatigue Prevention

### Warning Signs

- Difficulty keeping eyes open
- Drifting from your lane
- Missing exits or turns
- Yawning repeatedly
- Difficulty focusing

### Prevention Strategies

- Get 7-8 hours of sleep before driving
- Take a 15-20 minute break every 2 hours on long trips
- Share driving duties on road trips
- Avoid driving during your normal sleep hours
- Pull over and rest if you feel drowsy

## Adverse Weather Driving

### Rain

- Reduce speed by at least 1/3
- Increase following distance to 5-6 seconds
- Use headlights (required by law in most states)
- Avoid cruise control on wet roads
- Watch for hydroplaning at speeds above 35 MPH

### Snow and Ice

- Reduce speed significantly
- Accelerate and decelerate slowly
- Increase following distance to 8-10 seconds
- Use low gears for better traction
- Avoid sudden movements

### Fog

- Use low-beam headlights (high beams reflect back)
- Reduce speed and increase following distance
- Use right edge of road as a guide
- Do not stop on the roadway

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Driving too fast for conditions**: The posted speed limit is the
   maximum for ideal conditions. In rain, snow, fog, or heavy traffic,
   you must drive slower.

2. **Not scanning intersections**: Most accidents happen at intersections.
   Always look left-right-left before entering, even on a green light.

3. **Following too closely**: Tailgating is the leading cause of rear-end
   collisions. Maintain at least a 3-second following distance.

## Summary

Defensive driving means anticipating hazards and making safe decisions.
Key techniques include maintaining a 3-second following distance (increased
in adverse conditions), scanning 12-15 seconds ahead, watching for common
hazards at intersections and crosswalks, and preventing fatigue on long
trips. Adverse weather requires reduced speed, increased following distance,
and extra caution.

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Following Distance Calculation

You are driving at 60 MPH on a highway. The vehicle ahead passes a mile
marker, and you pass it 2 seconds later.

**Analysis**: At 60 MPH, you travel 88 feet per second. 2 seconds = 176
feet. The 3-second rule requires 264 feet. You are 88 feet too close.

**Action**: Increase your following distance to at least 3 seconds (264
feet at 60 MPH).

### Example 2: Fatigue Decision

You have been driving for 3 hours on a highway. You notice your eyes
dropping and you missed your last exit.

**Analysis**: You are showing classic signs of fatigue. Continuing is
dangerous.

**Action**: Pull over at the next rest area or exit. Take a 15-20 minute
nap or switch drivers. Do not continue driving while drowsy.
