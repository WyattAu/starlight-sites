## The Two-Axis System

Pavement markings encode two independent facts:

- **Color separates directions of travel**: yellow = traffic on your
  left has the opposing flow (or the line separates opposing
  directions); white = same-direction traffic only.
- **Pattern governs crossing**: dashed = pass/merge freely when safe;
  solid = crossing is discouraged; double solid = never cross.

Every written test leans on this grid. Learn it once and most marking
questions answer themselves.

## Yellow Lines

| Marking | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Broken yellow | Passing allowed from your side when safe |
| Solid + broken yellow | Pass only from the broken side |
| Double solid yellow | No passing either direction; left turns across are still legal |

The double-solid nuance is the classic trap: you may not *pass* across
double yellows, but you may *turn left* into a driveway or intersection
across them. Only a painted median or a physical barrier changes that.

## White Lines

| Marking | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Broken white | Lane changes allowed |
| Solid white | Lane changes discouraged (approaches, tunnel lanes, fog lines) |
| Double solid white | Do not change lanes (tunnels, bridge approaches) |
| Dotted (short) white | Lane extension through an intersection — follow it |

Solid white fog lines mark the road's right edge; solid yellow marks
the left edge on divided roads. Edge-line color is a quick test
question.

## Reversible and Shared Lanes

- **Reversible lanes**: marked by a double-broken yellow or a lane
  with overhead signals — direction follows the signal, not habit.
- **Center turn lane** (solid-and-dashed yellow both sides): a shared
  left-turn pocket. Enter no more than ~200 feet before your turn,
  wait inside the lines, never use it for passing.
- **Bike lanes**: solid white boundary; cars must yield when merging
  across or turning through; broken sections mark merge zones.

## Crosswalks and Stop Lines

- **Continental (ladder) crosswalks**: wide bars — the most visible
  style, used at uncontrolled crossings.
- **Transverse (two solid lines)**: the classic minimal crosswalk.
- Stop before the **stop line**, not the crosswalk — the line sits
  4+ feet back to keep your bumper out of the pedestrian zone.
- Yield lines (white triangles) at roundabouts and yield points:
  the point of no decision — slow and yield before them.

## Railroad Crossings

The pavement X with "RR" marks the approach. Rules: stop within 15-50
feet when gates or flashers are active; never stop on the tracks; a
full stop is legally required for school buses and hazmat vehicles at
every crossing, marked or not. Tests frequently pair this with the
round yellow advance-warning sign.

## Curb Colors

| Curb | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| White | Short loading, passenger pickup |
| Yellow | Loading zone — freight/passengers, limited time |
| Red | No stopping, standing, or parking |
| Green | Time-limited parking per sign |
| Blue | Disabled-parking permit holders only |

## How Markings Are Tested

1. Color/pattern grid questions (pass or not across this line?).
2. The double-yellow left-turn exception.
3. Center-turn-lane length and use rules.
4. Stop-line vs crosswalk position.
5. Curb color meanings (urban states especially).
