## The Agency: Florida FLHSMV

Florida licensing runs through the Florida Highway Safety and Motor
Vehicle agency (FLHSMV), with county tax-collector offices handling
most in-person service. Florida's testing ecosystem is unusually
digital: the knowledge exam can be taken online for teens, and
third-party providers administer much of driver education.

## Learner's License

- **Minimum age**: 15.
- **Prerequisite**: the 4-hour **TLSAE course** (Traffic Law and
  Substance Abuse Education) — mandatory for every first-time Florida
  driver, regardless of age.
- **Knowledge test**: 50 questions on road rules and road signs;
  40 correct (80%) passes. Teens may take it online with parental
  supervision; adults take it in person.
- **Vision and hearing screening** at the office.
- **Supervision**: a licensed driver 21+ in the front passenger seat,
  at all times, for the first 3 months (daylight only).

## Intermediate License (16-17)

After holding the learner license for **12 months** (or at 18) and
logging **50 hours of practice (10 at night)**, take the driving test.

Night curfews are the core Florida restriction:

- **Age 16**: 6 a.m. to 11 p.m. only, unless going to/from work.
- **Age 17**: 5 a.m. to 1 a.m., with the same work exception.
- At 18, all time restrictions end.

## Adults 18+

Adults still need the TLSAE course. There is no holding period, so an
18-year-old can take knowledge and road tests back-to-back.

## What the FL Written Test Emphasizes

From the official Florida Driver License Handbook:

- Road signs are 20 of the 50 questions — Florida separates rules and
  signs into distinct question banks, so study both.
- Florida's insurance requirement: **PIP (Personal Injury Protection)**
  no-fault coverage — heavily tested.
- Right-of-way at four-way stops, left turns, and pedestrians in
  crosswalks.
- Speed limits: 70 mph maximum on rural interstates, 30 mph business
  districts, 20 mph school zones.
- Florida's move-over law for stopped emergency and service vehicles.
- BAC limits: 0.08% adults, 0.02% under 21, 0.04% commercial.

## Study Plan for the Florida Permit Test

1. Take the TLSAE course first — it doubles as an orientation to
   Florida traffic law.
2. Drill the road-sign bank separately; knowing shapes and colors
   cold banks 20 easy points.
3. Practice until 45+/50 consistently. The 80% bar is fair but the
   sign questions punish casual studying.
4. If taking the online exam (teens), do it at home with a parent
   proctor per FLHSMV rules; you get one online attempt before
   retaking in an office.

## Common Florida Test Mistakes

- Confusing PIP requirements with bodily-injury liability (PIP is the
  tested one for licensing).
- Forgetting the 3-month daylight-only rule for brand-new permit
  holders.
- Misreading school-zone speed signs: the limit applies only during
  posted hours when lights flash.
- Missing that Florida requires helmets for riders under 21 without
  $10,000 medical coverage — a favorite motorcycle-law question.
