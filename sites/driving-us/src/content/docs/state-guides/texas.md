## The Agency: Texas DPS

Texas handles driver licensing through the Department of Public Safety
(DPS) Driver License Division, not a "DMV" — a common point of
confusion for newcomers. Many services (practice tests, appointment
booking, license status) run through the DPS online portal.

## Phase 1: Learner License

- **Minimum age**: 15, after completing the **Impact Texas Drivers
  (ITD)** program and the classroom phase of driver education
  (32 hours classroom + behind-the-wheel phases for teens).
- **Knowledge test**: 30 questions; 21 correct (70%) passes. Included
  in many driver-ed courses, which can spare you the DPS line.
- **Vision test** required at application.
- **Supervision**: a licensed driver 21+ must occupy the front seat.

## Phase 2: Provisional License

At 16 (after holding the learner license **6 months**) you may take
the behind-the-wheel driving test. Until 18, Phase 2 restrictions:

- No driving **midnight to 5 a.m.** except for work, school, or
  emergencies.
- No passengers **under 21** unless family, and no more than one
  passenger under 21 who is not family.
- Wireless-device use is prohibited for all drivers under 18 — even
  hands-free.

## Adults 18-24

Texas requires the **6-hour adult driver education course** plus the
ITD course before the road test for any first-time driver 18-24.
The 25+ crowd takes only the knowledge and driving tests, though the
course is still recommended.

## Fees and Timelines

- Learner license fee covers application through the provisional
  stage (about $25 for under-18s at time of writing).
- Permits and licenses expire on your birthday; the permit must be
  valid on road-test day.
- DPS appointments book out weeks in metro areas (Houston, Dallas,
  Austin) — schedule the road test as soon as you become eligible.

## What the TX Written Test Emphasizes

From the official Texas Driver Handbook:

- Right-of-way: intersections, turn lanes, driveways, emergency
  vehicles, and funeral processions (a Texas-specific favorite).
- Speed limits: 70 mph rural interstates, 15 mph school zones,
  night limits on some roads.
- Insurance laws: the state's minimum liability coverage and the
  consequences of driving uninsured.
- Work zones: fines double when workers are present.
- BAC limits: 0.08% adults; Texas zero-tolerance under 21 — any
  detectable alcohol is a DUI charge.

## Study Plan for the Texas Permit Test

1. Read the handbook's right-of-way and penalty sections twice —
   Texas tests legal consequences more than most states.
2. Memorize numeric limits with flashcards: speeds, distances,
   insurance minimums, curfew hours.
3. Score 30/30 on practice runs before test day; the 70% bar is low
   but Houston and Dallas offices fail plenty of unprepared testers.
4. Bring paperwork complete: proof of identity, SSN, residency, VOE
   (Verification of Enrollment) if in school, and ITD certificate.

## Common Texas Test Mistakes

- Confusing DUI (under 21, any alcohol) with DWI (0.08%+ adults).
- Missing that you must yield to oncoming traffic when leaving a
  driveway or parking lot.
- Forgetting that passing a school bus with red lights flashing is
  illegal on both sides of undivided roads.
- Assuming turn signals are optional in roundabouts — Texas roundabout
  rules mirror standard yield-to-left practice.
