### Additional Flashcard Topics

- **Isotopes**: atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons. They have the same atomic number (protons) but different mass numbers (protons + neutrons). Carbon-12, carbon-13, and carbon-14 are isotopes of carbon.

- **Mass Spectrometry**: technique used to determine relative atomic mass. The mass spectrum shows peaks at different m/z ratios, with height proportional to abundance. Ar = Σ(isotope mass × abundance) / 100.

- **Electron Configuration**: electrons fill orbitals in order: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p. Each orbital holds 2 electrons (Pauli exclusion principle). Hund's rule: electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly first.

- **Periodic Trends**: atomic radius decreases across a period (more protons, stronger nuclear pull), increases down a group (more shells). Ionisation energy decreases down a group, increases across a period.

## Intuition

Atomic structure is like a building: the nucleus is the foundation (protons + neutrons), and electrons are the rooms arranged in floors (energy levels) and corridors (orbitals). The building rules (quantum mechanics) determine how many rooms fit on each floor and how they connect.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing atomic number with mass number**: atomic number = protons (defines the element), mass number = protons + neutrons. They are not the same.
- **Forgetting isotopes have same chemistry**: isotopes of an element have identical electron configurations, so they react the same way chemically. Only nuclear properties differ.
- **Misreading mass spectra**: the tallest peak is the most abundant isotope, not necessarily the one with the lowest mass.
