### Atomic Structure and Electron Configuration

### Practice Problem Tips

- **Write out the full configuration**: for elements beyond calcium, remember the 4s fills before 3d. Chromium and copper are exceptions: Cr is [Ar]3d⁵4s¹, Cu is [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹.
- **Count electrons carefully**: for ions, add electrons for anions, remove for cations. Fe²⁺ loses 4s electrons first, not 3d.
- **Use the periodic table**: the block (s, p, d, f) tells you which orbital is being filled. Group number relates to valence electrons.

### Approach Strategy

- For isotope questions, use the weighted average formula: Ar = (mass₁ × abundance₁ + mass₂ × abundance₂) / 100.
- For electron configuration, identify the element's position in the periodic table to determine which orbitals are occupied.
- For periodic trend questions, consider both nuclear charge (increases across period) and electron shielding (increases down group).

## Intuition

Atomic structure practice is about applying rules systematically. Each question tests whether you can follow the building-up principle (Aufbau), apply exclusion and Hund's rules, or predict trends based on nuclear charge and shielding.

## Common Mistakes

- **Forgetting the 4s exception**: for transition metals, 4s electrons are lost before 3d when forming cations. Fe²⁺ is [Ar]3d⁶, not [Ar]3d⁴4s².
- **Miscounting subshell electrons**: s holds 2, p holds 6, d holds 10, f holds 14. Students often forget p holds 6, not 8.
- **Confusing first and successive ionisation energies**: the first jump in ionisation energy indicates a new electron shell (e.g., from 2nd to 3rd in Li indicates 1s² core).
