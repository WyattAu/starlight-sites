### Additional Flashcard Topics

- **GDP vs GNP**: GDP measures output within a country's borders, GNP measures output by a country's citizens regardless of location. GNP = GDP + net income from abroad. Hong Kong's GNP is higher than GDP because of overseas investment income.

- **Inflation Types**: demand-pull inflation (too much money chasing too few goods), cost-push inflation (rising production costs), built-in inflation (wage-price spiral). Each requires different policy responses.

- **Fiscal Policy**: government use of taxation and spending to influence the economy. Expansionary fiscal policy (lower taxes, higher spending) stimulates growth but may increase deficits. Contractionary policy does the opposite.

- **Monetary Policy**: central bank use of interest rates and money supply to control inflation and growth. Lower interest rates encourage borrowing and spending; higher rates cool the economy but may slow growth.

## Intuition

Macroeconomics is about the economy as a whole, like a doctor examining a patient's overall health rather than a specific organ. GDP is the "pulse," inflation is the "temperature," and unemployment is the "blood pressure." Policy tools (fiscal, monetary) are the "treatments" applied to keep the economy healthy.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing GDP growth with standard of living**: GDP per capita may rise while inequality increases. GDP measures output, not well-being.
- **Assuming lower interest rates always help**: while they stimulate borrowing, they also reduce savings income and may cause asset bubbles.
- **Ignoring the trade-off**: expansionary policy may reduce unemployment but increase inflation. There's rarely a free lunch in macroeconomics.
