### Additional Flashcard Topics

- **Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)**: measures responsiveness of quantity demanded to price change. PED = % change in Qd / % change in P. If |PED| > 1, demand is elastic; if < 1, inelastic. Determinants include availability of substitutes, necessity vs luxury, and time period.

- **Consumer Surplus**: the difference between what consumers are willing to pay and what they actually pay. Graphically, it's the area below the demand curve and above the price line. It represents welfare gained from trade.

- **Market Failure Types**: positive externalities (education benefits society), negative externalities (pollution), public goods (non-rival, non-excludable), information asymmetry (used car market). Each requires different government intervention.

- **Perfect Competition Characteristics**: many buyers and sellers, homogeneous product, perfect information, free entry and exit. Firms are price takers and earn zero economic profit in long-run equilibrium.

## Intuition

Microeconomics studies individual decision-making: consumers maximising utility, firms maximising profit, and how markets coordinate these decisions through prices. Think of it as the "physics" of economic behaviour—simple rules (supply, demand) creating complex outcomes (market prices, resource allocation).

## Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing movement along a curve with shift of a curve**: price change causes movement along demand/supply curve; non-price factors (income, tastes) shift the curve.
- **Forgetting the ceteris paribus assumption**: when analysing one variable, all others are held constant. Real-world changes often involve multiple variables shifting simultaneously.
- **Mixing up normal and inferior goods**: normal goods have positive income elasticity (buy more when richer), inferior goods have negative income elasticity (buy less when richer).
