### GDP and GNP Measurement

### Practice Problem Tips

- **Distinguish real vs nominal GDP**: nominal GDP uses current prices; real GDP adjusts for inflation using a base year. Real GDP growth shows true output changes.
- **Identify the multiplier effect**: government spending multiplies through the economy. If the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is 0.8, the multiplier is 1/(1-MPC) = 5.
- **Read graphs carefully**: AD-AS diagrams show equilibrium output and price level. Shifts in AD or AS change both; movements along curves are caused by price level changes.

### Approach Strategy

- For GDP calculation questions, identify which components (C, I, G, X-M) are given and sum them.
- For policy questions, determine whether the economy needs expansionary (recession) or contractionary (inflation) policy.
- For trade questions, remember: exports are credit items (+), imports are debit items (-).

## Intuition

Macroeconomics practice is about diagnosing economic conditions and prescribing policies. Each question asks: what is the problem (inflation, unemployment, slow growth)? What tool (fiscal, monetary, trade) can address it? What are the side effects?

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing fiscal and monetary policy**: fiscal = government (taxes/spending), monetary = central bank (interest rates/money supply). Students often swap their roles.
- **Ignoring time lags**: policies take time to work. Monetary policy has shorter lags than fiscal policy. Don't assume immediate effects.
- **Forgetting the trade-off**: expansionary policy may reduce unemployment but increase inflation. There's no single policy that solves all problems simultaneously.
