### GDP and GNP Measurement

### Practice Problem Tips

- **Distinguish real vs nominal GDP**: nominal GDP uses current prices; real GDP adjusts for inflation using a base year. Real GDP growth shows true output changes. If nominal GDP grows 5% but inflation is 3%, real GDP growth is only 2%.
- **Identify the multiplier effect**: government spending multiplies through the economy. If the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is 0.8, the multiplier is 1/(1-MPC) = 5. A $100 million government spending increase raises GDP by $500 million.
- **Read graphs carefully**: AD-AS diagrams show equilibrium output and price level. Shifts in AD or AS change both; movements along curves are caused by price level changes. Label all axes and curves correctly.
- **Calculate CPI changes**: CPI = (cost of basket in current year / cost of basket in base year) × 100. Inflation rate = ((CPI₂ - CPI₁)/CPI₁) × 100%. Always use percentage change, not absolute change.

### Approach Strategy

- For GDP calculation questions, identify which components (C, I, G, X-M) are given and sum them. Watch for net exports (X-M), not just exports.
- For policy questions, determine whether the economy needs expansionary (recession) or contractionary (inflation) policy. Then identify the correct tool: fiscal (government) or monetary (central bank).
- For trade questions, remember: exports are credit items (+), imports are debit items (-). A trade surplus means exports > imports.
- For unemployment questions, calculate the unemployment rate = (unemployed / labour force) × 100%. The labour force includes only those actively seeking work, not the entire population.

## Intuition

Macroeconomics practice is about diagnosing economic conditions and prescribing policies. Each question asks: what is the problem (inflation, unemployment, slow growth)? What tool (fiscal, monetary, trade) can address it? What are the side effects? The key skill is linking causes to consequences to policy responses. For example: recession → low AD → expansionary fiscal policy → higher government spending → multiplier effect → increased output and employment.

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing fiscal and monetary policy**: fiscal = government (taxes/spending), monetary = central bank (interest rates/money supply). Students often swap their roles. The HKMA conducts monetary policy; the government conducts fiscal policy.
- **Ignoring time lags**: policies take time to work. Monetary policy has shorter lags (months) than fiscal policy (years). Don't assume immediate effects—policies announced today may take 12-18 months to fully impact the economy.
- **Forgetting the trade-off**: expansionary policy may reduce unemployment but increase inflation. There's no single policy that solves all problems simultaneously. Policymakers must prioritise.
- **Misidentifying equilibrium**: equilibrium is where AD = AS. If the economy is below full employment, there is a recessionary gap; if above, an inflationary gap. The government aims to close these gaps.
- **Confusing budget deficit with national debt**: deficit = annual shortfall (spending > revenue); debt = accumulated deficits over time. A country can have a deficit without increasing debt if it runs surpluses in other years.
