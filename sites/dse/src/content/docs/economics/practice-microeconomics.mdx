### Practice Problem Tips

- **Identify the market structure**: perfect competition (many firms, price takers), monopoly (one firm, price maker), oligopoly (few firms, strategic interaction), monopolistic competition (differentiated products).
- **Calculate elasticity precisely**: PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP. Use the midpoint formula for arc elasticity: (Q2-Q1)/((Q2+Q1)/2) ÷ (P2-P1)/((P2+P1)/2).
- **Find equilibrium graphically**: where supply and demand curves intersect. At this point, quantity supplied equals quantity demanded.

### Approach Strategy

- For demand/supply questions, first draw the diagram, then identify shifts vs movements along curves.
- For elasticity questions, remember: elastic demand means quantity changes more than price; inelastic means quantity changes less.
- For market structure questions, compare profit maximisation (MR = MC) across different firm types.

## Intuition

Microeconomics practice is about understanding how individuals and firms make decisions under scarcity. Each question tests whether you can predict behaviour: if price rises, what happens to quantity demanded? If a tax is imposed, who bears the burden?

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing shifts with movements**: a price change causes movement along the curve; a change in income, tastes, or related goods shifts the curve.
- **Misidentifying equilibrium**: equilibrium is where Qs = Qd, not where supply or demand is highest.
- **Ignoring elasticity for revenue**: if demand is elastic, raising price reduces total revenue; if inelastic, raising price increases total revenue. Don't assume higher price always means more revenue.
