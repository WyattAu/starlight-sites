### Basic Economic Concepts

### Practice Problem Tips

- **Identify the market structure**: perfect competition (many firms, price takers), monopoly (one firm, price maker), oligopoly (few firms, strategic interaction), monopolistic competition (differentiated products). Each has different profit-maximising behaviour.
- **Calculate elasticity precisely**: PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP. Use the midpoint formula for arc elasticity: (Q₂-Q₁)/((Q₂+Q₁)/2) ÷ (P₂-P₁)/((P₂+P₁)/2). This gives a more accurate measure than using initial values.
- **Find equilibrium graphically**: where supply and demand curves intersect. At this point, quantity supplied equals quantity demanded. The equilibrium price and quantity are read from the axes.
- **Calculate total revenue**: TR = P × Q. If demand is elastic, raising price reduces TR. If inelastic, raising price increases TR. Always check elasticity before predicting the effect of price changes on revenue.

### Approach Strategy

- For demand/supply questions, first draw the diagram, then identify shifts vs movements along curves. Label axes, curves, and equilibrium clearly.
- For elasticity questions, remember: elastic demand means quantity changes more than price; inelastic means quantity changes less. A vertical demand curve is perfectly inelastic; a horizontal one is perfectly elastic.
- For market structure questions, compare profit maximisation (MR = MC) across different firm types. In perfect competition, P = MR; in monopoly, P > MR.
- For government intervention questions, identify the type of market failure and the appropriate remedy: tax for negative externalities, subsidy for positive externalities, regulation for information asymmetry.

## Intuition

Microeconomics practice is about understanding how individuals and firms make decisions under scarcity. Each question tests whether you can predict behaviour: if price rises, what happens to quantity demanded? If a tax is imposed, who bears the burden? The key skill is identifying the relevant market, the decision-makers, and the constraints they face. Economic reasoning is about trade-offs: every choice has an opportunity cost, and rational decision-makers compare marginal benefits with marginal costs.

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing shifts with movements**: a price change causes movement along the curve; a change in income, tastes, or related goods shifts the curve. This is the single most tested concept in microeconomics.
- **Misidentifying equilibrium**: equilibrium is where Qs = Qd, not where supply or demand is highest. The equilibrium may shift when external factors change.
- **Ignoring elasticity for revenue**: if demand is elastic, raising price reduces total revenue; if inelastic, raising price increases total revenue. Don't assume higher price always means more revenue.
- **Forgetting ceteris paribus**: when analysing one change, assume all other factors are held constant. Real-world markets involve multiple simultaneous changes, but exam questions isolate one factor.
- **Misunderstanding producer surplus**: producer surplus is the area above the supply curve and below the price line. It represents the benefit producers gain from selling at a higher price than their minimum acceptable price.
