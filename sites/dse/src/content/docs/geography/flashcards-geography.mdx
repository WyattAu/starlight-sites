### Additional Flashcard Topics

- **Demographic Transition Model**: describes population change through five stages: high birth/death (pre-industrial), falling death rate (transitional), falling birth rate (industrial), low birth/death (post-industrial), and declining population. Each stage has different implications for development.

- **Plate Tectonics**: divergent boundaries (plates move apart, new crust forms), convergent boundaries (plates collide, subduction or mountain building), transform boundaries (plates slide past each other, earthquakes). These explain volcanic and seismic activity patterns.

- **Urbanisation Patterns**: primate cities dominate national urban systems (e.g., Bangkok in Thailand), while gamma cities are smaller regional centres. Urbanisation rates vary by development level.

- **Climate Classification**: the Köppen system uses temperature and precipitation patterns to classify climates into five major groups (A-E). Tropical (A), arid (B), temperate (C), continental (D), polar (E).

## Intuition

Geography connects people to place. Think of it as understanding why things are where they are: why cities form at river mouths, why earthquakes follow fault lines, why some countries are rich and others poor. Each flashcard reveals a pattern that connects physical processes to human outcomes.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing weather and climate**: weather is short-term (days), climate is long-term average (30+ years). "It's cold today" is weather; "London has mild winters" is climate.
- **Ignoring scale**: a local flood is different from a regional monsoon pattern. Always consider whether the question asks about local, regional, or global scale.
- **Overlooking human factors**: physical geography explains potential, but human decisions (policy, technology, culture) determine outcomes. Don't ignore the human dimension.
