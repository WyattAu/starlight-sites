## Intuition

**Learning through practice:** Practice problems are like training drills — they help you apply knowledge under exam conditions and identify areas that need more study.

**Why it matters:** Regular practice builds confidence and reveals patterns in how questions are asked. Each problem reinforces key concepts and exam techniques.

**The key insight:** Making mistakes during practice is valuable — each error points to a concept that needs clarification before the real exam.

### Economic Activities

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"According to von Thunen\'s model, which type of farming would be located closest to the central market?&quot; options={[ 'Extensive cattle ranching', 'Grain farming', 'Dairy farming and vegetable production', 'Commercial forestry', ]} correctAnswer={2} explanation=&quot;Von Thunen\'s model predicts that perishable and high-value products are located closest to the market because they have high transport costs and spoil quickly. Dairy farming and vegetable production are perishable, bulky, and require daily delivery, so farmers locate them nearest to the market to minimise transport costs and losses. Extensive activities like ranching and forestry are placed furthest from the market where land is cheaper."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"A transnational corporation (TNC) decides to build a new factory in a Special Economic Zone (SEZ) in southern China. While proximity to raw materials and markets can be factors, the defining feature of SEZs is their favourable government policies. TNCs locate in SEZs primarily to take advantage of these economic incentives, lower operating costs, and export-oriented infrastructure."} difficulty="medium"  />

### Physical Geography

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"At which type of plate boundary is a volcanic island arc most likely to form?&quot; options={[ 'Divergent boundary', 'Continental-continental convergent boundary', 'Oceanic-continental convergent boundary', 'Transform boundary', ]} correctAnswer={2} explanation=&quot;Volcanic island arcs form at oceanic-continental convergent boundaries where an oceanic plate subducts beneath a continental plate. As the oceanic plate descends into the mantle, it melts and generates magma that rises through the continental plate, forming a chain of volcanoes parallel to the boundary. Examples include the Andes (South America) and the Cascade Range (North America). Divergent boundaries produce mid-ocean ridges, not island arcs. Continental-continental collisions produce fold mountains (e.g., Himalayas)."} difficulty="hard"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"A river floodplain is formed primarily by which processes?&quot; options={[ 'Only erosion by the river during flood events', 'Deposition of alluvium (sediment) when the river overflows its banks during flood events', 'Chemical weathering of bedrock in the river channel', 'Tectonic uplift along the river valley', ]} correctAnswer={1} explanation=&quot;River floodplains are formed by the deposition of alluvium (fine sediment such as silt, clay, and sand) when a river overflows its banks during flood events. As floodwater spreads across the flat land adjacent to the channel, it loses energy and velocity, causing sediment to settle and build up in layers over time. This creates the flat, fertile floodplain characteristic of mature river valleys. While erosion occurs in the channel, it is deposition on the banks that creates the floodplain."} difficulty="hard"  />

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing physical and human geography topics:** Physical geography covers natural processes (climate, landforms). Human geography covers human activities (urbanisation, migration). Don't mix them up in exam answers.

**Forgetting to use specific case studies:** DSE Geography rewards specific examples (e.g., Sha Tin new town, Typhoon Mangkhut). Generic answers score fewer marks than those with named locations and data.

**Mixing up push and pull factors in migration:** Push factors drive people away from places (poverty, conflict). Pull factors attract people to destinations (jobs, education). Always identify which is which.
