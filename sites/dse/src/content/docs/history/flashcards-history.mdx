### Additional Flashcard Topics

- **Causes of WWI**: alliance system (Triple Alliance vs Triple Entente), militarism, imperialism, nationalism. The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in 1914 was the immediate trigger, but underlying tensions had been building for decades.

- **Chinese Communist Revolution**: Mao Zedong's Long March (1934-35) preserved the CCP. The CCP won peasant support through land reform. By 1949, the PRC was established after the civil war against the KMT.

- **Hong Kong handover**: the Sino-British Joint Declaration (1984) guaranteed Hong Kong's autonomy under "one country, two systems" for 50 years after the 1997 handover. This was a significant moment in decolonisation.

- **Cold Warproxy conflicts**: Korea (1950-53), Vietnam (1955-75), and the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962) were key episodes where the US and USSR competed through allies rather than direct confrontation.

## Intuition

History is about understanding cause and effect across time. Think of events as links in a chain: WWI led to WWII, which led to the Cold War, which influenced decolonisation. Each flashcard helps you see not just what happened, but why it happened and what followed.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Confusing correlation with causation**: just because two events happened around the same time doesn't mean one caused the other. Look for direct evidence of causation.
- **Presentism**: judging historical events by modern standards without considering the context of the time. Understanding historical context is essential for fair analysis.
- **Over-simplifying complex events**: the Chinese Revolution wasn't just about Mao vs Chiang; it involved warlords, Japan, the US, and millions of ordinary people.
