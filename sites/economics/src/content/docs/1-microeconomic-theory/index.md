Microeconomic theory is the mathematics of choice under scarcity. Its results are theorems —
existence of utility representations, existence and structure of demand, duality between what
consumers maximise and what firms minimise, the welfare theorems — and this track proves them
rather than asserting them, because the hypotheses (continuity, convexity, local nonsatiation)
are exactly what applied work leans on.

## The dependency chain

1. [Preferences and Utility](/1-microeconomic-theory/1_preferences-and-utility/) — the axioms,
   and the theorem that continuous preferences admit a continuous utility representation.
2. [Utility Maximisation](/1-microeconomic-theory/2_utility-maximisation/) — the consumer problem,
   Marshallian demand, indirect utility, expenditure function, Shephard's lemma, and the Slutsky
   decomposition — with proofs.
3. [Production, Cost, and the Firm](/1-microeconomic-theory/5_production-and-firm/) — cost minimisation by Lagrangian, Shephard's lemma proved, Hotelling's lemma, and the duality between production and cost.
4. General equilibrium and the welfare theorems (coming).

## Prerequisites

The mathematics site's real-analysis track (continuity, compactness, Weierstrass) and the
Langrangian method from multivariable calculus. Convexity is used everywhere and proved where it
is proved.

## The standard

Every theorem on this site carries its proof or an honestly-labelled sketch with the reference.
Terms are defined before use, and each page closes with a self-test keyed to its sections. The
prerequisites and the learning-science rationale are on the [study hub](/hub/).

## The self-test convention

Each page closes with a self-test keyed to its sections, and the harder items ask where a proof
used which hypothesis — the question applied work keeps asking. The [study hub](/hub/) explains
the learning-science rationale: recalling an answer beats rereading it, and the recall that
matters in economics is of assumptions.
