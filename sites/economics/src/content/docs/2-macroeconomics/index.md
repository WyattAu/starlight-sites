Macroeconomics on this site is model-first: each page states a model precisely, derives its
mechanics, and reads the results off the derivation. Where a result is a theorem (convergence in
Solow, Ricardian equivalence conditions, the term structure of policy effects), it is proved.

## Planned and landing pages

- **The Solow growth model** — the law of motion $\dot{k} = sf(k) - (n + \delta)k$ derived,
  existence and stability of the steady state proved, the golden rule computed, and convergence
  dynamics read off the phase diagram.
- **The Ramsey problem** — the household's Euler equation derived from the utility
  maximisation, the modified golden rule, and why the discount rate disciplines the steady state.
- **Fluctuations** — the multiplier derived from the Keynesian cross, then the IS--LM
  apparatus as comparative statics, then the policy questions the apparatus can and cannot
  answer.

The microeconomic foundations come from the [micro theory track](/1-microeconomic-theory/):
macro models here are consistent with the household and firm problems proved there, which is
exactly the discipline microfoundations is supposed to impose.

## The method of the track

Each model on this site is treated the same way: state the agents and their constraints, derive
the equilibrium condition, prove the comparative statics. The discipline is microfoundations —
macro models here are consistent with the household and firm problems proved in the micro track,
so a reduced-form relation is never doing work the structure forbids.

The growth pages come first because their results are theorems: the Solow steady state exists
and is stable, convergence follows from diminishing returns, and the golden rule is a computable
object — all provable with one-variable calculus and a phase diagram. Fluctuations come after
because their models trade tractability for realism, and the trade is easier to see once the
clean case is proved.

## What the track is not

This is not a forecast site. Models here make conditional statements — if the assumptions hold,
the mechanics follow — and the empirical question of whether they hold belongs to the
[econometrics track](/3-econometrics/), whose theorems exist precisely to police that boundary.
