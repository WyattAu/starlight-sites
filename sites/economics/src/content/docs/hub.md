This site treats economics the way [mathematics](https://mathematics.wyattau.com) treats
mathematics: the central results are proved, the hypotheses are named, and the failures of
applied work are traced to the hypothesis that failed. Economics is a theorem-first subject at
university level, and the proofs are where the assumptions become visible; which assumption buys
unbiasedness, which buys efficiency, which makes an equilibrium exist.

## The tracks

- **[Microeconomic Theory](/1-microeconomic-theory/)**; preferences, the utility representation
  theorem, the consumer problem, expenditure duality with Shephard's lemma proved, the Slutsky
  decomposition derived exactly, and the welfare theorems. Start here: the consumer's problem is
  the load-bearing model for everything downstream, and the duality machinery is reused by every
  later track.
- **[Macroeconomics](/2-macroeconomics/)**; growth and fluctuations from models whose mechanics
  are derived rather than described: the Solow steady state proved to exist and be stable, the
  Ramsey Euler equation derived from the household problem, and the multiplier read off the
  Keynesian cross before IS--LM is laid over it.
- **[Econometrics](/3-econometrics/)**; the OLS estimator from the orthogonality condition up,
  unbiasedness with the exact location of the exogeneity input located inside the proof, and
  Gauss--Markov in full by completing the square; including the table of which hypothesis buys
  which part of the conclusion, because that table is what applied work consults when a result
  misbehaves.
- **[Game Theory](/4-game-theory/)**; dominance that survives iterated elimination (and why
  weak dominance does not), best responses, and Nash existence for finite games proved via
  Kakutani with all four hypotheses verified. The proof structure; compact convex space,
  nonempty convex values, closed graph, fixed point; is the template nearly every existence
  theorem in economics reuses.

## How to read this site

Terms are defined before they are used, every theorem carries its proof or an honestly-labelled
sketch with the complete argument referenced, and each page closes with a self-test keyed to its
sections. The self-test items are written as retrieval practice, and the harder ones deliberately
ask *where a proof used a hypothesis*; because that is the question applied work keeps asking.

## Prerequisites

Micro and game theory need comfort with sets, continuity, and convexity; the Lagrangian method
appears in the consumer problem and is developed on the mathematics site's multivariable
calculus track. Econometrics needs matrix algebra through positive-definite quadratic forms and
probability through conditional expectation. The
[mathematics site](https://mathematics.wyattau.com) covers both from first principles, and its
proof standard is the bar this site holds itself to.

## The relationship to exam-board economics

A-Level and IB economics teach institutions, diagrams, and evaluation technique; this site
teaches the machinery underneath. Supply and demand is the exam-board treatment of what the
micro track proves as constrained optimisation meeting market clearing; elasticity is a
comparative static; game theory at IB level is the informal version of the equilibrium existence
result proved here. The two levels reinforce each other, and the cross-references between them
are deliberate.

## Corrections and error reporting

Errors in proofs are the worst defects a site like this can ship, and readers find them. Anything
found wrong is fixed and the correction is recorded on the page. The same policy covers notation:
if a proof uses a convention (row vectors, preference directions) that differs from your course's,
the divergence is stated where it occurs.
