Economics at university level is a theorem-first subject, and this site treats it that way: the
central results — existence of demand, the welfare theorems, Gauss--Markov, Nash existence — are
proved with their hypotheses named, because the hypotheses are what applied work leans on and
what applied failures reveal.

## Start here

- New to university economics: begin with
  [Preferences and Utility](/1-microeconomic-theory/1_preferences-and-utility/) — the axioms are
  the foundation every later theorem consumes.
- Coming from A-Level or IB economics: the [study hub](/hub/) explains how the exam-board
  treatment maps onto the university one, and the cross-references run both directions.
- Here for the empirical side: the [econometrics track](/3-econometrics/) starts at the OLS
  estimator and proves Gauss--Markov on the second page.

## The standard

Every theorem carries its proof or an honestly-labelled sketch with the complete argument
referenced. Hypotheses are named where they are used. Terms are defined before use. Each page
ends with a self-test keyed to its sections. The [study hub](/hub/) and [about page](/about/)
spell out the standard; the [glossary](/glossary/) collects the vocabulary.
