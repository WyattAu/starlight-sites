This site provides free, university-level finance notes in which the core arguments are proved.
No-arbitrage arguments are written out completely; optimisation results are derived, not stated;
and every model says which frictions it ignores.

## The standard

Theorems carry proofs or honestly-labelled sketches. Hypotheses are named where they are used.
Assumptions famous for being false in practice; frictionless markets, continuous trading,
common beliefs; are stated as assumptions, with the robustness question, what survives their
failure, addressed rather than hidden.

## Prerequisites and company

Probability through expectation and variance is the only mathematical prerequisite. The
[economics site's](https://economics.wyattau.com) econometrics track is the empirical
continuation, and the [mathematics site](https://mathematics.wyattau.com) covers the
foundations both assume.

## Not investment advice

The models here are teaching models. Their assumptions are false in the real market, on purpose
and in instructive ways; nothing on this site is a recommendation, and the gap between model and
market is where practitioner skill lives; this site teaches the models.

## Corrections

Mistakes in pricing arguments are worse than useless; they teach miscalibration. Anything found
wrong is fixed and the correction is recorded in the page, because knowing where a document was
wrong is part of knowing how much to trust it.

## Self-tests

Each page closes with self-test items keyed to its sections, written as retrieval practice. The
harder ones ask where an argument used no-arbitrage versus where it used a preference assumption: the distinction that separates pricing results from equilibrium results, and the one exam
answers most often blur.

## The writing convention

Definitions inline, arguments broken at their genuine moves, and cross-references that name what
the referenced page proves. The static results (law of one price, parity, bounds) are separated
cleanly from the equilibrium results (CAPM); pricing needs only no-arbitrage; equilibrium needs
preferences and clearing; and the site keeps that boundary visible, because conflating the two
is the most common confusion in the subject.

## The maintenance model

Content is checked by tooling on every change: depth minimums, frontmatter validity, link
integrity, and LaTeX parseability. The pricing arguments carry proofs precisely so that the
tooling-checked pages teach checkable things.

## The network

This site is part of a network: exam-board sites for the school-facing treatments, subject sites
(mathematics, physics, chemistry, computer science, economics, accounting) for the
discipline-facing layer, and a machine-learning site for the applied layer. Cross-references
between them are maintained by tooling.

## How to report an error

Anything that looks wrong; a replication step, a bound, a definition; is worth reporting. The
fix lands with the correction recorded, and a page that needs repeated patches gets a rewrite,
because repeated patches mean the structure is wrong rather than the sentences.

## The level in one paragraph

The level is the university asset-pricing sequence: present value done properly, mean--variance
from the Lagrangian, the CAPM from clearing, the binomial model with the replication proof. That
sequence is the spine of the subject; the pages are its complete arguments, with the empirical
and institutional layers named as theirs.

## The self-test convention

Self-test items are keyed to sections and written as retrieval practice. The harder items ask
which assumption an argument used; no-arbitrage, market clearing, or a preference restriction -
because pricing results and equilibrium results fail differently, and the diagnosis starts with
knowing which family an argument belongs to.

## What to read first

The study hub's track summaries, then Principles. Everything else is sequenced from there. The
glossary is the reference layer, not the entry point; terms are defined where they are first
used, and the glossary collects them for recall.

## The proof standard, restated

A proof on this site means every inequality and every equality is justified at the step where it
appears, with the justification named: a law, a definition, or a previous theorem. Steps that are
obvious may be compressed; steps that are merely plausible may not. That is the same standard the
mathematics site uses, and it is the reason arguments here transfer to exams and to papers
unchanged.

## The standard, restated

Proved not cited, hypotheses named, assumptions honest, corrections recorded. Four sentences of
standard; the site is their execution.
