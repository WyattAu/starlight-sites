Finance on this site is built the way it is taught at strong universities: from the no-arbitrage
principle outward. Nearly every headline result — the law of one price, put-call parity, the
CAPM, risk-neutral pricing — is either a direct consequence of no-arbitrage or a theorem proved
from it, and the proofs are short enough to actually learn rather than memorise.

## The tracks

- **[Principles of Finance](/1-principles/)** — present value, the law of one price, and
  no-arbitrage bounds proved; the time value of money done properly, with the discounting
  algebra derived rather than asserted.
- **[Portfolio Theory](/2-portfolio-theory/)** — mean--variance optimisation solved by
  Lagrangian, the two-fund separation theorem proved, and the CAPM derived from market clearing
  plus everyone holding the tangency portfolio.
- **[Derivatives](/3-derivatives/)** — the binomial model as the cleanest laboratory: the
  replication argument proved, risk-neutral probabilities constructed, put-call parity from
  no-arbitrage, and the road toward the continuous-time limit.

## What the proofs buy

Finance is unusually kind to the reader: its core arguments are complete in a few lines. Put-call
parity is two portfolios; the risk-neutral argument is one change of measure justified by
replication; two-fund separation is a Lagrangian and a market-clearing observation. Learning them
as proofs — rather than as slogans — is what lets you see which assumption a real market violates
and what that violation does to the price.

## Prerequisites and reading order

Probability through expectation and variance is the only mathematical prerequisite; the
[mathematics site](https://mathematics.wyattau.com) covers it. The tracks are sequential:
[Principles](/1-principles/) before [Portfolio Theory](/2-portfolio-theory/) before
[Derivatives](/3-derivatives/). The [economics site's](https://economics.wyattau.com)
econometrics track is the natural continuation for the empirical side — factor models are
tested, not just derived.

## What the models are not

The models here are teaching models: frictionless, complete where needed, and false in precisely
the ways that make them illuminating. Their assumptions are stated because the gap between model
and market is where practitioner skill lives — and this site teaches the models, not the
execution craft, which does not survive transfer through a page.

## Corrections and error reporting

Pricing arguments are checkable, and checkable arguments should be checked. Errors found by
readers are fixed and recorded on the page. The same policy covers assumptions: if a result
silently requires market completeness or continuous trading, and the page did not say so, the
omission is the error.

## The exam-board connection

School-level business and economics syllabi touch present value and diversification informally;
the exam-board sites carry those treatments. This site is the layer underneath — the arguments
the informal versions gesture at — and the cross-references are maintained by tooling.

## The self-test convention

Each page closes with a self-test keyed to its sections, written as retrieval practice. The
harder items ask where an argument used no-arbitrage versus where it used a preference
assumption — the distinction that separates pricing results from equilibrium results, and the one
exam answers most often blur.

## Where the site is going

The principles and portfolio tracks' opening pages land next, then the binomial model, then
put-call parity and the bounds, then the road to the continuous-time limit. Every addition keeps
the replication logic visible, because that is what transfers when the products get exotic.

## The self-test standard

Self-test items are execution-first: replicate this payoff, derive this frontier, check this
bound. The verification habit — every price checked against a replication — is the subject's
own discipline, and the self-tests are where it gets rehearsed before it matters.

## The teaching order inside a page

Pages open with the claim, then the argument, then the reading — what the result means and where
its assumptions bind. That order is deliberate: arguments land better when the destination is
named first, and the reading section is where the assumptions stop being formal and start being
economic.

## The one-sentence version

Price = cost of the portfolio that copies the payoffs; portfolios that everyone must hold become
the benchmark everyone is priced against. The tracks prove the first sentence, then the second.

## The line that summarises the site

Price what you can replicate; require a price for what you cannot; know which one you are doing.
The tracks teach the first sentence as arithmetic, the second as theory, and the distinction as
judgment — the order matters, and the hub exists to keep it visible.

## The reading discipline

One track at a time, self-tests before the next page, and the replication check after every
price. The site is short by design; the discipline is what makes the shortness complete rather
than thin.
