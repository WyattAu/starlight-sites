Finance at university level is built from one principle outward: no-arbitrage. Nearly every
headline result — the law of one price, put-call parity, two-fund separation, the CAPM,
risk-neutral pricing — is either a direct consequence of no-arbitrage or a theorem proved from
it, and the proofs are short enough to learn completely.

## Start here

- Begin with [No-Arbitrage and the Law of One Price](/1-principles/1_no-arbitrage/) — the proof
  needs no preference assumptions, and everything downstream reuses its motion.
- Then the [portfolio track](/2-portfolio-theory/): the Lagrangian, the tangency portfolio, and
  the CAPM as a market-clearing consequence.
- The [derivatives track](/3-derivatives/) finishes the static spine: replication, risk-neutral
  pricing, parity.

## The standard

Every theorem carries its proof or an honestly-labelled sketch with the complete argument
referenced. Assumptions famous for being false in practice are stated as assumptions, with the
robustness question addressed rather than hidden. The [study hub](/hub/), [about page](/about/),
and [glossary](/glossary/) carry the details.
