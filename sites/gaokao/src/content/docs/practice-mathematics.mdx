## Intuition

**Mathematical problem-solving is a systematic process:** Every problem has a logical path to the solution. The challenge is identifying which concepts and techniques apply, then executing them precisely under time pressure.

**Why it matters:** Mathematics forms the backbone of science and engineering, and strong mathematical skills are essential for competitive examinations.

**The key insight:** Practice with past papers is the most effective preparation — it builds familiarity with question formats and reveals patterns in what examiners test.

## Common Mistakes

**Forgetting the domain when solving equations:** When solving $\sqrt{x} = x - 2$, squaring both sides introduces extraneous solutions. Always check that proposed solutions satisfy the original equation and any domain restrictions.

**Confusing arithmetic and geometric sequences:** The nth term formula $a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d$ (arithmetic) is fundamentally different from $a_n = a_1 \cdot r^{n-1}$ (geometric). Using the wrong formula gives incorrect answers for sum and term calculations.

**Sign errors in vector dot product:** The dot product $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta$ can be negative when $\theta > 90°$. Forgetting the sign leads to wrong angle calculations. Always track the sign through the computation.
