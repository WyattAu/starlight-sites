## Intuition

**Learning through practice:** Practice problems are like training drills — they help you apply knowledge under exam conditions and identify areas that need more study.

**Why it matters:** Regular practice builds confidence and reveals patterns in how questions are asked. Each problem reinforces key concepts and exam techniques.

**The key insight:** Making mistakes during practice is valuable — each error points to a concept that needs clarification before the real exam.

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing mitosis with meiosis:** Mitosis produces two identical diploid daughter cells for growth and repair; meiosis produces four genetically different haploid gametes for sexual reproduction. Students often swap the outcomes — remember: mitosis = "more of the same," meiosis = "half for reproduction."

**Mixing up arteries and veins by what they carry:** Arteries carry blood AWAY from the heart; veins carry blood TOWARD the heart. The pulmonary artery is the exception (carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs). Do not define them by oxygen content — define them by direction of flow.

**Assuming all enzymes work best at body temperature:** Enzymes from thermophilic bacteria (e.g., in hot springs) have optimum temperatures well above 37°C. In exams, always check whether the question specifies a particular organism's enzymes. Human enzymes in standard practice work best at 37°C, but this is not universal.
