## Common Mistakes

**Confusing simile with metaphor:** A simile compares using "like" or "as" (e.g., "brave as a lion"). A metaphor states something IS something else (e.g., "the world is a stage"). In exams, misidentifying the device loses marks even if your analysis is otherwise strong. Check for "like" or "as" before deciding.

**Writing plot summary instead of analysis:** In essay questions, students often retell the story rather than analysing how the writer creates meaning. Every point must link back to the writer's methods and their effects. Ask: "What technique is used?" and "Why does the writer do this?" for every paragraph.

**Forgetting PEEL paragraph structure:** Point → Evidence → Explanation → Link. Many students write a point and evidence but skip the Explanation (analysing the quotation) or the Link (connecting back to the question). The Explanation and Link are where most marks are awarded.
