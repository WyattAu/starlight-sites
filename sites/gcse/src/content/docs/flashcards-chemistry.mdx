## Intuition

Chemistry is about **understanding what matter is made of and how it transforms**. The key insight is that all matter consists of atoms, and chemical reactions are just atoms rearranging — nothing is created or destroyed, only rearranged.

**Atomic structure intuition:** Atoms have a dense, positive nucleus surrounded by negative electrons in energy levels. The number of protons (atomic number) determines the element. The number of neutrons can vary (isotopes), and the number of electrons determines the atom's chemical behaviour. Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons, which is why they share similar properties.

**Bonding intuition:** Atoms bond to achieve a full outer shell (the octet rule). Ionic bonding transfers electrons (metal → non-metal), creating charged ions that attract. Covalent bonding shares electrons (non-metal + non-metal), forming molecules. Metallic bonding involves a "sea" of delocalised electrons, which explains why metals conduct electricity and are malleable.

**Rates of reaction intuition:** Three factors control reaction rate: concentration (more particles → more collisions), temperature (faster particles → more energetic collisions), and surface area (more exposed particles → more collisions). A catalyst provides an alternative pathway with lower activation energy without being consumed.

## Key Formulas

| Formula | What It Calculates | Units |
| --------- | ------------------- | ------- |
| **Number of moles** | $n = \frac{m}{M}$ | mol |
| **Concentration** | $c = \frac{n}{V}$ | mol/dm³ |

| **Energy released** | $Q = mc\Delta T$ | J |
| **Percentage yield** | $\frac{\text{actual}}{\text{theoretical}} \times 100\%$ | % |

## Common Pitfalls

**Confusing atoms, molecules, and ions.** An atom is a single particle. A molecule is two or more atoms bonded together. An ion is an atom or molecule that has gained or lost electrons, giving it a charge. An element can exist as individual atoms (noble gases) or as molecules (O₂, N₂).

**Forgetting that mass is conserved in reactions.** The total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products. This is because atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed. If a reaction appears to lose mass, gas has escaped.

**Confusing empirical and molecular formulas.** The empirical formula gives the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. The molecular formula gives the actual number of atoms in one molecule. For example, the empirical formula of glucose is CH₂O, but the molecular formula is C₆H₁₂O₆.
