## General Examination Terms

**Assessment Objective (AO)**: The skills and knowledge being assessed. Different subjects have different AOs (e.g., AO1: demonstrate knowledge, AO2: apply knowledge, AO3: analyze and evaluate).

**Coursework**: Written or practical assignments completed during the course, contributing to the final grade.

**Grade Boundary**: The minimum marks required to achieve each grade (9-1) for each subject and paper.

**Foundation Tier**: The lower tier of a subject, covering grades 1-5. More accessible content and questions.

**Higher Tier**: The upper tier of a subject, covering grades 4-9. More challenging content and questions.

**Non-Exam Assessment (NEA)**: Assessment that is not through a formal examination. Includes coursework, practical assessments, and oral assessments.

**Paper**: A written examination component. Most subjects have 1-3 papers.

**Specimen Paper**: A sample paper provided by the exam board to illustrate the format and style of questions.

**Tiered Assessment**: Splitting a subject into Foundation and Higher tiers to accommodate different ability levels.

## English Language

**Analogy**: A comparison between two things, highlighting similarities for explanatory effect.

**Antithesis**: A rhetorical device contrasting two opposing ideas in a balanced phrase. Example: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind."

**Colloquial Language**: Informal, everyday language used in conversation. Creates a casual, relatable tone.

**Connotation**: An idea or feeling a word invokes in addition to its literal meaning. "Home" connotes warmth and safety.

**Denotation**: The literal, primary meaning of a word, independent of associated feelings.

**Dialect**: A regional or social variety of a language distinguished by pronunciation, grammar, and vocabulary.

**Euphemism**: A mild or indirect word or expression substituted for one considered too harsh. "Passed away" instead of "died."

**Hyperbole**: Exaggerated statements not meant to be taken literally. Used for emphasis or effect.

**Imagery**: Language that appeals to the senses. Types: visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory, gustatory.

**Inference**: A conclusion drawn from evidence and reasoning rather than explicit statements.

**Irony**: Expression meaning the opposite of the literal meaning. Types: verbal, situational, dramatic.

**Juxtaposition**: Placing two contrasting elements side by side for comparison and contrast.

**Metaphor**: A figure of speech comparing two unlike things without using "like" or "as." "Time is money."

**Motif**: A recurring element (image, symbol, idea) that develops or illuminates a theme.

**Onomatopoeia**: Words that imitate sounds. "Buzz," "sizzle," "crash."

**Oxymoron**: A figure of speech combining contradictory terms. "Jumbo shrimp," "deafening silence."

**Personification**: Attributing human qualities to non-human things. "The wind whispered through the trees."

**Rhetorical Question**: A question asked for effect, not requiring an answer. Engages the reader.

**Simile**: A comparison using "like" or "as." "Brave as a lion," "quick like lightning."

**Symbolism**: The use of symbols to represent ideas or qualities. A dove symbolizes peace.

**Tone**: The attitude of the writer toward the subject or audience. Can be formal, informal, humorous, serious.

## English Literature

**Allegory**: A story with a hidden moral or political meaning. Characters and events represent abstract ideas.

**Archetype**: A universally understood symbol or pattern. The hero, the villain, the mentor.

**Atmosphere**: The mood or feeling created by the writer through setting, imagery, and language.

**Catharsis**: The release of strong emotions through art or literature. Aristotle's concept from tragedy.

**Climax**: The turning point or most intense moment of a narrative.

**Comedy**: A literary work that is amusing and satirical, ending happily.

**Conflict**: A struggle between opposing forces. Types: character vs. character, nature, society, self.

**Denouement**: The resolution of a story's plot after the climax.

**Dramatic Irony**: When the audience knows something a character does not, creating tension.

**Enjambment**: Continuing a sentence beyond the end of a line in poetry. Creates a sense of urgency.

**Foreshadowing**: Hints or clues about what will happen later in the story.

**Free Verse**: Poetry without regular meter or rhyme scheme.

**Genre**: A category of artistic composition characterized by shared conventions. Examples: tragedy, comedy, novel.

**Hubris**: Excessive pride or self-confidence, leading to a character's downfall.

**Imagery**: Vivid descriptive language appealing to the senses, creating mental pictures.

**Juxtaposition**: Placing contrasting elements side by side for effect.

**Literary Device**: Techniques used by writers to convey meaning, create effects, or enhance style.

**Lyric Poetry**: Poetry expressing personal emotions and feelings, often in song-like form.

**Monologue**: A long speech by one character in a play or story.

**Narrative Voice**: The perspective from which a story is told. First person, second person, third person (limited, omniscient).

**Pathetic Fallacy**: Attributing human emotions to nature or weather. Rain reflecting sadness.

**Plot**: The sequence of events in a narrative. Structure: exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution.

**Prose**: Written language in ordinary form, without metrical structure.

**Satire**: The use of humor, irony, or exaggeration to expose and criticize human follies.

**Stanza**: A grouped set of lines in a poem, separated by blank lines.

**Symbolism**: The use of symbols to represent ideas or qualities beyond their literal meaning.

**Theme**: The central idea or underlying meaning of a literary work.

**Tragedy**: A drama dealing with tragic events and unhappy ending, often involving the downfall of a protagonist.

## Mathematics

**Algebra**: The use of letters and symbols to represent numbers and quantities in equations and formulas.

**Angle**: The space between two intersecting lines or surfaces, measured in degrees or radians.

**Area**: The measure of the surface enclosed by a two-dimensional shape. Unit: square units.

**Average**: A single value representing a set of values. Types: mean, median, mode.

**Bearing**: A direction measured in degrees clockwise from north. Expressed as a three-digit number (e.g., 045°).

**Circle Theorems**: Theorems describing relationships between angles, chords, tangents, and arcs in circles.

**Compound Interest**: Interest calculated on the initial principal and accumulated interest. A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt).

**Congruence**: Two shapes are congruent if they have the same shape and size.

**Data Handling**: Collecting, organizing, analyzing, and interpreting data. Includes charts, graphs, and statistics.

**Enlargement**: A transformation that changes the size of a shape but not its proportions. Described by scale factor and center.

**Factorization**: Expressing a number or expression as a product of factors. x² - 5x + 6 = (x-2)(x-3).

**Frequency**: The number of times a value occurs in a dataset.

**Frequency Diagram**: A graphical representation of frequency data. Types: bar chart, histogram, frequency polygon.

**Gradient**: The steepness and direction of a line. Calculated as (change in y) / (change in x).

**Highest Common Factor (HCF)**: The largest number that divides two or more numbers without a remainder.

**Indices**: Powers or exponents. aⁿ means a multiplied by itself n times.

**Inequality**: A mathematical statement comparing two expressions using <, >, ≤, ≥, or ≠.

**Linear Equation**: An equation of the form y = mx + c. Graphs as a straight line.

**Lowest Common Multiple (LCM)**: The smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.

**Mean**: The sum of all values divided by the number of values.

**Median**: The middle value when data is ordered from smallest to largest.

**Mode**: The value that appears most frequently in a dataset.

**Parallel Lines**: Lines in a plane that never meet. Always the same distance apart.

**Perimeter**: The total distance around the edge of a two-dimensional shape.

**Pie Chart**: A circular chart divided into sectors, showing proportions of a whole.

**Probability**: The likelihood of an event occurring, expressed as a number between 0 and 1.

**Pythagorean Theorem**: In a right-angled triangle, a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse.

**Quadratic Equation**: An equation of the form ax² + bx + c = 0. Solved by factoring, completing the square, or formula.

**Ratio**: A comparison of two quantities by division. Written as a:b or a/b.

**Reflection**: A transformation that flips a shape over a line (mirror line).

**Rotation**: A transformation that turns a shape around a fixed point (center of rotation).

**Similarity**: Two shapes are similar if they have the same shape but different sizes. Corresponding angles are equal.

**Standard Deviation**: A measure of how spread out numbers are from the mean.

**Symmetry**: A shape has symmetry if it can be divided into matching parts. Types: line, rotational.

**Transformation**: An operation that moves, changes size, or reflects a shape. Types: translation, rotation, reflection, enlargement.

**Translation**: A transformation that slides a shape without rotating or reflecting it.

**Trigonometry**: The study of relationships between angles and sides of triangles. SOH CAH TOA.

**Volume**: The amount of space occupied by a three-dimensional object. Unit: cubic units.

## Science (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)

**Atom**: The basic unit of a chemical element, consisting of a nucleus (protons and neutrons) and electrons.

**Carbon Cycle**: The biogeochemical cycle by which carbon is exchanged among the biosphere, atmosphere, oceans, and geosphere.

**Cell**: The basic structural and functional unit of all living organisms.

**Chemical Change**: A change resulting in the formation of new substances with different properties.

**Chromosome**: A thread-like structure of DNA and proteins carrying genetic information.

**Condensation**: The change of state from gas to liquid. The reverse of evaporation.

**Control Variable**: A variable kept constant in an experiment to ensure a fair test.

**Density**: The mass per unit volume of a substance. ρ = m/V.

**Diffusion**: The movement of particles from an area of higher concentration to lower concentration.

**Ecosystem**: A community of living organisms interacting with their non-living environment.

**Electromagnetic Spectrum**: The range of all types of electromagnetic radiation, from radio waves to gamma rays.

**Element**: A substance that cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means.

**Enzyme**: A biological catalyst that speeds up chemical reactions in living organisms.

**Evaporation**: The change of state from liquid to gas at the surface below boiling point.

**Food Chain**: A linear sequence of organisms through which nutrients and energy pass.

**Force**: A push or pull acting on an object, measured in newtons (N). F = ma.

**Gene**: A unit of heredity that is transferred from parent to offspring and determines a trait.

**Genetic Variation**: Differences in genes among individuals within a population.

**Habitat**: The natural environment where an organism lives.

**Homeostasis**: The maintenance of a stable internal environment in living organisms.

**Independent Variable**: The variable that is changed or controlled in an experiment to test its effects.

**Inheritance**: The process by which genetic information is passed from parents to offspring.

**Kinetic Energy**: The energy of motion. KE = ½mv².

**Magnetic Field**: The area of force around a magnet where magnetic materials experience a force.

**Mass**: The amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms (kg).

**Metabolism**: The sum of all chemical reactions occurring in a living organism.

**Mixture**: A combination of two or more substances that are not chemically bonded.

**Natural Selection**: The process by which organisms better adapted to their environment tend to survive and reproduce.

**Nucleus**: The central part of an atom containing protons and neutrons. In cells, the control center containing DNA.

**Periodic Table**: A tabular arrangement of chemical elements organized by atomic number and electron configuration.

**Photosynthesis**: The process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy. 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.

**Predator**: An animal that hunts and kills other animals for food.

**Prey**: An animal that is hunted and killed by a predator for food.

**Protein**: A complex organic molecule made of amino acids, essential for growth and repair.

**Respiration**: The process of releasing energy from glucose. Aerobic: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy.

**Solution**: A homogeneous mixture of a solute dissolved in a solvent.

**Variable**: Any factor that can change in an experiment. Types: independent, dependent, controlled.
