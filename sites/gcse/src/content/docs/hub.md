## Why This Guide Exists

The GCSE (General Certificate of Secondary Education) is the standardised examination taken by students in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland at the end of Key Stage 4, typically at age 16. GCSE results shape A-level choices, college admissions, and future career pathways. This hub page links every resource on this site — organised by subject, with study plans and exam strategies to help you prepare efficiently.

Every topic below links to detailed notes, practice questions, flashcards, and diagnostic quizzes. Use this page as your starting point, then drill into the subjects and topics where you need the most work. The content is designed to work across all major exam boards — AQA, Edexcel, and OCR — with board-specific guidance where syllabi diverge.

## Exam Board Alignment

The GCSE content on this site is designed to work across the three main exam boards used in England. While the core concepts are identical, there are differences in emphasis, assessment style, and specific content.

### AQA

AQA is the most widely taken board for GCSE sciences and mathematics. Key characteristics:

- **Science:** 100% exam-based (no coursework). Foundation and Higher tiers. Required practicals assessed through written questions, not practical exams.
- **Mathematics:** Three papers (one calculator, two non-calculator). Emphasis on problem-solving and mathematical reasoning.
- **English:** Paper 1 focuses on creative reading and writing. Paper 2 covers writers' viewpoints and perspectives.
- **Computer Science:** Written exams testing theory and programming concepts. Programming project internally assessed.

### Edexcel

Edexcel (Pearson) is the second most popular board. Key characteristics:

- **Science:** 100% exam-based. Foundation and Higher tiers. Required practicals assessed in written papers.
- **Mathematics:** Three papers (one non-calculator, two calculator). Slightly different question style from AQA — more structured multi-part questions.
- **English:** Similar structure to AQA but with different set texts and assessment objectives.
- **Computer Science:** Theory exams and a programming controlled assessment.

### OCR

OCR (Oxford, Cambridge and RSA) is used by a smaller but significant proportion of schools. Key characteristics:

- **Science:** 100% exam-based. Foundation and Higher tiers. Gateway and Twenty First Century specifications with different emphases.
- **Mathematics:** Four papers (two calculator, two non-calculator). Slightly different topic weighting from AQA and Edexcel.
- **English:** Different set texts and a stronger emphasis on contextual analysis.
- **Computer Science:** Two written papers covering theory and programming concepts.

### How to Use This Site Across Boards

All topic notes on this site cover the common content shared by AQA, Edexcel, and OCR. Where boards diverge significantly, additional notes are provided. Always check your exam board's specification to confirm which topics and sub-topics you need to study.

## Recommended Study Plan

Preparing for GCSEs is a sustained effort that requires consistency over two years. Here is a structured approach that builds understanding progressively.

### Year 10: Building Foundations

- Start with the subject you find hardest — your brain learns best when it is fresh
- Read through all topic notes for each subject, marking areas of uncertainty
- Complete flashcards daily for active recall
- Aim for one diagnostic quiz per subject per week
- Build strong habits: note-taking, summarising, and self-testing

### Year 11 (Autumn): Consolidation

- Revisit weak areas identified by diagnostic quizzes
- Begin practice questions in exam conditions (timed, no notes)
- Cross-reference between subjects — for example, link Physics energy calculations to Maths ratio and proportion
- Start working through past papers
- Attend revision sessions and ask questions on topics you find difficult

### Year 11 (Spring): Exam Readiness

- Focus almost entirely on practice questions and past papers
- Analyse examiner reports to understand common marking criteria
- Do full-length timed papers under real conditions
- Use flashcards to maintain recall on topics you already know well
- Reserve the last two weeks before exams for light revision and rest

### Daily Routine

| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| Morning | Active recall — flashcards for 20 minutes |
| Afternoon | Topic study — read notes on one new topic |
| Evening | Practice — complete 5–10 practice questions |
| Weekend | Diagnostic quiz + review weak areas |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How should I start using this site?

Take a diagnostic quiz for each subject. This takes about 10–15 minutes per subject and gives you a clear picture of your strengths and weaknesses. Then prioritise studying the topics where you scored lowest.

### How many subjects should I study at once?

Most students take 8–10 GCSEs. Study no more than 3–4 subjects per day to maintain focus. Alternate between a science subject, a humanity, English, and mathematics each day.

### Are the practice questions similar to real GCSE exams?

The practice questions are modelled on GCSE-style problems. They test the same concepts and use similar formats, but they are not past papers. Use them alongside official exam board past papers (AQA, Edexcel, OCR) for the best preparation.

### How often should I use the flashcards?

Daily. Flashcards use spaced repetition — each time you get a card right, it appears less frequently. This is the most efficient way to retain factual knowledge. Spend 15–20 minutes each morning.

### What is the difference between topic notes and practice questions?

Topic notes explain concepts, definitions, and worked examples. Practice questions test whether you can apply that knowledge independently. Study the notes first, then test yourself with the questions.

### How do I know which exam board my school uses?

Ask your teacher or check your school's website. The exam board determines which specification your exams follow. While the core content is similar across boards, there are differences in specific topics, assessment style, and grading.

### Can I use this site alongside my school revision?

Absolutely. These notes are designed to complement classroom teaching, not replace it. Use them to fill gaps in your understanding, review topics before exams, and practise applying knowledge to new problems.
