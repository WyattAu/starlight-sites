## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Percentage Change

**Problem:** A house price increases from £120,000 to £135,000. What is the percentage increase?

**Solution:**
Step 1: Find the increase: $£135,000 - £120,000 = £15,000$

Step 2: Divide by the original value: $\frac{15,000}{120,000} = 0.125$

Step 3: Convert to percentage: $0.125 \times 100 = 12.5\%$

**Key insight:** Percentage change always uses the ORIGINAL value as the denominator.

---

### Example 2: Simultaneous Equations

**Problem:** Solve $2x + y = 7$ and $x - y = 2$.

**Solution:**
Step 1: Add the equations: $(2x + y) + (x - y) = 7 + 2$

Step 2: Simplify: $3x = 9 \Rightarrow x = 3$

Step 3: Substitute into equation 2: $3 - y = 2 \Rightarrow y = 1$

Step 4: Check in equation 1: $2(3) + 1 = 7$ ✓

**Key insight:** Adding equations works well when one variable has opposite coefficients (here, $+y$ and $-y$ cancel).

---

### Example 3: Circle Theorems

**Problem:** In a circle, angle ABC = 40° where A, B, C are on the circumference. Find the angle at the centre, AOC.

**Solution:**
Step 1: Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference (both subtended by the same arc AC)

Step 2: $\angle AOC = 2 \times 40° = 80°$

**Key insight:** The angle at the centre is always double the angle at the circumference when both subtend the same arc. This is one of the most tested circle theorems.

### Statistics

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"The five numbers 3, 7, 4, 9, 7 are arranged in ascending order: 3, 4, 7, 7, 9. With five values, the third value is the median: 3, 4, 7, 7, 9, so the median is 7. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle values.
