## Additional Worked Examples

### Example: Energy Transfer in a Pendulum

A pendulum bob of mass 0.2 kg is released from rest at a height of 0.3 m above its lowest point. Calculate its speed at the lowest point.

**Solution:**

Using conservation of energy:
$$mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

$$v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 0.3} = \sqrt{5.88} = 2.42 \text{ m/s}$$

**Exam tip:** Always state the energy stores involved. Here, gravitational potential energy is transferred to kinetic energy.

### Example: Resistance in a Parallel Circuit

Calculate the total resistance of three resistors (6 $\Omega$, 3 $\Omega$, and 2 $\Omega$) connected in parallel.

**Solution:**

For parallel circuits:
$$\frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$$

$$\frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{6} + \frac{2}{6} + \frac{3}{6} = \frac{6}{6} = 1$$

$$R_{total} = 1 \text{ }\Omega$$

**Common mistake:** Adding resistances directly in parallel. The total resistance in parallel is always less than the smallest individual resistance.

### Example: Newton's Second Law on an Incline

A 5 kg box is placed on a smooth (frictionless) inclined plane at 30° to the horizontal. Calculate the acceleration of the box down the incline.

**Solution:**

Component of weight along the incline:
$$F = mg\sin\theta = 5 \times 9.8 \times \sin 30° = 5 \times 9.8 \times 0.5 = 24.5 \text{ N}$$

Using Newton's second law:
$$a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{24.5}{5} = 4.9 \text{ m/s}^2$$

**Exam tip:** On a frictionless incline, $a = g\sin\theta$. This is independent of mass.

### Example: Power in an Electrical Circuit

A 12 V battery is connected to a 4 $\Omega$ resistor and a 6 $\Omega$ resistor in series. Calculate the power dissipated in the 4 $\Omega$ resistor.

**Solution:**

Total resistance: $R_{total} = 4 + 6 = 10 \text{ }\Omega$

Current: $I = \frac{V}{R_{total}} = \frac{12}{10} = 1.2 \text{ A}$

Power in 4 $\Omega$ resistor:
$$P = I^2 R = (1.2)^2 \times 4 = 1.44 \times 4 = 5.76 \text{ W}$$

**Common mistake:** Using $P = V^2/R$ with the total voltage instead of the voltage across the specific resistor.
