## Intuition

Biology is the study of life at every scale — from molecular interactions inside cells to ecosystems spanning entire continents. Cell biology is the foundation: cells are the basic unit of life, and understanding their structure (membrane, organelles, DNA) explains how organisms grow, reproduce, and respond to their environment. Metabolism connects the chemical reactions inside cells to the energy that powers all living processes.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Mitosis vs meiosis confusion**: Mitosis produces two identical diploid cells for growth/repair, while meiosis produces four genetically unique haploid cells for reproduction — mixing up the outcomes is a common exam error.
- **Photosynthesis equation balance**: Forgetting that 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ requires light energy — the equation is not spontaneous.
- **DNA vs RNA**: DNA has deoxyribose sugar and is double-stranded; RNA has ribose sugar and is single-stranded — confusing the two leads to wrong answers about transcription and translation.
- **Active vs passive transport confusion**: Active transport requires ATP and moves against the concentration gradient; passive transport (diffusion, osmosis) requires no energy and moves down the gradient.
- **Enzyme specificity**: Forgetting that enzymes are specific to substrates — the lock-and-key model explains why, and changing pH or temperature denatures the enzyme, destroying specificity.

## Study Tips

- **Draw cell diagrams from memory:** Label the nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, and cell membrane. Visual recall of cell structures is essential for essay questions.
- **Practise genetic cross diagrams:** For genetics questions, draw Punnett squares systematically. Track dominant and recessive alleles carefully to avoid ratio errors.
- **Use past paper mark schemes:** SQA mark schemes reveal the exact wording examiners expect. Match your answers to mark scheme language for maximum marks.
- **Create comparison tables:** For topics like mitosis vs meiosis, DNA vs RNA, or mitosis vs meiosis, create side-by-side comparison tables. Visual organisation aids recall.
- **Link theory to practical applications:** Understanding why photosynthesis requires light energy (it powers the light-dependent reactions) is more useful than memorising the equation alone.
- **Practise essay writing:** Biology exams require structured essays with scientific terminology. Practise writing timed essays on topics like genetics, ecology, and cell biology.
- **Use mnemonics for taxonomic classification:** Remember "King Philip Came Over For Good Soup" (Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species) for the classification hierarchy.
