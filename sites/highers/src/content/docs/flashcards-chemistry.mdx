## Intuition

Chemistry explains how atoms bond to form everything around you — the water in your glass, the steel in a bridge, the DNA in your cells. Atomic structure determines bonding: metals donate electrons (ionic bonding), non-metals share them (covalent bonding), and the resulting structures dictate properties like melting point, conductivity, and solubility. Enthalpy tells you whether a reaction releases energy (exothermic, like combustion) or absorbs it (endothermic, like photosynthesis).

## Common Pitfalls

- **Balancing equations**: Forgetting to balance the entire equation — you cannot change subscripts to balance; only add coefficients in front of formulas.
- **Electron configuration**: Writing the configuration for transition metals incorrectly — remember that 4s fills before 3d, but 3d loses electrons first during ionisation.
- **Organic nomenclature**: Mixing up IUPAC naming rules — the longest chain gets the base name, substituents are listed alphabetically, and numbering starts from the end nearest the first substituent.
- **Mole calculations**: Forgetting to convert between grams, moles, and molecules using the correct molar mass — always write out the conversion factor explicitly.
- **pH vs pOH confusion**: pH = -log[H⁺] and pOH = -log[OH⁻], and pH + pOH = 14 at 25°C — mixing these up leads to wrong answers in acid-base calculations.

## Study Tips

- **Write electron configurations systematically:** Start from 1s, fill each subshell before moving to the next, and mark the valence electrons separately. This prevents errors with transition metals.
- **Practise titration calculations:** Work through acid-base and redox titration problems step by step. Write out the balanced equation first, then use mole ratios.
- **Use molecular model kits:** For organic chemistry and bonding, physical models help visualise 3D structures that are hard to represent on paper.
- **Practise IUPAC naming:** Write out the naming rules and apply them to unfamiliar organic molecules. Speed with nomenclature comes from pattern recognition.
- **Review thermochemistry cycle diagrams:** For enthalpy questions, draw Hess cycles and apply the principle of conservation of energy. This systematic approach prevents sign errors.
- **Practise practical questions:** SQA Chemistry includes practical assessments. Familiarise yourself with common实验 techniques (titration, colorimetry, chromatography) and their error sources.
- **Review periodic table trends:** Understand how atomic radius, ionisation energy, and electronegativity change across periods and down groups. These trends predict chemical behaviour.
- **Practise organic reaction mechanisms:** Draw curly arrow mechanisms for nucleophilic substitution, electrophilic addition, and elimination. Understanding electron movement is key to organic chemistry.
