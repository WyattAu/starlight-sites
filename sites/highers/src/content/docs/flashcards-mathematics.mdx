## Intuition

Higher Mathematics builds on algebra and introduces the language of change (calculus) and the mathematics of shape (trigonometry, vectors). Logarithms are the inverse of exponentiation — they answer the question "what power must I raise this base to in order to get this number?" Calculus splits into two: differentiation finds rates of change (the slope of a curve), while integration accumulates quantities (the area under a curve). The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects these two operations as inverses.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Logarithm rules**: Confusing log(a × b) = log(a) + log(b) with log(a + b) which has no simplification — the product rule is the most commonly misapplied identity.
- **Chain rule omission**: When differentiating composite functions, forgetting to multiply by the derivative of the inner function — this is the single most common calculus error.
- **Recurrence relation assumptions**: Assuming a recurrence relation always converges — check the characteristic equation roots to determine behaviour (convergent, divergent, or oscillating).
- **Vector dot product sign errors**: Forgetting that a·b = |a||b|cosθ — if θ > 90°, the dot product is negative. Always check the angle before assuming the sign.
- **Integration by parts formula confusion**: Mixing up ∫u dv = uv − ∫v du — choose u and dv strategically so that the resulting integral is simpler than the original.

## Study Tips

- **Practise logarithm simplification:** Write out the log rules on a reference card and apply them to complex expressions. Speed with logarithm manipulation comes from pattern recognition.
- **Verify calculus answers graphically:** After differentiating or integrating, sketch the original and result to check they make geometric sense.
- **Use past papers extensively:** SQA Higher Maths papers follow consistent patterns. Practising under timed conditions builds familiarity with the question style and time management.
- **Master the binomial expansion:** Practise expanding (a+b)^n for various values of n, including negative and fractional indices. This technique appears across many topics.
- **Practise vector operations:** Dot product, cross product, and vector equations of lines are fundamental. Sketch diagrams to visualise 3D vector problems.
- **Review trigonometric identities:** Know the compound angle formulae, double angle formulae, and harmonic form identities by heart. These appear frequently in exam questions.
- **Practise recurrence relations:** Write out the first few terms, find the characteristic equation, and determine the general solution. Check convergence by examining the roots.
- **Master integration techniques:** Practise substitution, integration by parts, and partial fractions. Recognising which technique to apply is a key exam skill.
