## Intuition

Higher Mathematics builds on algebra and introduces the language of change (calculus) and the mathematics of shape (trigonometry, vectors). Logarithms are the inverse of exponentiation — they answer the question "what power must I raise this base to in order to get this number?" Calculus splits into two: differentiation finds rates of change (the slope of a curve), while integration accumulates quantities (the area under a curve). The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects these two operations as inverses.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Logarithm rules**: Confusing log(a × b) = log(a) + log(b) with log(a + b) which has no simplification — the product rule is the most commonly misapplied identity.
- **Chain rule omission**: When differentiating composite functions, forgetting to multiply by the derivative of the inner function — this is the single most common calculus error.
- **Recurrence relation assumptions**: Assuming a recurrence relation always converges — check the characteristic equation roots to determine behaviour (convergent, divergent, or oscillating).
