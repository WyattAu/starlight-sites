## Intuition

Physics describes how the universe works through mathematical laws. Mechanics tells you how objects move under forces (F = ma), while dynamics extends this to rotating and oscillating systems. Electricity flows through circuits governed by Ohm's law (V = IR), and waves transfer energy without transferring matter — from sound to light. Particles at the subatomic level behave nothing like everyday objects, introducing quantum behaviour and nuclear physics.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Unit inconsistencies**: Mixing SI units (metres, seconds, kilograms) with non-SI units (cm, ms, g) without converting — always convert to SI before applying formulas.
- **Free body diagrams**: Forgetting to include all forces (especially friction and weight) or drawing forces in the wrong direction — a systematic approach prevents omissions.
- **Wave equation sign errors**: Using v = fλ without checking that frequency (Hz), wavelength (m), and speed (m/s) are in compatible units — frequency in kHz or wavelength in nm must be converted first.
- **Energy conservation mistakes**: Forgetting that kinetic energy is ½mv² (not mv) or that gravitational potential energy depends on height difference, not absolute height.
- **Circuit analysis errors**: Confusing series and parallel combinations — in series, current is the same and voltages add; in parallel, voltage is the same and currents add.

## Study Tips

- **Draw complete free body diagrams:** For every mechanics problem, identify and label all forces before writing equations. Missing friction or weight is the most common error.
- **Practise unit conversions explicitly:** Write out the conversion factor (e.g., 1 km = 1000 m) rather than doing it mentally. This prevents arithmetic errors under exam pressure.
- **Use the SQA data sheet:** Familiarise yourself with the formulae and constants provided in the exam. Knowing what is available saves time and prevents memorisation errors.
- **Practise wave diagrams:** For wave questions, draw displacement-distance and displacement-time graphs. Understanding the relationship between wavelength, frequency, and speed is essential.
- **Review circuit diagrams systematically:** For circuit problems, label all known values, identify series/parallel combinations, and apply Ohm's law step by step.
- **Practise working with vectors:** Resolve vectors into components, calculate magnitude and direction, and apply vector addition. This is essential for mechanics and waves.
- **Review energy conservation principles:** Understand the work-energy theorem, conservation of mechanical energy, and energy dissipation. Apply these to mechanics problems systematically.
