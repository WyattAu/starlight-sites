## Qualifications and Structure

**Scottish Highers**: The qualifications taken by students in Scotland, typically in S5, used for university admissions across the UK and internationally.

**Advanced Higher**: Taken in S6, covering university-level content in more depth. Closer to first-year university material.

**National 5**: The qualification taken in S4, equivalent to GCSEs in England. A prerequisite for Highers.

**SQA (Scottish Qualifications Authority)**: The body that designs, administers, and certifies Scottish qualifications.

**S5**: The fifth year of secondary school, when most students sit their Highers.

**S6**: The sixth year of secondary school, typically for Advanced Highers and university preparation.

**SCQF (Scottish Credit and Qualifications Framework)**: The national framework for defining qualification levels in Scotland. Highers are SCQF Level 6.

**Credit Level**: The SCQF level at which qualifications are classified, determining the level of difficulty and depth.

**Unit-Based Assessment**: Highers include unit assessments alongside the final exam, providing ongoing evidence of learning.

**Course Code**: A unique identifier assigned to each Higher subject by the SQA for registration and exam purposes.

## Assessment and Exams

**SQA Exam**: The final examination set and marked by the SQA, typically held in May.

**Coursework**: Assessed work completed during the course, contributing to the final grade.

**Marking Instructions**: The SQA's official guide showing how marks are awarded for each question.

**Grade Boundaries**: The minimum marks required for each grade (A, B, C, D), determined after each exam session.

**Grade A**: The highest grade, typically requiring 70%+ of available marks.

**Grade B**: Typically requiring 60–69% of available marks.

**Grade C**: Typically requiring 50–59% of available marks.

**Grade D**: The minimum pass grade, typically requiring 40–49% of available marks.

**No Award**: Below the minimum pass threshold, indicating the candidate did not achieve a grade.

**Band Descriptors**: Detailed descriptions of what students must demonstrate for each grade in each subject.

**Structured Questions**: Questions with sub-parts that guide you through a problem step by step.

**Extended Response**: Longer questions requiring detailed explanations, evaluations, or arguments.

**Data Analysis**: Interpreting and drawing conclusions from presented data such as graphs, tables, and experimental results.

## Biology Terms

**Cell Biology**: The study of cell structure, organelles, and membrane transport.

**Metabolism**: All chemical reactions in living organisms, including enzyme-catalysed reactions.

**Enzymes**: Biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions without being consumed.

**Homeostasis**: The maintenance of a stable internal environment in organisms.

**DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)**: The molecule carrying genetic instructions for development and function.

**Genetics**: The study of heredity and variation in organisms.

**Natural Selection**: The process by which organisms with favourable traits survive and reproduce.

**Evolution**: Change in the inherited characteristics of populations over successive generations.

**Respiration**: The process of releasing energy from glucose, either aerobically or anaerobically.

**Photosynthesis**: The process by which plants convert light energy into chemical energy (glucose).

**Multicellular Organisms**: Organisms composed of many cells organised into tissues, organs, and systems.

## Chemistry Terms

**Structure and Bonding**: The study of ionic, covalent, and metallic bonding and intermolecular forces.

**Ionic Bonding**: The electrostatic attraction between positive and negative ions.

**Covalent Bonding**: The sharing of electron pairs between atoms.

**Metallic Bonding**: The attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons.

**Intermolecular Forces**: Forces between molecules, including van der Waals and hydrogen bonding.

**Enthalpy**: The heat content of a chemical system at constant pressure.

**Kinetics**: The study of reaction rates and the factors affecting them.

**Equilibrium**: A state where forward and reverse reaction rates are equal.

**pH**: A measure of the acidity or alkalinity of a solution.

**Organic Chemistry**: The study of carbon-containing compounds and their reactions.

**Analytical Techniques**: Methods for identifying and quantifying substances, such as chromatography, spectroscopy, and mass spectrometry.

## Computer Science Terms

**Hardware**: The physical components of a computer system.

**Software**: The programs and data that run on computer hardware.

**Algorithm**: A step-by-step procedure for solving a problem.

**Pseudocode**: An informal, human-readable description of an algorithm.

**Database**: An organised collection of structured data.

**SQL (Structured Query Language)**: The language used to manage and query relational databases.

**Network**: A collection of computers connected to share resources.

**Operating System**: Software managing computer hardware and providing services for programs.

**CPU (Central Processing Unit)**: The processor that executes instructions.

**RAM (Random Access Memory)**: Temporary storage for data and programs in use.

## Mathematics Terms

**Polynomial**: An expression with variables and coefficients combined using addition, subtraction, and multiplication.

**Surd**: An irrational number expressed as a root, such as √2.

**Indices (Exponents)**: Rules for working with powers: multiplication, division, power of a power.

**Trigonometric Functions**: Sine, cosine, and tangent, relating angles to side ratios in right-angled triangles.

**Calculus**: The study of rates of change (differentiation) and accumulation (integration).

**Differentiation**: Finding the rate of change of a function with respect to a variable.

**Integration**: Finding the area under a curve or the antiderivative of a function.

**Vectors**: Quantities with both magnitude and direction.

**Scalar Product**: The dot product of two vectors, resulting in a scalar.

**Normal Distribution**: A bell-shaped probability distribution used in statistics.

## Physics Terms

**Kinematics**: The study of motion without considering forces.

**Dynamics**: The study of forces and their effect on motion.

**Newton's Laws**: Three laws describing the relationship between forces and motion.

**Momentum**: The product of mass and velocity, conserved in collisions.

**Work**: Energy transferred when a force moves an object through a distance.

**Power**: The rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

**Electromagnetic Spectrum**: The range of all electromagnetic radiation, from radio waves to gamma rays.

**Quantum Phenomena**: Behaviour of matter and energy at atomic and subatomic scales.

**Wave Properties**: Wavelength, frequency, amplitude, and speed of waves.

**Radioactivity**: The spontaneous emission of radiation from unstable atomic nuclei.

## University Admissions

**UCAS (Universities and Colleges Admissions Service)**: The UK-wide system for applying to university.

**Personal Statement**: A written account of your academic interests, motivation, and preparation for your chosen course.

**Predicted Grades**: Grades estimated by teachers for students in their final year, used in UCAS applications.

**Higher Offer**: A university offer based on achieving specific Higher grades.

**Advanced Higher Offer**: Offers requiring specific Advanced Higher grades, often for competitive courses.

**UCAS Tariff Points**: Points assigned to qualifications for university entry requirements.

**Adjustment**: A period after results day when students with better-than-expected grades can apply to more competitive courses.

**Clearing**: The UCAS process for finding available places at universities after results day.

**Extra**: The UCAS process for applying to courses that still have vacancies after the main application cycle.

**SQA Results Day**: The day in August when SQA examination results are released, a key date for university admissions.

## Study Strategies

**Active Recall**: Actively retrieving information from memory, such as through flashcards or practice questions, rather than passively reading notes.

**Spaced Repetition**: Reviewing material at increasing intervals to strengthen long-term retention.

**Past Papers**: Official SQA exams from previous years, the single most important revision resource.

**Timed Practice**: Completing practice questions under exam conditions with a time limit.

**Diagnostic Quiz**: A short test to identify strengths and weaknesses across a subject's topics.

**Revision Notes**: Concise summaries of key concepts for quick review.

**Study Timetable**: A planned schedule allocating specific time blocks to each subject.

**Flashcards**: Study cards for memorizing key facts, formulas, and definitions.
