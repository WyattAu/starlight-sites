# Reading

HSC english study notes - Reading

## Key Concepts

### Close Reading Techniques

**Annotation:** Mark key ideas, vocabulary, and literary devices while reading.

**SQ3R Method:**
- Survey: Skim the text for structure and main ideas
- Question: Form questions about the content
- Read: Read actively to answer your questions
- Recite: Summarise key points in your own words
- Review: Revisit and reflect on the text

### Essay Structure

**Introduction:** Hook, context, thesis statement

**Body paragraphs:** Topic sentence, evidence, analysis, link to thesis

**Conclusion:** Restate thesis, synthesise key ideas, broader significance

### Literary Devices

**Techniques:** Metaphor, simile, personification, imagery, symbolism

**Language features:** Tone, diction, syntax, voice

**Structure:** Chronological, thematic, episodic, frame narrative

### Analytical Framework

**Context:** Historical, cultural, biographical factors

**Text:** Language, structure, form, style

**Reader:** Interpretation, response, personal connection

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Essay Introduction

**Problem:** Write an introduction for an essay on the theme of identity in a novel.

**Solution:**

"Identity is not a fixed entity but a fluid construct shaped by societal expectations, personal experiences, and cultural heritage. In [Author]'s [Title], the protagonist's journey of self-discovery reveals the tension between individual desire and collective belonging. Through a careful examination of character development and narrative structure, this essay argues that the author presents identity as an ongoing negotiation rather than a predetermined destination."

**Answer:** See the introduction above

### Example 2: Analysing a Quote

**Problem:** Analyse the following quote from a novel: "She wore her scars like armour, each one a testament to battles fought and survived."

**Solution:**

Step 1: Identify the technique: Extended metaphor (scars as armour)

Step 2: Explain the literal meaning: The character's physical or emotional scars are visible

Step 3: Explain the figurative meaning: The metaphor suggests that suffering has been transformed into strength; the character uses past trauma as protection

Step 4: Connect to theme: This reflects the broader theme of resilience and the idea that pain can become a source of power

**Answer:** The extended metaphor of "scars as armour" transforms the character's suffering into a symbol of strength, suggesting that past trauma has been repurposed as emotional protection.

### Example 3: Thesis Statement

**Problem:** Write a thesis statement for an essay on how power is represented in a Shakespeare play.

**Solution:**

"In [Play], Shakespeare interrogates the nature of power by demonstrating that its acquisition inevitably leads to moral corruption. Through the trajectories of [Character A] and [Character B], Shakespeare suggests that power is not merely a political commodity but a force that fundamentally transforms identity."

**Answer:** See thesis above

## Exam Tips

1. Always ground your analysis in specific textual evidence
2. Use the PEEL structure: Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link
3. Consider multiple interpretations rather than a single "correct" reading
4. Manage your time: allocate roughly 40 minutes per essay in the HSC exam
5. Proofread for spelling, grammar, and clarity
6. Use present tense when discussing texts (the author "argues," not "argued")

## Practice Problems

1. Analyse the opening paragraph of a novel of your choice using the close reading techniques
2. Write a thesis statement for an essay on a theme in a text you have studied
3. Practise the SQ3R method on a non-fiction article

## Why This Matters

Reading comprehension and analytical writing are foundational skills across all subjects. The ability to close read a text, identify literary techniques, and construct a well-supported argument transfers to every discipline in the HSC and beyond.
