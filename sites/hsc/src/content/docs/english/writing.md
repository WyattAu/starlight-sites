# Writing

HSC english study notes - Writing

## Key Concepts

### Creative Writing

**Narrative voice:** First person, third person limited, third person omniscient

**Setting:** Time, place, atmosphere; how setting reflects character or theme

**Characterisation:** Direct description, dialogue, action, inner thoughts

**Show don't tell:** Use sensory details and actions rather than stating emotions

### Persuasive Writing

**Argument structure:** Claim, evidence, reasoning, counterargument, rebuttal

**Rhetorical devices:** Rhetorical questions, repetition, inclusive language, appeals to ethos/pathos/logos

**Tone:** Formal, authoritative, empathetic, urgent

### Short Answer Techniques

**Technique identification:** State the technique, provide evidence, explain the effect

**Formula:** "The author uses [technique] in [quote] to [effect], which [significance]"

### Essay Writing

**Introduction:** Context + thesis + roadmap

**Body:** Topic sentence + evidence + analysis + link

**Conclusion:** Restate thesis + synthesise + wider significance

## Worked Examples

### Example 1: Creative Writing Opening

**Problem:** Write the opening of a short story that immediately engages the reader.

**Solution:**

"The last time I saw my father, he was standing in the rain without an umbrella. I was twelve, and I thought he was waiting for something. Now I know he was leaving."

**Answer:** The opening uses retrospective narration, concrete imagery, and a twist in the final sentence to create intrigue.

### Example 2: Short Answer on Technique

**Problem:** How does the author use metaphor to explore the theme of memory?

**Solution:**

"The author employs the extended metaphor of 'fading photographs' to illustrate the gradual erosion of memory. In the line 'her face blurred at the edges like a photograph left in the sun,' the comparison suggests that memory is not lost suddenly but deteriorates over time. This metaphor reinforces the theme that memory is fragile and impermanent, evoking a sense of loss and nostalgia."

### Example 3: Essay Body Paragraph

**Problem:** Write a body paragraph on how a character develops throughout a novel.

**Solution:**

"At the outset of the novel, the protagonist is characterised by passivity and reluctance, as evidenced by her refusal to intervene when confronted with injustice. However, by the novel's climax, she has undergone a fundamental transformation. The pivotal scene in which she confronts her antagonist demonstrates her newfound agency: 'For the first time, she did not look away.' This moment marks a turning point in her development, as the author uses direct speech and decisive action to convey her shift from spectator to participant. This transformation underscores the novel's broader argument that courage is not the absence of fear but the decision to act despite it."

## Exam Tips

1. In creative writing, establish voice and tone early
2. Use specific, concrete details rather than vague descriptions
3. In analytical writing, always link technique to theme
4. Manage your time: spend no more than 40 minutes per essay
5. Leave 5 minutes at the end to proofread
6. Read your work aloud to check for flow and clarity

## Practice Problems

1. Write a 500-word creative piece that begins with the line "The door was unlocked"
2. Write a short answer response analysing the use of symbolism in a text you have studied
3. Write an essay introduction for a question on a text you have studied
