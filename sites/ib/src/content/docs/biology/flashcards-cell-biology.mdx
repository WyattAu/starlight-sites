### Key Concepts

**Cell Theory**: All living things are made of cells; cells are the basic unit of life; all cells arise from pre-existing cells. This is the foundation of modern biology and was established by Schleiden, Schwann, and Virchow in the 19th century.

**Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells**: Prokaryotes (bacteria) have no membrane-bound nucleus or organelles, circular DNA, and are smaller (0.1-5 μm). Eukaryotes have a true nucleus, membrane-bound organelles (mitochondria, ER, Golgi), linear DNA, and are larger (10-100 μm).

**Fluid Mosaic Model**: The cell membrane is a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins (integral and peripheral), cholesterol, and carbohydrates. The "fluid" refers to the ability of lipids and proteins to move laterally. This structure allows selective permeability.

**Membrane Transport**: Passive transport (no energy required) includes diffusion, osmosis, and facilitated diffusion. Active transport requires energy (ATP) to move substances against the concentration gradient. Endocytosis and exocytosis move large molecules in bulk.

## Intuition

Think of a cell as a factory. The nucleus is the CEO's office (directs operations), mitochondria are the power stations (generate ATP), the ER is the production line, the Golgi is the packaging department, and the cell membrane is the security gate (controls what enters and leaves). Prokaryotic cells are like small start-up workshops -- efficient but limited. Eukaryotic cells are large factories with specialised departments.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing active and passive transport. Passive = no energy, moves down the gradient. Active = requires energy (ATP), moves against the gradient. The type of transport determines what the cell can and cannot do.
- Assuming all eukaryotic cells have the same organelles. Plant cells have chloroplasts and large central vacuoles; animal cells have centrioles and lysosomes. Red blood cells lack a nucleus entirely.
- Forgetting that the fluid mosaic model describes structure, not just function. The "mosaic" of proteins in the lipid bilayer is what allows selective permeability -- without the proteins, the membrane would be a poor barrier.
