### Key Concepts

**Functional Groups and Homologous Series**: A functional group determines the chemical properties of an organic molecule. A homologous series is a family of compounds with the same functional group but differing by CH₂ units. Examples: alkanes (C-C single bonds), alkenes (C=C double bonds), alcohols (-OH), carboxylic acids (-COOH).

**Reaction Mechanisms**: Organic reactions proceed via specific mechanisms. Nucleophilic substitution involves a nucleophile attacking an electron-deficient carbon. Electrophilic addition involves an electrophile adding across a double bond. Elimination removes atoms to form a double bond. Free radical substitution involves initiation, propagation, and termination steps.

**Isomerism**: Structural isomers have the same molecular formula but different bonding arrangements (chain, position, functional group). Stereoisomers have the same structural formula but different spatial arrangements. Optical isomers are non-superimposable mirror images (chiral centres with 4 different groups).

**Analytical Techniques**: IR spectroscopy identifies functional groups by their characteristic absorption wavelengths. Mass spectrometry provides molecular mass and fragmentation patterns. NMR spectroscopy reveals the carbon-hydrogen framework. ¹H NMR shows the number and environment of hydrogen atoms.

## Intuition

Think of organic chemistry as LEGO. Functional groups are the special bricks (wheels, windows, connectors) that give each piece its function. Homologous series are like building bigger and bigger structures by adding the same brick repeatedly. Reaction mechanisms are the instructions for how pieces connect or disconnect. Analytical techniques are like X-ray vision -- they let you see what's inside without taking the structure apart.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing nucleophiles (electron-pair donors, attracted to positive centres) with electrophiles (electron-pair acceptors, attracted to negative centres). Remember: nucleophiles are "nucleus-loving" (attracted to positive); electrophiles are "electron-loving" (attracted to negative).
- Forgetting that optical isomers require a chiral centre (carbon with 4 different groups). Not all molecules with C=C bonds or ring structures are chiral.
- Misidentifying functional groups in IR spectra. The O-H stretch (broad, 2500-3300 cm⁻¹) and C=O stretch (sharp, ~1700 cm⁻¹) are the most distinctive. Always check for multiple characteristic absorptions before identifying a compound.
