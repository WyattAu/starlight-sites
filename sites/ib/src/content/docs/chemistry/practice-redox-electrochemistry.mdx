<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"In the reaction: MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O, which species is the reducing agent?&quot; options={['Fe²⁺', 'MnO₄⁻', 'H⁺', 'Fe³⁺']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;The reducing agent is the species that gets oxidised (loses electrons). Mn in MnO₄⁻ goes from +7 to +2 (reduction, gains 5e⁻). Fe²⁺ goes to Fe³⁺ (oxidation, loses 1e⁻). Since Fe²⁺ causes the reduction of MnO₄⁻ by donating electrons, Fe²⁺ is the reducing agent. Conversely, MnO₄⁻ is the oxidising agent (it gets reduced)."} difficulty="easy"  />

### Electrochemistry

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"A voltaic cell is constructed with Zn|Zn²⁺ (1.0 mol dm⁻³) and Cu|Cu²⁺ (1.0 mol dm⁻³). Given E°(Zn²⁺/Zn) = -0.76 V and E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V, what is the standard cell potential?&quot; options={['+1.10 V', '-1.10 V', '+0.42 V', '+0.76 V']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;In a voltaic cell, the more negative E° value represents the anode (oxidation). Zn²⁺/Zn has the more negative E° (-0.76 V), so Zn is oxidised: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻. Cu²⁺/Cu has the more positive E° (+0.34 V), so Cu²⁺ is reduced: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. E°cell = E°(cathode) - E°(anode) = +0.34 - (-0.76) = +0.34 + 0.76 = +1.10 V. The positive value confirms the cell reaction is spontaneous."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"In the electrolysis of molten NaCl using inert electrodes, what products form at the anode and cathode respectively?&quot; options={[ 'Chlorine gas at the anode and sodium metal at the cathode', 'Hydrogen gas at the cathode and chlorine gas at the anode', 'Oxygen gas at the anode and sodium hydroxide at the cathode', 'Sodium ions at the anode and chloride ions at the cathode', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;In molten NaCl electrolysis (not aqueous), the only ions present are Na⁺ and Cl⁻. At the cathode (negative electrode): Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na(l) (sodium metal is produced). At the anode (positive electrode): 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂(g) + 2e⁻ (chlorine gas is produced). In aqueous NaCl, H₂ would be produced at the cathode instead (H⁺ is preferentially reduced over Na⁺ in water), but since the electrolyte is molten, Na metal forms. The key distinction is molten vs aqueous."} difficulty="medium"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"How long (in minutes) must a current of 2.50 A be passed through molten Al₂O₃ to deposit 5.40 g of aluminium? (M_Al = 27.0, F = 96500 C mol⁻¹)&quot; options={['386 min', '193 min', '77.2 min', '64.3 min']} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;The half-equation is: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al. Three moles of electrons are required per mole of Al. Moles of Al = 5.40/27.0 = 0.200 mol. Total charge required: Q = 3 × F × n(Al) = 3 × 96500 × 0.200 = 57900 C. Using Q = It: t = Q/I = 57900/2.50 = 23160 s. Converting to minutes: 23160/60 = 386 min."} difficulty="hard"  />

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"What is the function of the salt bridge in a voltaic cell?&quot; options={[ 'It maintains electrical neutrality by allowing ions to flow between the two half-cells, completing the circuit.', 'It provides electrons to flow from the anode to the cathode.', 'It prevents the reaction from occurring by keeping the solutions separate.', 'It increases the cell potential by adding extra ions to the solutions.', ]} correctAnswer={0} explanation=&quot;The salt bridge contains an inert electrolyte (such as KNO₃ or KCl) in a gel. As the voltaic cell operates, positive charge builds up in the anode half-cell (as metal ions enter solution) and negative charge builds up in the cathode half-cell (as cations are removed from solution). Without the salt bridge, this charge imbalance would quickly stop the reaction. The salt bridge allows anions to flow to the anode half-cell and cations to the cathode half-cell, maintaining electrical neutrality so the cell can continue to operate. Electrons flow through the external wire, not through the salt bridge."} difficulty="medium"  />
