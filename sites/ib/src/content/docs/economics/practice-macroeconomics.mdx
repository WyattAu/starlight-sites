## Intuition

For macroeconomics questions, always identify which policy tool is being discussed (fiscal, monetary, or supply-side) and which objective it targets (growth, inflation, unemployment, or balance of payments). Remember that policies have trade-offs -- stimulating growth may increase inflation; reducing inflation may increase unemployment. Use AD-AS analysis to show the mechanism connecting policy to outcome.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing fiscal and monetary policy. Fiscal = government spending and taxation (Parliament/Congress). Monetary = interest rates and money supply (Central Bank). They are controlled by different institutions and have different time lags.
- Assuming unemployment and inflation always move in opposite directions. The Phillips curve relationship has broken down in many economies (stagflation). Supply shocks can cause both simultaneously.
- Forgetting the time lag problem. Fiscal policy has implementation lags (Parliament must pass laws). Monetary policy has impact lags (it takes time for interest rate changes to affect spending). Supply-side policies have even longer lags (education reforms take years to show results).
