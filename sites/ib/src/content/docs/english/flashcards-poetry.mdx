### Key Concepts

**Figurative Language**: Metaphor compares two things directly ("the world is a stage"). Simile compares using "like" or "as" ("brave as a lion"). Personification gives human qualities to non-human things ("the wind whispered"). Hyperbole uses exaggeration for effect ("I've told you a million times").

**Sound Devices**: Alliteration repeats initial consonant sounds ("Peter Piper picked"). Assonance repeats vowel sounds ("go slow"). Consonance repeats consonant sounds at the end of words ("luck and lark"). Onomatopoeia words imitate sounds ("buzz", "crash").

**Structural Terms**: Stanza is a group of lines (like a paragraph in prose). Enjambment continues a sentence across line breaks. Caesura is a pause within a line (often marked by punctuation). Refrain is a repeated line or stanza, often at the end of a verse.

**Imagery**: Language that appeals to the senses -- visual (sight), auditory (sound), tactile (touch), olfactory (smell), gustatory (taste). Effective imagery creates vivid mental pictures and emotional responses in the reader.

## Intuition

Think of poetry as music made with words. Sound devices are the instruments -- alliteration is the drumbeat, assonance is the melody, consonance is the bass line. Figurative language is the paint -- metaphors and similes add colour and depth. Structure is the rhythm and timing -- stanzas are the verses, enjambment creates flow, caesura creates pauses. Together, they create a multi-sensory experience.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing metaphor and simile. Both make comparisons, but similes use "like" or "as" while metaphors state something IS something else. "Her eyes are diamonds" (metaphor) vs "Her eyes sparkle like diamonds" (simile).
- Assuming alliteration only matters at the start of words. It's the repetition of initial consonant SOUNDS, not just letters. "Unique unicorn" is not alliteration (different initial sounds); "cat kite" is alliteration (same initial /k/ sound).
- Forgetting to link poetic techniques to effect. IB exams require you to explain WHY a technique is used, not just identify it. Always ask: what does this technique show, suggest, or emphasise?
