### Key Concepts

**Water Balance** describes the relationship between precipitation, evapotranspiration, runoff, and groundwater flow in a drainage basin. When precipitation exceeds evapotranspiration, there is a water surplus; when evapotranspiration exceeds precipitation, there is a deficit. The balance varies seasonally.

**Hydrographs** show how river discharge responds to rainfall over time. Key features include the lag time (delay between peak rainfall and peak discharge), peak discharge, and base flow. Urbanisation increases peak discharge and reduces lag time due to impermeable surfaces.

**Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM)** is a process that coordinates the development and management of water, land, and related resources to maximise economic and social welfare without compromising environmental sustainability. It involves stakeholders at all levels.

**Transboundary Water Conflicts**: Many major rivers cross international borders (e.g., Nile, Mekong, Jordan). Upstream damming or water extraction can reduce flow downstream, creating tensions. Treaties and water-sharing agreements are needed but difficult to enforce.

## Intuition

A drainage basin is like a bathtub with holes. Rain is the tap filling it. Evapotranspiration is water evaporating back up. Runoff is water spilling over the rim. Groundwater flow is water slowly leaking through the bottom. A hydrograph tells the story of what happens after a rainstorm -- how quickly the "bathtub" fills and empties. Urbanisation is like replacing the bathtub's porous surface with plastic -- water runs off immediately instead of soaking in.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing lag time with response time. Lag time is specifically the delay between peak rainfall and peak discharge; response time is a broader term for how quickly the river responds overall.
- Forgetting that evapotranspiration includes both evaporation from surfaces and transpiration from plants. Students often only consider evaporation and underestimate water loss.
- Assuming water scarcity only occurs in dry climates. It can also result from over-extraction, pollution, or poor infrastructure in areas with adequate rainfall.
