### Key Concepts

**Urbanisation Trends**: Over 55% of the world's population now lives in urban areas, projected to reach 68% by 2050. Developing countries are urbanising fastest, often through rural-urban migration driven by economic opportunity and conflict. Megacities (10M+ population) are predominantly in the Global South.

**Urban Microclimates**: Cities are typically 2-5°C warmer than surrounding rural areas (urban heat island effect) due to concrete and asphalt absorbing heat, reduced vegetation, waste heat from vehicles and buildings, and canyon effects trapping heat between tall buildings.

**Smart Cities** use digital technology, data, and IoT sensors to improve urban services, reduce resource consumption, and enhance quality of life. Examples include smart grids, intelligent transport systems, and data-driven waste management.

**Sustainable Urban Development** integrates environmental, social, and economic goals. Strategies include green infrastructure, mixed-use zoning, public transit investment, affordable housing, and community engagement. The UN-Habitat New Urban Agenda provides a global framework.

## Intuition

A city is like a living organism. The urban heat island is its fever -- the built environment traps heat just as a body traps warmth during illness. Smart cities are the nervous system, using sensors (nerve endings) and data (signals) to respond to problems in real time. Sustainable development is about keeping the organism healthy long-term rather than just treating symptoms.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing urbanisation with urban growth. Urbanisation is the proportion of people living in cities; urban growth is the absolute increase in urban population. A city can grow without the country urbanising if rural populations also grow.
- Assuming all megacities are in developed countries. The majority are in Asia, Africa, and Latin America, where rapid urbanisation often outpaces infrastructure development.
- Thinking the urban heat island effect is only about temperature. It also affects air quality (trapped pollutants), energy demand (more cooling needed), and human health (heat-related illness).
