### Key Concepts

**Urbanisation Trends**: Over 55% of the world's population now lives in urban areas, projected to reach 68% by 2050. Developing countries are urbanising fastest, often through rural-urban migration driven by economic opportunity and conflict. Megacities (10M+ population) are predominantly in the Global South (Tokyo, Delhi, Shanghai, São Paulo, Cairo). Rapid urbanisation often outpaces infrastructure development, creating informal settlements (slums) housing over 1 billion people.

**Urban Microclimates**: Cities are in standard practice 2-5°C warmer than surrounding rural areas (urban heat island effect) due to concrete and asphalt absorbing heat, reduced vegetation (less evapotranspiration), waste heat from vehicles and buildings, and canyon effects trapping heat between tall buildings. The heat island effect increases energy demand (more air conditioning), worsens air quality (ground-level ozone formation), and increases heat-related mortality. Green infrastructure (parks, green roofs, street trees) can mitigate the effect.

**Smart Cities** use digital technology, data, and IoT sensors to improve urban services, reduce resource consumption, and enhance quality of life. Examples include smart grids (optimising energy distribution), intelligent transport systems (reducing congestion), data-driven waste management, and environmental monitoring. Smart city initiatives raise privacy concerns about surveillance and data collection. They require significant investment and digital literacy.

**Sustainable Urban Development** integrates environmental, social, and economic goals. Strategies include green infrastructure (parks, green roofs, permeable surfaces), mixed-use zoning (reducing commuting), public transit investment (reducing car dependency), affordable housing (preventing gentrification), and community engagement (participatory planning). The UN-Habitat New Urban Agenda provides a global framework for sustainable urbanisation.

## Intuition

A city is like a living organism. The urban heat island is its fever -- the built environment traps heat just as a body traps warmth during illness. Smart cities are the nervous system, using sensors (nerve endings) and data (signals) to respond to problems in real time. Sustainable development is about keeping the organism healthy long-term rather than just treating symptoms. Informal settlements are like scar tissue -- they form where the body has been damaged and are functional but not ideal.

**Why it matters**: Urbanisation is the defining demographic trend of the 21st century. How cities are designed and managed determines quality of life for billions of people. Understanding urban environments is essential for addressing climate change (cities produce 70% of global CO₂), inequality (urban poverty), and sustainability (resource consumption).

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing urbanisation with urban growth. Urbanisation is the proportion of people living in cities; urban growth is the absolute increase in urban population. A city can grow without the country urbanising if rural populations also grow.
- Assuming all megacities are in developed countries. The majority are in Asia, Africa, and Latin America, where rapid urbanisation often outpaces infrastructure development. Tokyo is the exception, not the rule.
- Thinking the urban heat island effect is only about temperature. It also affects air quality (trapped pollutants form smog), energy demand (more cooling needed), human health (heat-related illness), and water quality (warmer runoff affects aquatic ecosystems).
- Confusing urban sprawl with suburbanisation. Sprawl is unplanned, low-density expansion at city edges (car-dependent, inefficient land use). Suburbanisation is the movement of people from inner cities to suburbs -- it can be planned (garden cities) or unplanned (informal settlements).
