## Intuition

For climate change questions, always consider both the physical science (causes, mechanisms) and the human geography (impacts, responses). Case studies are essential -- use specific examples like the Sahel drought, Bangladesh flooding, or Pacific island nations to illustrate points. When evaluating responses, consider effectiveness, equity, and feasibility.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing climate with weather. Climate is the long-term average pattern; weather is the short-term state of the atmosphere. Climate change refers to shifts in long-term averages, not individual weather events.
- Assuming climate change impacts are evenly distributed. Developing countries, small island states, and vulnerable communities are disproportionately affected despite contributing least to emissions.
- Forgetting to evaluate both mitigation and adaptation in essay responses. A complete answer addresses reducing causes AND managing effects.
