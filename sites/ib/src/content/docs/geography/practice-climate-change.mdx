## Intuition

For climate change questions, always consider both the physical science (causes, mechanisms) and the human geography (impacts, responses). Case studies are essential -- use specific examples like the Sahel drought, Bangladesh flooding, or Pacific island nations to illustrate points. When evaluating responses, consider effectiveness, equity, and feasibility. The IB rewards well-evaluated answers that consider multiple perspectives -- not just describing what happened, but explaining why it happened and whether the response was adequate.

**Why it matters**: Climate change is the most significant global environmental challenge. Understanding its causes, impacts, and responses is essential for informed citizenship and for careers in environmental science, policy, and engineering. These practice questions test your ability to apply climate change knowledge to exam scenarios.

## Worked Example

**Question**: Evaluate the effectiveness of the Paris Agreement in addressing climate change.

**Answer Structure**: Describe the Paris Agreement goals (limit warming to well below 2°C, aim for 1.5°C, NDCs). Evaluate strengths: universal participation (195 countries), legally binding framework, recognition of common but differentiated responsibilities. Evaluate limitations: NDCs are voluntary and insufficient (current pledges lead to ~2.7°C warming), no enforcement mechanism, developed countries haven't met $100 billion/year finance commitment. Conclude that the Agreement provides a framework but lacks the ambition and enforcement needed to meet its goals.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing climate with weather. Climate is the long-term average pattern (30+ years); weather is the short-term state of the atmosphere (days to weeks). Climate change refers to shifts in long-term averages, not individual weather events. A cold winter does not disprove climate change.
- Assuming climate change impacts are evenly distributed. Developing countries, small island states, and vulnerable communities are disproportionately affected despite contributing least to emissions. This raises issues of climate justice and equity.
- Forgetting to evaluate both mitigation and adaptation in essay responses. A complete answer addresses reducing causes AND managing effects. Neither alone is sufficient -- some warming is already locked in (adaptation needed), but without mitigation, impacts will be catastrophic.
- Not using specific case studies. The IB rewards detailed, specific examples. Instead of saying "flooding will increase," say "Bangladesh, where 17% of the land could be inundated by 2050, affecting 20 million people."
