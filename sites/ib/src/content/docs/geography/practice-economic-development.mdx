## Intuition

When answering development questions, always use specific country examples to support your arguments. Compare countries at different development stages to show patterns. Remember that development is multidimensional -- economic indicators alone don't tell the full story. Consider historical context, colonial legacies, and global trade structures. The IB rewards evaluative answers that consider multiple perspectives and use specific evidence to support arguments.

**Why it matters**: Understanding economic development is essential for addressing global inequality, poverty, and environmental degradation. It helps you evaluate aid effectiveness, trade policies, and government strategies. Development geography connects economic, social, and environmental issues in a holistic framework. These practice questions test your ability to apply development concepts to exam scenarios. The ability to compare different development theories and apply them to real-world case studies is a core skill assessed in IB Geography examinations.

## Worked Example

**Question**: Compare Rostow's model of development with dependency theory, using examples to support your answer.

**Answer Structure**: Describe Rostow's five stages (traditional, preconditions, take-off, drive to maturity, high mass consumption) -- it assumes all countries follow the same linear path. Describe dependency theory (core-periphery exploitation, unequal trade). Compare: Rostow is optimistic (all countries can develop) while dependency theory is pessimistic (the system is rigged). Evaluate: Rostow is Eurocentric (based on Western development) and ignores colonial legacies; dependency theory explains why resource-rich countries remain poor but doesn't provide clear solutions. Use examples: South Korea followed Rostow's model (state-led industrialisation); many African countries fit dependency theory (resource extraction benefits the core).

## Common Mistakes

- Treating GDP per capita as a perfect measure of development. Always mention its limitations (ignores inequality, informal economy, environmental costs, unpaid work). Use HDI or MPI as complementary measures.
- Assuming all countries follow the same development path. Different political, cultural, and geographic contexts lead to different trajectories. Rostow's model is Eurocentric and doesn't account for dependency or colonial legacies.
- Forgetting to consider both internal and external factors in development. Domestic policies matter, but so do global trade rules, aid, colonial history, and multinational corporation behaviour. A complete answer considers both.
- Confusing modernisation theory with dependency theory. Modernisation theory argues countries develop through stages (internal factors); dependency theory argues underdevelopment is caused by exploitation by wealthy nations (external factors). Both have strengths and limitations.
