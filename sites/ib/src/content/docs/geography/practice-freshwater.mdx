## Intuition

For freshwater questions, always link physical processes to human impacts. A hydrograph question isn't just about reading the graph -- explain what causes the shape (urbanisation, deforestation, soil type). For water scarcity, distinguish between physical scarcity (arid climate) and economic scarcity (lack of infrastructure). Use case studies like the Murray-Darling Basin (Australia), Aral Sea (Central Asia), or Great Man-Made River (Libya) to illustrate points. The IB rewards answers that connect physical processes to human consequences and evaluate management strategies.

**Why it matters**: Freshwater is essential for life, agriculture, industry, and ecosystems. Understanding water management is essential for addressing water scarcity, flooding, pollution, and transboundary conflicts. These issues affect billions of people and will be exacerbated by climate change and population growth. These practice questions test your ability to apply freshwater knowledge to exam scenarios.

## Worked Example

**Question**: Using a hydrograph, explain how urbanisation affects river discharge.

**Answer Structure**: Describe what a hydrograph shows (discharge over time). Explain that urbanisation increases impermeable surfaces (concrete, asphalt), which reduces infiltration and groundwater recharge. This means more water reaches the river as surface runoff, which is faster than groundwater flow. The result: peak discharge increases (higher flood risk), lag time decreases (water reaches the river faster), and base flow may decrease (less groundwater to sustain flow between storms). Use a specific example: the 2007 UK floods were partly attributed to urbanisation in the Thames catchment.

---

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing surface runoff with groundwater flow. Surface runoff moves quickly over the land surface (hours to days); groundwater flow moves slowly through aquifers (months to years). Both contribute to river discharge but at very different timescales.
- Forgetting that impermeable surfaces (concrete, asphalt) increase surface runoff and reduce groundwater recharge, leading to both flooding and water table depletion. Urbanisation fundamentally alters the water balance.
- Assuming water management is purely a technical problem. Social, political, and economic factors (water pricing, governance, corruption, cultural values) are equally important. The Aral Sea disaster was caused by political decisions, not technical failures.
- Confusing water scarcity with water stress. Water stress occurs when water demand exceeds available supply; water scarcity is a more severe condition where availability threatens health, livelihoods, and ecosystems.
