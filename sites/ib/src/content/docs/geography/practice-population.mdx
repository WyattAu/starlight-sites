## Intuition

For population questions, always consider both the numerical data and the human stories behind it. Population pyramids reveal not just current structure but future implications -- a wide base today means a youth bulge in 15 years, which could be a demographic dividend or a source of instability. When discussing migration, always consider both push and pull factors and the impacts on both origin and destination.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing crude birth rate (per 1000 people) with total fertility rate (average children per woman). They measure different things and are not interchangeable.
- Assuming all population growth is natural increase. Migration can significantly affect population change, especially at the national level for countries with large immigrant or emigrant populations.
- Forgetting that population policies have unintended consequences. China's one-child policy reduced birth rates but created a gender imbalance and an ageing population. India's sterilisation programme damaged trust in government healthcare.
