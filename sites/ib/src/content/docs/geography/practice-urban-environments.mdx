## Intuition

For urban environment questions, think about the trade-offs between economic growth and environmental sustainability. Urban sprawl increases car dependency and pollution but provides affordable housing. Densification reduces sprawl but can create overcrowding. Smart cities offer efficiency but raise privacy concerns. There are no perfect solutions -- only context-dependent compromises. The IB rewards evaluative answers that consider multiple stakeholders and weigh benefits against costs.

**Why it matters**: Urbanisation is the defining demographic trend of the 21st century. How cities are designed and managed determines quality of life for billions of people. Understanding urban environments is essential for addressing climate change, inequality, and sustainability. These practice questions test your ability to apply urban geography knowledge to exam scenarios. The ability to evaluate competing urban management strategies and consider multiple stakeholder perspectives is a core skill assessed in IB Geography examinations.

## Worked Example

**Question**: Evaluate the view that smart cities are the best solution to urban environmental problems.

**Answer Structure**: Describe smart cities (digital technology, IoT sensors, data-driven management). Evaluate benefits: optimised energy use (smart grids reduce waste), reduced congestion (intelligent transport), improved waste management, better environmental monitoring. Evaluate limitations: high implementation costs (digital divide), privacy concerns (surveillance), requires digital literacy, may not address root causes (poverty, inequality). Conclude: smart cities offer useful tools but are not sufficient alone -- they must be combined with social policies, affordable housing, and community engagement to address urban environmental problems comprehensively.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing urban sprawl with suburbanisation. Sprawl is unplanned, low-density expansion at city edges (car-dependent, inefficient land use). Suburbanisation is the movement of people from inner cities to suburbs -- it can be planned (garden cities) or unplanned (informal settlements).
- Assuming all informal settlements (slums) are temporary. Many have existed for decades and have developed their own social structures, economies, and governance. Upgrading (providing services, land tenure) is often more effective than demolition and relocation.
- Forgetting to consider both positive and negative impacts of urbanisation. Urbanisation drives economic growth, innovation, and cultural exchange, but also creates pollution, inequality, and strain on services. A balanced answer considers both.
- Confusing gentrification with urban regeneration. Gentrification is the influx of wealthier residents into poorer areas, often displacing existing communities. Urban regeneration is broader and can include community-led improvement without displacement.
