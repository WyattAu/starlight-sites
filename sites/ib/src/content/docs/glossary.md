## IB Programme Structure

**Core**: The central component of the IB Diploma Programme, consisting of Theory of Knowledge (TOK), Extended Essay (EE), and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS).

**Diploma Programme (DP)**: A two-year educational programme for students aged 16-19, preparing them for university and life beyond.

**Group 1: Studies in Language and Literature**: The first subject group focusing on literature, language, and literary studies in the student's best language.

**Group 2: Language Acquisition**: The second subject group focusing on learning a second or additional language.

**Group 3: Individuals and Societies**: The fourth subject group including subjects like Economics, History, Psychology, and Business Management.

**Group 4: Sciences**: The subject group including Biology, Chemistry, Physics, and Computer Science.

**Group 5: Mathematics**: The subject group offering Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches and Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation.

**Group 6: The Arts**: The subject group including Visual Arts, Music, Theatre, Film, and Dance.

**Higher Level (HL)**: A course taken at a more advanced level, with greater depth and breadth of content. Typically 240 teaching hours.

**Standard Level (SL)**: A course taken at a standard level, with core content covered in less depth. Typically 150 teaching hours.

## Assessment

**CAS (Creativity, Activity, Service)**: A core requirement involving creative pursuits, physical activity, and community service over 18 months.

**Criterion-Referenced Assessment**: Assessment based on predetermined criteria rather than comparing students to each other.

**External Assessment**: Assessment conducted and marked outside the classroom, including examinations and written coursework.

**Grade Boundary**: The minimum marks required to achieve each grade (1-7) for each subject.

**Internal Assessment (IA)**: Assessment conducted within the school and marked by the teacher, then moderated externally.

**Moderation**: The process of checking teacher-marked internal assessments for consistency and accuracy.

**Oral Assessment**: An examination component testing spoken language skills or subject-specific oral presentation.

**Predicted Grade**: An estimate of the grade a student is expected to achieve, submitted by the school.

**Reflective Project**: A core CAS requirement where students reflect on their growth and learning throughout the programme.

**Tok Exhibition**: A component of TOK where students explore how TOK concepts manifest in the real world.

**Tok Essay**: A 1,600-word essay on a prescribed title, exploring knowledge questions across different areas of knowledge.

**Weighting**: The percentage contribution of each assessment component to the final grade.

## Theory of Knowledge (TOK)

**Areas of Knowledge (AOK)**: Different fields of human knowledge, including mathematics, natural sciences, human sciences, history, the arts, ethics, religious knowledge systems, and indigenous knowledge systems.

**Knowledge Framework**: A set of eight elements (scope, methodology, perspective, ethics, language, linkages, assumptions, responsibility) for analyzing knowledge.

**Knowledge Question (KQ)**: An open-ended question about knowledge itself, rather than about the world. Central to TOK inquiry.

**Knowledge Claim**: A statement asserting something to be true, which can be analyzed using TOK concepts.

**Knowledge Practitioner**: Someone who produces knowledge in a particular field or area of knowledge.

**Real-Life Situation (RLS)**: A specific event or scenario that illustrates TOK concepts and knowledge questions.

**Ways of Knowing (WOK)**: Methods used to acquire knowledge: language, sense perception, emotion, reason, imagination, faith, intuition, memory.

**Tokenism**: The superficial inclusion of diverse perspectives without genuine engagement. Should be avoided in TOK analysis.

## Extended Essay (EE)

**Annotated Bibliography**: A list of sources with brief descriptions of their content and relevance to the research question.

**Bibliography**: A list of all sources cited in the essay, formatted according to a recognized citation style.

**Criterion A: Focus and Method**: The clarity and appropriateness of the research question and methodology.

**Criterion B: Knowledge and Understanding**: The depth of understanding of the topic and relevant concepts.

**Criterion C: Critical Thinking**: The ability to analyze, evaluate, and synthesize information.

**Criterion D: Presentation**: The clarity, coherence, and formatting of the essay.

**Criterion E: Engagement**: The student's intellectual and creative engagement with the research process.

**Research Question**: A clear, focused question that the essay investigates. Central to the essay's structure.

**Supervisor**: A teacher who guides and supports the student throughout the Extended Essay process.

**Viva Voce**: A concluding interview with the supervisor, reflecting on the research process and learning.

## Subject-Specific Terms

**Analysis and Approaches (AA)**: A mathematics course focusing on algebraic, trigonometric, and calculus methods. More theoretical.

**Applications and Interpretation (AI)**: A mathematics course focusing on practical applications, statistics, and modeling. More applied.

**Biology HL/SL**: The study of living organisms, their structure, function, growth, evolution, and classification.

**Business Management**: The study of how organizations operate, including management, marketing, finance, and strategy.

**Chemistry HL/SL**: The study of matter, its properties, composition, structure, and the changes it undergoes.

**Computer Science**: The study of computational thinking, programming, algorithms, and the societal impact of technology.

**Design Technology**: The study of the design process, materials, manufacturing, and the impact of technology on society.

**Economics**: The study of how societies allocate scarce resources, covering microeconomics and macroeconomics.

**Film**: The study of film as an art form and medium of communication, including analysis and production.

**Geography**: The study of the Earth's physical features, human populations, and the interactions between humans and their environment.

**Global Politics**: The study of political issues, systems, and interactions at local, national, and international levels.

**History**: The study of past events, their causes, consequences, and significance in shaping the present.

**Philosophy**: The study of fundamental questions about existence, knowledge, values, reason, and mind.

**Physics HL/SL**: The study of matter, energy, and the fundamental forces of nature.

**Psychology**: The study of human behavior and mental processes, including biological, cognitive, and sociocultural approaches.

**Social and Cultural Anthropology**: The study of human societies and cultures across the world.

**Spanish AB Initio**: A beginner-level Spanish course for students with little or no prior experience.

**Theatre**: The study and practice of theatre arts, including performance, design, and theory.

**Visual Arts**: The study and creation of visual works, including painting, sculpture, photography, and digital art.

## IB Learner Profile

**Caring**: Showing empathy, compassion, and respect. Committing to service and making a positive difference.

**Communicator**: Expressing ideas confidently and creatively in multiple languages and modes. Collaborating effectively.

**Inquirer**: Developing curiosity and the skills to conduct inquiry-based learning. Loving the process of discovery.

**Knowledgeable**: Exploring concepts, ideas, and issues with depth and breadth. Connecting learning across disciplines.

**Manager**: Approaching learning with focus and determination. Organizing time and workload effectively.

**Open-Minded**: Understanding and appreciating their own cultures and personal histories. Open to perspectives, values, and traditions.

**Principled**: Acting with integrity, honesty, and a strong sense of fairness and justice. Taking responsibility for actions.

**Reflective**: Thoughtfully considering the world and their own ideas. Assessing their understanding and growth.

**Risk-Taker**: Approaching uncertainty with forethought and determination. Exploring new ideas and strategies.

**Thinker**: Using critical and creative thinking skills to analyze and take responsible action on complex problems.
