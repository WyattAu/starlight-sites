## IB Diploma Structure

The IB Diploma Programme consists of **six subject groups** plus three core requirements:

1. **Studies in Language and Literature** — first language
2. **Language Acquisition** — second language
3. **Individuals and Societies** — humanities and social sciences
4. **Sciences** — experimental sciences
5. **Mathematics** — mathematics courses
6. **The Arts** — visual arts, music, theatre (or another subject from groups 1-4)

### Core Requirements

- **Extended Essay (EE)** — 4,000-word independent research essay
- **Theory of Knowledge (TOK)** — essay and presentation on knowledge questions
- **Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS)** — 18 months of creative, physical, and service activities

### Assessment

- Grades: 1 (lowest) to 7 (highest) per subject
- Maximum: 42 points (6 subjects × 7)
- TOK/EE: up to 3 bonus points
- Total: 45 points maximum
- Pass threshold: 24 points with no grade below 3

## How to Use These Notes

1. **Start with the syllabus** — read the IBO curriculum document for each subject to understand what is and is not assessable
2. **Focus on command terms** — IB uses specific language (describe, explain, evaluate, compare) that determines answer depth
3. **Practice past papers** — IB publishes past papers with marking schemes — use them extensively
4. **Use the points to remember** — each topic page highlights key definitions and formulas
5. **Cross-reference with subject guides** — the IBO subject guides define the exact content and assessment criteria

## Study Strategy

- **Understand the assessment model** — IB uses both internal assessments (IA) and external examinations
- **Plan your IA early** — internal assessments require independent investigation with strict criteria
- **Master the command terms** — "analyse" requires more depth than "describe"; know the difference
- **Use the marking schemes** — past paper marking schemes reveal exactly what examiners award marks for
- **Time management** — IB exams are lengthy; practise under timed conditions

## Subject-Level Advice

- **Biology**: Draw and label diagrams frequently. The extended response questions require precise terminology.
- **Chemistry**: Master the mole concept early — it underpins stoichiometry, equilibrium, and electrochemistry.
- **Physics**: Practise mathematical derivations. IB Physics requires showing working, not just answers.
- **Mathematics**: Focus on understanding concepts, not memorising procedures. The new curriculum emphasises modelling.
- **Economics**: Use real-world examples in essays. IB rewards application of theory to current events.
- **Computer Science**: Implement data structures from pseudocode. The exam tests tracing and debugging.
