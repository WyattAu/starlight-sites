## Intuition

**The IB Diploma is about connections — between subjects, between theory and practice, and between cultures:** Unlike A-Levels which specialise, the IB requires breadth across sciences, humanities, and arts. This isn't just about taking more subjects — it's about seeing how they connect. Physics explains how bridges stand; Economics explains who pays for them; History explains why they were built; Language explores how they're described.

**Why it matters:** The IB develops well-rounded thinkers who can draw connections across disciplines. This holistic approach is valued by universities worldwide because it produces graduates who can think broadly, communicate evidently, and engage with complex, real-world problems.

**The key insight:** The IB's Theory of Knowledge (TOK) course asks "how do we know what we know?" — not just what we know. This epistemological focus is the IB's unique contribution: it teaches you to question assumptions, evaluate evidence, and recognise the limits of knowledge. This is the foundation of intellectual maturity.

## How to Use These Notes

1. **Start with the fundamentals** -- build a solid foundation before moving to advanced topics
2. **Work through examples** -- follow along with the worked examples to build intuition
3. **Test yourself** -- use the practice problems and flashcards to check your understanding
4. **Review regularly** -- spaced repetition helps retain what you have learned

## Common Mistakes

**Focusing only on content and ignoring command terms:** IB exams test understanding through specific command terms (explain, discuss, evaluate). Learn what each term requires.

**Not practicing with past papers:** IB marking schemes are specific. Practice with official past papers to understand what examiners expect.

**Underestimating the Internal Assessment:** The IA is worth 20-30% of your grade. Start early, follow the criteria, and seek feedback.
