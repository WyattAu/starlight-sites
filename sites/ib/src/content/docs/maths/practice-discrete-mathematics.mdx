### Logic

**Q9. Which of the following is the contrapositive of 'If it rains, then I carry an umbrella'?**

A. If I carry an umbrella, then it rains
B. If it does not rain, then I do not carry an umbrella
C. If I do not carry an umbrella, then it does not rain
D. If I do not carry an umbrella, then it rains

<details>
<summary>Show answer — C</summary>

**Answer: C** — The contrapositive of 'If P then Q' is 'If not Q then not P'. Here: If I do not carry an umbrella (not Q), then it does not rain (not P).

</details>

**Q10. According to De Morgan\\"s laws, ¬(P ∧ Q) is equivalent to:**

A. ¬P ∧ ¬Q
B. P ∨ Q
C. ¬(P ∨ Q)
D. ¬P ∨ ¬Q

<details>
<summary>Show answer — D</summary>

**Answer: D** — De Morgan's first law: ¬(P ∧ Q) ≡ ¬P ∨ ¬Q. The negation of a conjunction is the disjunction of the negations."

</details>

**Q11. Construct a truth table for (P → Q) ∧ (Q → P). When is this statement true?**

A. Always true
B. Only when P is true
C. Only when Q is true
D. Only when P and Q have the same truth value

<details>
<summary>Show answer — D</summary>

**Answer: D** — (P → Q) ∧ (Q → P) is the biconditional P ↔ Q. It is true only when P and Q have the same truth value (both true or both false)."

</details>

**Q12. Prove by contradiction that there is no largest prime number.**

A. Show that all integers greater than 1 are composite
B. Use induction to show infinitely many primes exist
C. Assume all numbers are prime and derive a contradiction
D. Assume a largest prime p exists, then p! + 1 is either prime or divisible by a prime larger than p — contradiction

<details>
<summary>Show answer — D</summary>

**Answer: D** — Assume p is the largest prime. Consider N = p! + 1. N is not divisible by any prime ≤ p (it leaves remainder 1). So N is either prime itself or divisible by a prime > p. Either way, a prime larger than p exists — contradiction."

</details>

**Q13. Which logical connective is represented by the truth table: TT→T, TF→F, FT→T, FF→T?**

A. Conjunction (P ∧ Q)
B. Disjunction (P ∨ Q)
C. The conditional (P → Q)
D. Biconditional (P ↔ Q)

<details>
<summary>Show answer — C</summary>

**Answer: C** — The conditional P → Q is false only when P is true and Q is false (TF→F). In all other cases it is true. This matches: TT→T, TF→F, FT→T, FF→T."

</details>

### Combinatorics

**Q14. How many ways can 5 people be arranged in a line?**

A. 25
B. 60
C. 720
D. 120

<details>
<summary>Show answer — D</summary>

**Answer: D** — 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120."

</details>

**Q15. How many 3-person committees can be formed from 10 people?**

A. 720
B. 30
C. 120
D. 1000

<details>
<summary>Show answer — C</summary>

**Answer: C** — This is a combination: C(10,3) = 10!/(3! × 7!) = (10 × 9 × 8)/(3 × 2 × 1) = 120."

</details>

**Q16. A password consists of 4 distinct digits chosen from \{0,1,2,...,9\}. How many possible passwords are there?**

A. 10000
B. 5040
C. 210
D. 720

<details>
<summary>Show answer — B</summary>

**Answer: B** — Permutation of 4 from 10: ¹⁰P₄ = 10 × 9 × 8 × 7 = 5040."

</details>

**Q17. How many ways can the letters of the word 'BANANA' be arranged?**

A. 60
B. 720
C. 120
D. 360

<details>
<summary>Show answer — A</summary>

**Answer: A** — Total letters = 6, with 3 A's and 2 N's. Arrangements = 6!/(3! × 2!) = 720/12 = 60."

</details>

**Q18. By the pigeonhole principle, in any group of 13 people, at least how many share the same birth month?**

A. 1
B. 3
C. 2
D. 13

<details>
<summary>Show answer — C</summary>

**Answer: C** — There are 12 months (pigeonholes) and 13 people (pigeons). By the pigeonhole principle, at least ⌈13/12⌉ = 2 people share the same birth month."

</details>

**Q19. Using inclusion-exclusion, in a class of 40 students: 25 study Maths, 20 study Physics, 10 study both. How many study neither?**

A. 5
B. 10
C. 15
D. 0

<details>
<summary>Show answer — A</summary>

**Answer: A** — |M ∪ P| = |M| + |P| − |M ∩ P| = 25 + 20 − 10 = 35. Neither = 40 − 35 = 5."

</details>

**Q20. How many 4-digit numbers can be formed from \{1,2,3,4,5,6\} if digits can be repeated?**

A. 1296
B. 360
C. 720
D. 15

<details>
<summary>Show answer — A</summary>

**Answer: A** — Each of the 4 positions can be filled in 6 ways (with repetition). Total = 6⁴ = 1296."

</details>

**Q21. How many bit strings of length 8 contain exactly five 1s?**

A. 8
B. 128
C. 56
D. 336

<details>
<summary>Show answer — C</summary>

**Answer: C** — Choose 5 positions out of 8 for the 1s: C(8,5) = C(8,3) = 8!/(3!5!) = (8×7×6)/(3×2×1) = 56."

</details>

### Number Theory

**Q22. Find the GCD of 48 and 36.**

A. 6
B. 24
C. 4
D. 12

<details>
<summary>Show answer — D</summary>

**Answer: D** — Using the Euclidean algorithm: 48 = 1(36) + 12. 36 = 3(12) + 0. So GCD(48,36) = 12."

</details>

**Q23. Find the prime factorisation of 360.**

A. 2² × 3³ × 5
B. 2⁴ × 3² × 5
C. 2³ × 3 × 5²
D. 2³ × 3² × 5

<details>
<summary>Show answer — D</summary>

**Answer: D** — 360 = 2 × 180 = 2 × 2 × 90 = 2² × 2 × 45 = 2³ × 9 × 5 = 2³ × 3² × 5."

</details>

**Q24. Find 17 mod 5.**

A. 2
B. 3
C. 17
D. 5

<details>
<summary>Show answer — A</summary>

**Answer: A** — 17 = 3 × 5 + 2, so 17 mod 5 = 2."

</details>

**Q25. Use the Euclidean algorithm to find GCD(252, 105).**

A. 21
B. 7
C. 42
D. 3

<details>
<summary>Show answer — A</summary>

**Answer: A** — 252 = 2(105) + 42. 105 = 2(42) + 21. 42 = 2(21) + 0. GCD = 21."

</details>

**Q26. Find the LCM of 12 and 18 using their prime factorisations.**

A. 6
B. 36
C. 72
D. 216

<details>
<summary>Show answer — B</summary>

**Answer: B** — 12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3². LCM = 2² × 3² = 4 × 9 = 36."

</details>

**Q27. Which of the following numbers is divisible by 9?**

A. 736
B. 734
C. 738
D. 737

<details>
<summary>Show answer — C</summary>

**Answer: C** — A number is divisible by 9 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 9. 7+3+8 = 18 (divisible by 9). 7+3+6 = 16, 7+3+4 = 14, 7+3+7 = 17. Only 738 works."

</details>

**Q28. Find the multiplicative inverse of 7 modulo 11.**

A. 5
B. 8
C. 3
D. 2

<details>
<summary>Show answer — B</summary>

**Answer: B** — We need x such that 7x ≡ 1 (mod 11). Try: 7(8) = 56 = 5(11) + 1, so 7(8) ≡ 1 (mod 11). The inverse is 8."

</details>

**Q29. Using Fermat's Little Theorem, find 3¹⁰ mod 11.**

A. 3
B. 10
C. 1
D. 0

<details>
<summary>Show answer — C</summary>

**Answer: C** — Fermat's Little Theorem: if p is prime and gcd(a,p) = 1, then a^(p−1) ≡ 1 (mod p). Here a = 3, p = 11. So 3¹⁰ ≡ 1 (mod 11)."

</details>
