### Key Concepts

**Ohm's Law** states that the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it, provided temperature remains constant. Written as V = IR, this relationship forms the foundation of circuit analysis. The gradient of a V-I graph for an ohmic conductor gives resistance.

**Kirchhoff's Junction Rule** (KCL) states that the total current entering a junction equals the total current leaving it. This is a consequence of conservation of charge -- charge cannot accumulate at a point in a circuit.

**Power Dissipation** in a circuit element is given by P = IV = I²R = V²/R. Choosing the right form depends on what quantities are known. For example, using P = I²R is useful when current is constant (series circuits), while P = V²/R is better when voltage is constant (parallel circuits).

**Internal Resistance** means every real battery has some resistance inside it. The terminal PD is less than the EMF by an amount equal to Ir, where I is the current and r is the internal resistance. At maximum power transfer, the external resistance equals the internal resistance.

## Intuition

Think of voltage as the "pressure" pushing charge through a pipe (the wire), and resistance as the pipe's narrowness. A wider pipe (lower resistance) lets more water (current) flow for the same pressure. Internal resistance is like a kink inside the battery itself -- some of the battery's own pressure is used up pushing charge through that internal kink before it even reaches the external circuit.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing EMF with terminal PD: EMF is the total energy per unit charge the source provides, while terminal PD is what's actually available to the external circuit. They differ by Ir.
- Forgetting that ammeters are placed in series and voltmeters in parallel -- reversing them gives incorrect readings and can damage the meter.
- Assuming V = IR applies to all components. It only works for ohmic conductors; diodes and filament lamps have non-linear I-V characteristics.
