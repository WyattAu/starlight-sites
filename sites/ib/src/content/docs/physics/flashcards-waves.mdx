### Key Concepts

**Wave Equation**: v = fλ, where v is wave speed, f is frequency, and λ is wavelength. Frequency is determined by the source and does not change when a wave enters a different medium. Wavelength and speed change together to maintain this relationship. Transverse waves have oscillations perpendicular to the direction of travel (light, water waves); longitudinal waves have oscillations parallel to the direction of travel (sound, compression springs). The period T = 1/f is the time for one complete oscillation.

**Snell's Law** governs refraction: n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂. When light enters a denser medium (higher n), it bends toward the normal. Total internal reflection occurs when light travels from a denser to a less dense medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, where sinθc = n₂/n₁. Refractive index n = c/v, where c is the speed of light in vacuum and v is the speed in the medium. Fibre optics use total internal reflection to transmit light over long distances.

**Superposition Principle** states that when two or more waves overlap, the resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements. Constructive interference occurs when waves are in phase (crest meets crest); destructive interference occurs when they are out of phase by half a wavelength (crest meets trough). Path difference determines interference type: constructive when path difference = nλ; destructive when path difference = (n + ½)λ.

**Standing Waves** form when two identical waves travel in opposite directions. Nodes (zero displacement) and antinodes (maximum displacement) are fixed in position. For a string fixed at both ends, the fundamental frequency has wavelength λ = 2L, where L is the string length. Harmonics occur at integer multiples of the fundamental frequency. Standing waves are responsible for musical tones in instruments and resonance phenomena.

## Intuition

Think of waves as ripples on a pond. When two sets of ripples meet, they directly add together -- where two crests coincide you get a bigger crest (constructive), and where a crest meets a trough they cancel (destructive). Frequency is like the rhythm of your hand tapping the water: it stays the same no matter how the ripples spread. For Snell's law, imagine a line of soldiers marching from pavement into mud at an angle: the ones hitting the mud first slow down, causing the line to pivot toward the normal. Standing waves are like a jump rope held at both ends -- the rope oscillates but the ends stay fixed.

**Why it matters**: Waves are everywhere -- light, sound, radio signals, earthquakes, and ocean waves. Understanding wave behaviour is essential for telecommunications, medical imaging (ultrasound, MRI), music, and understanding the nature of light itself.

## Common Pitfalls

- Thinking frequency changes during refraction. Only wavelength and speed change; the source sets the frequency and it stays constant. This is a very common exam trap -- always remember that frequency is the "invariant" quantity.
- Confusing diffraction with refraction: diffraction is the spreading of waves around an obstacle or through a gap; refraction is the bending of waves due to a change in speed between media. Diffraction depends on gap size relative to wavelength; refraction depends on refractive index difference.
- Forgetting that the Doppler effect formula Δf/f = v/c uses the relative speed between source and observer, and the sign depends on whether they are approaching (positive, higher frequency) or receding (negative, lower frequency).
- Assuming that all waves need a medium. Electromagnetic waves (light, radio, X-rays) travel through vacuum. Mechanical waves (sound, water) require a medium.
