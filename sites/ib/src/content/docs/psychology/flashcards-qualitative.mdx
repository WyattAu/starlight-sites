### Key Concepts

**Thematic Analysis**: A method for identifying, analysing, and reporting patterns (themes) within qualitative data. Braun and Clarke's six-phase approach involves familiarisation, coding, theme generation, theme review, theme definition, and report writing. It is flexible and can be used across different theoretical frameworks.

**Content Analysis**: A systematic method for quantifying qualitative data by counting the frequency of themes, words, or concepts. It can be manifest (counting explicit content) or latent (interpreting underlying meaning). It bridges qualitative and quantitative approaches.

**Grounded Theory**: An inductive approach where theory emerges from data rather than being imposed on it. Data collection and analysis occur simultaneously through theoretical sampling and constant comparison. The goal is to generate theory that is "grounded" in participants' experiences.

**Credibility and Trustworthiness**: Qualitative research must demonstrate credibility (do findings reflect participants' experiences?), transferability (can findings apply to other contexts?), dependability (is the process consistent?), and confirmability (are findings based on data, not researcher bias?).

## Intuition

Think of qualitative research as painting a portrait rather than taking a photograph. A photograph (quantitative) captures a precise snapshot; a portrait (qualitative) captures the essence, mood, and character. Thematic analysis is like identifying the recurring colours and shapes in a painting. Grounded theory is like letting the painting tell you what it's about rather than deciding in advance.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing thematic analysis with content analysis. Thematic analysis identifies patterns of meaning; content analysis counts occurrences of explicit content. They serve different purposes and have different strengths.
- Assuming qualitative research is "less scientific" than quantitative. Qualitative research follows rigorous procedures and produces valuable knowledge that quantitative methods cannot capture -- understanding meaning, experience, and context.
- Forgetting about researcher bias in qualitative studies. The researcher is the instrument, so reflexivity (acknowledging how their perspective influences the research) is essential for credibility.
