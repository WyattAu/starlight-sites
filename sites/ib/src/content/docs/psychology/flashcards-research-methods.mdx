### Key Concepts

**Experimental Design**: True experiments manipulate the IV and measure the DV while controlling extraneous variables. Between-groups designs use different participants for each condition; within-subjects designs use the same participants in all conditions (counterbalancing reduces order effects). Matched-pairs designs pair participants on relevant variables.

**Sampling Methods**: Random sampling gives every member an equal chance (reduces bias but may not be representative). Stratified sampling ensures subgroups are proportionally represented. Opportunity sampling is convenient but biased. Volunteer sampling suffers from self-selection bias.

**Reliability and Validity**: Reliability is consistency of results (test-retest reliability measures stability; inter-rater reliability measures agreement between observers). Internal validity is whether the study measures what it claims (free from confounding variables). External validity is generalisability to other settings, times, and populations.

**Ethical Guidelines**: The APA and BPS ethical principles include informed consent, right to withdraw, confidentiality, protection from harm, and deception only when justified. Debriefing must follow any deception. These guidelines protect participants but can limit ecological validity.

## Intuition

Think of research methods as the quality control system for psychological knowledge. Reliability is like a weighing scale that gives the same reading every time you step on it. Validity is whether the scale is actually measuring your weight, not something else. Ethics are the safety regulations -- they protect participants but sometimes make it harder to study certain topics naturally.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing reliability with validity. A study can be reliable (consistent results) but not valid (measuring the wrong thing). A bathroom scale that always reads 5 kg too heavy is reliable but not valid.
- Confusing internal and external validity. Internal validity is about whether the study's design actually tests the hypothesis (free from confounds). External validity is about whether findings generalise to the real world.
- Assuming larger samples are always better. Sample size matters less than sampling method -- a large biased sample is worse than a smaller representative one for generalisability.
