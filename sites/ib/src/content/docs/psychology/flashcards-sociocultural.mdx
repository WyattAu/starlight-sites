### Key Concepts

**Social Identity Theory (Tajfel)**: People categorise themselves and others into in-groups and out-groups. In-group favouritism and out-group discrimination arise from the need for positive social identity. Minimal group studies show that even arbitrary group assignments can produce discrimination.

**Conformity**: Asch's line study demonstrated that social pressure can cause people to agree with obviously wrong answers. About 75% of participants conformed at least once. Explanations include normative social influence (desire to fit in) and informational social influence (relying on others' judgement in ambiguous situations).

**Cultural Dimensions (Hofstede)**: Individualism-collectivism (self vs group orientation), power distance (acceptance of hierarchy), uncertainty avoidance (tolerance for ambiguity), masculinity-femininity (achievement vs nurturing values), and long-term orientation (future vs present focus). These dimensions vary across cultures.

**Stereotype Threat**: When individuals are aware of a negative stereotype about their group, their performance can decline because anxiety about confirming the stereotype consumes cognitive resources. Steele and Aronson showed this effect for African American students on academic tests.

## Intuition

Think of the sociocultural level as the "social software" running in our brains. We're wired to belong to groups (SIT), to follow others when uncertain (conformity), and to absorb cultural programming (Hofstede). Stereotype threat is like having a pop-up window in your mind that says "you probably can't do this" -- it drains processing power even when you try to ignore it. Acculturation is like updating your operating system when you move to a new cultural environment.

## Common Pitfalls

- Confusing normative and informational social influence. Normative = going along with the group to be liked or avoid rejection. Informational = genuinely believing the group knows better. Both cause conformity but for different reasons.
- Assuming cultural dimensions are fixed. Hofstede's dimensions describe tendencies, not absolutes. Individuals within any culture vary enormously, and cultures change over time.
- Forgetting that SIT explains both in-group favouritism AND out-group discrimination. The theory isn't just about liking your own group -- it's also about how that affects treatment of others.
