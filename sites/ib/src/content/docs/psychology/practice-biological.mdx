## Intuition

For biological level questions, always consider how biological evidence (brain scans, genetics, neurotransmitters) supports or challenges a particular explanation. Remember that biological determinism (claiming biology completely determines behaviour) is a limitation -- the interactionist approach (nature + nurture) is more nuanced and better supported. Use specific examples like the amygdala in fear conditioning, serotonin in depression, or dopamine in schizophrenia. When evaluating twin studies, consider the equal environments assumption (that MZ and DZ twins share equally similar environments) as a methodological weakness.

**Why it matters**: The biological level of analysis provides the most objective evidence in psychology -- brain scans, genetic studies, and neurotransmitter assays are measurable and replicable. Understanding biological factors helps explain why some disorders run in families and why medications can be effective treatments. These practice questions test your ability to evaluate biological evidence critically.

## Worked Example

**Question**: Discuss the use of brain imaging techniques in investigating the biological basis of depression.

**Answer Structure**: Describe fMRI and PET scans -- these show increased or decreased activity in specific brain regions. Studies have found reduced activity in the prefrontal cortex and increased activity in the amygdala in depressed patients. Evaluate: brain imaging provides objective, visual evidence of brain differences (strength). However, correlation does not prove causation -- different brain activity could be a consequence of depression, not a cause (limitation). Additionally, brain imaging studies often use small samples and may not generalise. The interactionist approach would argue that brain differences interact with environmental triggers (stress, trauma) to produce depression.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing the functions of brain regions. The amygdala (emotion/fear), hippocampus (memory), Broca's area (speech production), and Wernicke's area (speech comprehension) have distinct roles. Mixing them up is a frequent error. Remember: Broca's area = "B for Babbling" (producing speech); Wernicke's area = "W for Words" (understanding meaning).
- Assuming genetic influence means genetic determinism. Having a gene for a trait doesn't guarantee you'll express it -- epigenetics shows that environmental factors can switch genes on and off. Heritability estimates describe population-level variation, not individual outcomes.
- Forgetting that brain imaging studies show correlation, not causation. Different brain activity in a clinical group doesn't prove that brain difference causes the condition -- the relationship could be bidirectional (the condition could cause the brain difference) or there could be a third factor.
- Not evaluating the Minnesota Twin Study critically. While concordance rates are higher for MZ than DZ twins, the study relies on self-report, may have sampling bias (volunteers), and the equal environments assumption may not hold.
