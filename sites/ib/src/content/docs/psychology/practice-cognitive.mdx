## Intuition

For cognitive level questions, focus on how mental processes (memory, thinking, decision-making) explain behaviour. Memory models are hierarchical -- the MSM is the foundation, the WMM extends it. When discussing biases, always provide specific examples of how the bias operates in real life. For schema theory, use studies like Bartlett's to show how schemas affect memory.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing the stores in the multi-store model. Sensory register (very brief, modality-specific), STM (limited capacity, ~18 seconds), and LTM (unlimited, potentially permanent) have very different characteristics. Mixing them up loses marks.
- Assuming schemas are always harmful. They enable rapid information processing -- without them, we'd have to process every situation from scratch. The trade-off is occasional errors when schemas don't fit.
- Confusing proactive and retroactive interference. Proactive = old disrupts new (your old phone number keeps popping up when you try to remember the new one). Retroactive = new disrupts old (learning a new language makes it harder to recall your first language).
