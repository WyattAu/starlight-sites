## Intuition

For sociocultural questions, always consider how social and cultural factors shape behaviour. Don't just describe a theory -- evaluate it with strengths and limitations. Use real-world applications to show understanding: how does SIT explain real-world conflict? How does conformity research apply to real social situations? Cross-cultural studies are especially valuable for showing the limits of Western-centric research.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing normative and informational social influence. Normative = conforming to be accepted (you know the answer is wrong but go along with the group). Informational = conforming because you think the group knows better (you genuinely doubt your own judgement in an ambiguous situation).
- Assuming Hofstede's dimensions apply equally to all individuals within a culture. They describe cultural tendencies, not individual behaviour. There is enormous variation within any culture.
- Forgetting to evaluate research on conformity. Asch's study used artificial tasks with male American students in the 1950s -- ecological validity and generalisability are limited. Modern replications show lower conformity rates.
