## IELTS Terms

**IELTS (International English Language Testing System)**: A standardised test assessing English language proficiency for study, work, or migration, jointly managed by the British Council, IDP, and Cambridge Assessment English.

**Academic IELTS**: The version of IELTS designed for university admissions and professional registration, featuring more complex academic texts and tasks.

**General Training IELTS**: The version of IELTS for work experience, training programmes, or migration, featuring everyday and workplace-related texts.

**Band Score**: IELTS scores range from 0 to 9 in half-band increments, with each band representing a level of English proficiency.

**Band 9 (Expert User)**: The highest IELTS band, indicating full operational proficiency with no errors or misunderstandings.

**Band 7 (Good User)**: A score indicating operational proficiency with occasional inaccuracies and misunderstandings in complex language.

**Band 6 (Competent User)**: A score indicating generally effective use of English with some inaccuracies and misunderstandings.

**Listening Section**: A 30-minute test with four sections of increasing difficulty, featuring conversations, monologues, and academic discussions.

**Reading Section**: A 60-minute test with three passages from books, journals, and newspapers, testing comprehension, inference, and skimming.

**Writing Section**: A 60-minute test with two tasks: describing visual data (Task 1) and writing an essay (Task 2).

**Speaking Section**: An 11–14 minute face-to-face interview with an examiner, comprising three parts covering familiar and abstract topics.

**Task Response**: The IELTS Writing criterion assessing how fully the candidate addresses all parts of the task with a clear position.

**Coherence and Cohesion**: The IELTS Writing criterion assessing logical organisation and the use of linking words and devices.

**Lexical Resource**: The IELTS Writing and Speaking criterion assessing the range and accuracy of vocabulary used.

**Grammatical Range and Accuracy**: The IELTS Writing and Speaking criterion assessing the variety and correctness of grammatical structures.

## TOEFL Terms

**TOEFL (Test of English as a Foreign Language)**: A standardised test measuring English language ability of non-native speakers, administered by ETS.

**TOEFL iBT (Internet-Based Test)**: The most common format of the TOEFL, delivered via the internet with sections for Reading, Listening, Speaking, and Writing.

**TOEFL Essentials**: A shorter, more flexible version of the TOEFL measuring core English skills in a streamlined format.

**Reading Section**: A 35–54 minute section testing academic reading comprehension with passages from university-level texts.

**Listening Section**: A 36–53 minute section testing understanding of lectures, classroom discussions, and conversations.

**Speaking Section**: A 16-minute section with four tasks testing the ability to speak English in academic settings.

**Writing Section**: A 29-minute section with an integrated task (reading, listening, writing) and an independent writing task.

**Total Score**: TOEFL scores range from 0 to 120, with each of the four sections scored from 0 to 30.

**ETS (Educational Testing Service)**: The organisation that develops and administers the TOEFL and GRE tests.

**TOEFL Score Validity**: TOEFL scores are valid for two years from the test date.

**MyBest Scores**: ETS's feature combining the highest scores from multiple TOEFL attempts across all sections.

**Independent Writing Task**: Writing an essay in response to a prompt expressing and supporting an opinion on a given topic.

**Integrated Writing Task**: Writing a response that summarises information from both a reading passage and a listening lecture.

## TOEIC Terms

**TOEIC (Test of English for International Communication)**: A test measuring English proficiency in workplace and business contexts, also administered by ETS.

**TOEIC Listening and Reading Test**: A 2-hour test with 100 multiple-choice questions testing listening comprehension and reading ability.

**TOEIC Speaking and Writing Test**: A computer-delivered test assessing productive English skills in professional contexts.

**TOEIC Score Range**: Listening and Reading scores range from 10 to 990. Speaking scores range from 0 to 200; Writing from 0 to 200.

**Brown Score**: Another term for the TOEIC score, derived from the original developers.

## Cambridge English Exams

**Cambridge Assessment English**: The non-profit organisation producing Cambridge English Qualifications, including KET, PET, FCE, CAE, and CPE.

**KET (Key English Test)**: The entry-level Cambridge exam at CEFR level A2, testing basic English communication.

**PET (Preliminary English Test)**: A Cambridge exam at CEFR level B1, testing everyday English for social and work situations.

**FCE (First Certificate in English)**: A Cambridge exam at CEFR level B2, testing ability to communicate confidently in most everyday situations.

**CAE (Certificate in Advanced English)**: A Cambridge exam at CEFR level C1, testing advanced English for academic and professional settings.

**CPE (Certificate of Proficiency in English)**: The highest Cambridge exam at CEFR level C2, demonstrating near-native English proficiency.

**Grade A, B, C**: Cambridge exams are graded A (exceptional), B (good), or C (adequate) at the relevant CEFR level. Below C is a Level B2 certificate for CAE/CPE.

**CEFR (Common European Framework of Reference)**: An international standard for describing language ability, with levels A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, and C2.

## General Language Testing Terms

**Proficiency Test**: A test designed to measure a person's ability to use a language in real-world situations, rather than knowledge of grammar rules.

**Placement Test**: A test used to determine a learner's current level to place them in an appropriate course or class.

**Diagnostic Test**: A test identifying a learner's strengths and weaknesses to guide teaching and learning.

**Achievement Test**: A test measuring what a learner has mastered after completing a course of study.

**Norm-Referenced Test**: A test where scores are compared against the scores of other test-takers, ranking performance relative to the group.

**Criterion-Referenced Test**: A test where scores are compared against a fixed standard or set of criteria, measuring mastery of specific skills.

**Valid (Validity)**: The extent to which a test measures what it claims to measure, with high validity indicating accurate and meaningful results.

**Reliable (Reliability)**: The consistency of test scores across different occasions, raters, or versions of the test.

**Authenticity**: The degree to which test tasks reflect real-world language use.

**Washback**: The effect that a test has on teaching and learning, influencing what is taught and how it is assessed.

**Backwash**: Another term for washback, referring to the impact of testing on curriculum and instruction.

## Grammar and Vocabulary Terms

**Grammar**: The system of rules governing the structure of language, including word order, tenses, and agreement.

**Syntax**: The arrangement of words and phrases to create well-formed sentences in a language.

**Morphology**: The study of the forms of words, including roots, prefixes, and suffixes.

**Lexis**: The vocabulary of a language, including individual words and their meanings.

**Collocation**: Words that naturally go together, such as "make a decision" rather than "do a decision."

**Register**: The level of formality in language use, ranging from informal (casual speech) to formal (academic writing).

**Discourse**: Connected speech or writing forming a meaningful whole, such as a conversation, lecture, or essay.

**Cohesion**: The use of linking words and devices to connect sentences and create logical flow in a text.

**Inference**: Drawing conclusions from implied information rather than what is directly stated.

**Paraphrasing**: Restating text or ideas in different words while preserving the original meaning.
