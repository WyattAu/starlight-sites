## Qualifications and Structure

**Leaving Certificate**: The state examination taken by students in Ireland at the end of secondary school, typically at age 17–18.

**SEC (State Examinations Commission)**: The body responsible for setting, marking, and certifying the Leaving Certificate exams.

**Ordinary Level (O-Level)**: The standard level of examination, suitable for most students.

**Higher Level (H-Level)**: The more advanced level, required for competitive university courses.

**HC (Higher Certificate)**: Achievement at Higher Level.

**OC (Ordinary Certificate)**: Achievement at Ordinary Level.

**LC (Leaving Certificate)**: The qualification itself, with grades from H1/O1 to H8/O8.

**Junior Certificate**: The examination taken at the end of Junior Cycle (age ~15), a prerequisite for the Leaving Certificate.

**Transition Year (TY)**: An optional year between Junior and Senior Cycle focused on personal development.

**Sixth Year**: The final year of secondary school when the Leaving Certificate is taken.

**Subjects**: Students typically sit 6–7 subjects, with the best 6 counting for their CAO points score.

## Grading and Points

**H1**: 90–100%, 100 CAO points.

**H2**: 80–89%, 88 CAO points.

**H3**: 70–79%, 77 CAO points.

**H4**: 60–69%, 66 CAO points.

**H5**: 40–59%, 56 CAO points.

**H6**: 30–39%, 46 CAO points.

**H7**: 20–29%, 37 CAO points.

**H8**: Below 20%, 0 CAO points.

**O1**: 80–100%, 56 CAO points.

**O2**: 70–79%, 46 CAO points.

**O3**: 55–69%, 37 CAO points.

**O4**: 40–54%, 28 CAO points.

**CAO Points Score**: The total of the best 6 subject grades, used for university admissions.

**Bonus Points for Higher Level Maths**: An additional 25 points are awarded for achieving H6 or above in Higher Level Mathematics.

## Assessment and Exams

**Paper 1**: The first exam paper for a subject, typically covering specific topics.

**Paper 2**: The second exam paper, typically covering different topics.

**Marking Scheme**: The SEC's official guide showing how marks are awarded for each question.

**Coursework**: Practical assessment components in subjects like Construction Studies, Engineering, and Home Economics.

**Project**: Extended practical work assessed as part of certain subjects.

**Oral Examination**: Spoken assessments in modern languages (French, German, Spanish, Irish).

**Aural Examination**: Listening comprehension tests in modern languages.

**Practical Examination**: Hands-on assessments in science, technology, and arts subjects.

**Lab Work**: Practical laboratory exercises assessed as part of science subjects.

## Biology Terms

**Cell Biology**: The study of cell structure, organelles, and membrane transport.

**Ecology**: The study of ecosystems, energy flow, and nutrient cycling.

**Genetics**: The study of inheritance, DNA, and gene expression.

**Plant Biology**: Photosynthesis, transpiration, and plant responses to stimuli.

**Human Body**: Organ systems, homeostasis, and disease.

**DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)**: The molecule carrying genetic instructions.

**Enzymes**: Biological catalysts that speed up chemical reactions.

**Homeostasis**: Maintenance of a stable internal environment.

**Photosynthesis**: Conversion of light energy to chemical energy in plants.

**Respiration**: Release of energy from glucose in cells.

**Immune System**: The body's defence against pathogens.

## Chemistry Terms

**Atomic Structure**: Electron configuration, periodic trends, and isotopes.

**Bonding**: Ionic, covalent, and metallic bonding.

**Stoichiometry**: Mole concept, balancing equations, and yield calculations.

**Acids and Bases**: pH, titrations, and neutralisation reactions.

**Organic Chemistry**: Hydrocarbons, functional groups, and reactions.

**Mole Concept**: The unit of amount of substance, 6.022 × 10²³ particles.

**Enthalpy**: Heat content of a chemical system at constant pressure.

**Equilibrium**: State where forward and reverse reaction rates are equal.

**Electrochemistry**: Galvanic cells, electrolysis, and the Nernst equation.

**Functional Group**: An atom or group of atoms defining the chemical properties of an organic compound.

## Computer Science Terms

**Hardware**: Physical components of a computer system.

**Programming**: Writing instructions for computers to execute.

**Databases**: Organised collections of structured data.

**Networks**: Connected computers sharing resources.

**Algorithms**: Step-by-step procedures for solving problems.

**SQL (Structured Query Language)**: Language for managing relational databases.

**Binary**: The base-2 number system used by computers.

**Boolean Logic**: Logic using true/false values and operators AND, OR, NOT.

**Pseudocode**: Human-readable description of an algorithm.

**Recursion**: A function calling itself to solve smaller instances of a problem.

## Mathematics Terms

**Algebra**: Manipulating symbols and expressions to solve equations.

**Calculus**: Study of rates of change (differentiation) and accumulation (integration).

**Geometry**: Properties of shapes, space, and spatial relationships.

**Trigonometry**: Relationships between angles and side ratios in triangles.

**Probability**: The likelihood of events occurring.

**Statistics**: Collection, analysis, and interpretation of data.

**Sequences**: Ordered lists of numbers following patterns (arithmetic, geometric).

**Series**: The sum of terms in a sequence.

**Functions**: Relations mapping inputs to outputs.

**Limits**: The value a function approaches as the input approaches a point.

## Physics Terms

**Mechanics**: Motion, forces, and Newton's laws.

**Waves**: Wave properties, sound, light, and diffraction.

**Electricity**: Circuits, voltage, current, resistance, and power.

**Heat**: Thermal energy, specific heat capacity, and specific latent heat.

**Modern Physics**: Radioactivity, nuclear reactions, and the photoelectric effect.

**Energy**: The capacity to do work, existing in various forms.

**Momentum**: Product of mass and velocity.

**Electromagnetic Spectrum**: Range of all electromagnetic radiation.

**Nuclear Physics**: Structure of the nucleus, radioactive decay, and nuclear reactions.

**Quantum**: Discrete packets of energy.

## CAO and University Admissions

**CAO (Central Applications Office)**: The organisation processing applications for Irish undergraduate courses.

**CAO Points Score**: Total of the best 6 subject grades, used for course allocation.

**First Preference Points**: The minimum CAO points for entry to a course's first-choice applicants.

**Offer Round**: The scheduled dates when CAO offers are issued to applicants.

**Round 1 (Round A)**: The first offer round, typically in August after results.

**Round 2 (Round B)**: Subsequent rounds for remaining places and changes of mind.

**Deferral**: Postponing university entry for a year after receiving an offer.

**Mature Applicant**: Students applying to university based on work experience or mature years rather than Leaving Certificate results.

**Non-CAO Application**: Direct applications to some institutions (e.g., private colleges, international universities).

**Apprenticeship**: A work-based training programme combining on-the-job training with classroom learning.
