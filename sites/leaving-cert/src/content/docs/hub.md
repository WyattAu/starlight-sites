## Why This Guide Exists

The Leaving Certificate is the state examination taken by students in Ireland at the end of secondary school, typically at age 17–18. It is the primary qualification used for university admissions in Ireland and is recognised by universities across the UK and internationally. Students typically sit 6–7 subjects, with the best 6 counting for their CAO points score.

This hub page links every resource on this site — organised by subject, with study plans and exam strategies tailored to the Irish exam system. Every topic below links to detailed notes, practice questions, flashcards, and diagnostic quizzes.

## Recommended Study Plan

Preparing for the Leaving Cert is a marathon. Here is a structured approach that balances all subjects while building deep understanding.

### Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1–4)

- Study all topic notes for each subject, marking areas of uncertainty
- Complete flashcards daily for active recall
- Aim for one diagnostic quiz per subject per week
- Focus on understanding concepts, not memorising facts

### Phase 2: Consolidation (Months 5–8)

- Revisit weak areas identified by diagnostic quizzes
- Begin practice questions under timed conditions
- Work through past Leaving Cert papers — they reveal the exam's style and marking schemes
- Practise extended-response questions for Biology and Science subjects

### Phase 3: Exam Readiness (Months 9–12)

- Focus almost entirely on practice questions and past papers
- Study the marking schemes — they show exactly what markers look for
- Do full-length timed papers under real conditions
- Use flashcards to maintain recall on well-known topics
- Reserve the last two weeks for light revision and rest

### Daily Routine

| Time | Activity |
| ------ | ---------- |
| Morning | Active recall — flashcards for 20 minutes |
| Afternoon | Topic study — read notes on one new topic |
| Evening | Practice — complete 5–10 practice questions |
| Weekend | Diagnostic quiz + review weak areas |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How should I start using this site?

Take a diagnostic quiz for each subject. This takes about 10–15 minutes per subject and gives you a clear picture of your strengths and weaknesses. Then prioritise studying the topics where you scored lowest.

### How many subjects should I study at once?

Most students take 6–7 subjects for the Leaving Cert. Study no more than 3 subjects per day to maintain focus. Alternate between a science, a mathematics, and a humanity each day.

### Are the practice questions similar to real Leaving Cert exams?

The practice questions are modelled on Leaving Cert-style problems. They test the same concepts and use similar formats, but they are not past papers. Use them alongside official SEC past papers and marking schemes for the best preparation.

### How often should I use the flashcards?

Daily. Flashcards use spaced repetition — each time you get a card right, it appears less frequently. This is the most efficient way to retain factual knowledge. Spend 15–20 minutes each morning.

### What CAO points do I need for university?

CAO points vary by course and institution. Generally, competitive courses require 400–600 points. Each Higher Level grade is worth 5–100 points (H1 = 100, H2 = 88, H3 = 77, etc.). Plan your subject choices strategically to maximise your points.

### How do I manage the workload across so many subjects?

Use a study timetable. Allocate specific time blocks to each subject and stick to them. Prioritise your weakest subjects but do not neglect your strongest ones. Regular revision is more effective than last-minute cramming.
