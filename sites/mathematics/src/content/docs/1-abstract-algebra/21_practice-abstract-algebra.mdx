## Intuition

**Symmetry in mathematics:** Abstract algebra studies symmetry through algebraic structures — groups capture rotational symmetry, rings capture arithmetic operations, and fields capture the structure of numbers. It is the mathematics of "what stays the same when things change."

**Why it matters:** Group theory underpins particle physics (symmetries of fundamental forces), cryptography (elliptic curve groups), and chemistry (molecular symmetry). Understanding algebraic structures reveals deep connections between seemingly unrelated problems.

**The key insight:** A group is like a set of moves in a game — you can combine moves (composition), undo them (inverses), and there is always a do-nothing move (identity). This simple structure captures the essence of symmetry.

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing group and ring axioms:** Groups require only one binary operation with closure, associativity, identity, and inverses. Rings require two operations (addition and multiplication) where addition forms an abelian group and multiplication is associative and distributes over addition. Do not assume multiplication in a ring has inverses — that only holds for division rings and fields.

**Assuming every subgroup is normal:** A subgroup $H \leq G$ is normal only if $gHg^{-1} = H$ for all $g \in G$. In non-abelian groups (like $S_3$), most subgroups are not normal. Always verify the normality condition before applying the quotient group construction or the first isomorphism theorem to a specific subgroup.

**Confusing prime ideals with maximal ideals in non-commutative rings:** In commutative rings with unity, every maximal ideal is prime, but the converse fails. In non-commutative rings, the definitions diverge further and neither implies the other without additional hypotheses. Always check which definition applies to your ring.
