## Intuition

Differential geometry studies the geometry of curved spaces using calculus. A manifold is a space that looks locally like Euclidean space but may be globally curved (like the surface of a sphere). Vector fields assign a tangent vector to each point — think of them as "flows" on the surface. The Riemannian metric measures distances and angles on curved surfaces, and curvature quantifies how much the surface deviates from flat space. The Gauss-Bonnet theorem is a masterpiece: the total curvature of a closed surface depends only on its topology (Euler characteristic).

## Common Pitfalls

- **Curvature sign convention**: Gaussian curvature K is positive for spheres (saddle-shaped curves in both directions), negative for saddles, and zero for planes — but sign conventions vary between authors.
- **Parallel transport on curved surfaces**: Parallel transporting a vector around a closed loop on a curved surface returns a rotated vector — the rotation angle equals the enclosed curvature, not zero as in flat space.
- **Geodesic ≠ straight line**: Geodesics are "straightest" curves on curved surfaces, not straight lines in the ambient space — a great circle on a sphere is a geodesic but curved in 3D.
