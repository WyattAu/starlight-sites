## Intuition

**Geometry of curved spaces:** Differential geometry studies the geometry of surfaces and curves using calculus — it describes how space curves, bends, and twists. It is the mathematics of general relativity (spacetime curvature) and computer graphics (surface rendering).

**Why it matters:** General relativity describes gravity as spacetime curvature, which is fundamentally a differential geometry problem. Computer graphics needs differential geometry to render smooth surfaces realistically.

**The key insight:** The Gaussian curvature of a surface is intrinsic — the answer varies based on only on distances measured on the surface, not on how the surface sits in space. This insight led to Einstein theory of general relativity.

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing the tangent bundle with the cotangent bundle:** The tangent bundle $TM$ consists of tangent vectors (velocity vectors), while the cotangent bundle $T^*M$ consists of covectors (1-forms, linear functionals on tangent spaces). Both have dimension $2n$, but they are different manifolds. The differential $df$ lives in $T^*M$, not $TM$.

**Assuming a closed form is exact without checking topology:** By Poincaré's lemma, every closed form is exact on contractible (directly connected) domains. But on spaces with nontrivial topology (like $S^1$ or $T^2$), closed forms need not be exact. The form $d\theta$ on $S^1 \setminus \{0\}$ is closed ($d(d\theta) = 0$) but not exact — its integral around $S^1$ is $2\pi \neq 0$.

**Misidentifying symmetries of the Riemann curvature tensor:** The Riemann tensor $R_{ijkl}$ satisfies $R_{ijkl} = -R_{jikl}$, $R_{ijkl} = -R_{ijlk}$, and $R_{ijkl} = R_{klij}$, but it does NOT satisfy $R_{ijkl} = R_{ikjl}$. Swapping the second and third indices (interior swap) is not a symmetry. Misidentifying symmetries leads to incorrect simplifications in curvature computations.
