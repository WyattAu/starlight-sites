## Intuition

**The mathematics of vectors and transformations:** Linear algebra is the mathematics of flat things — vectors, lines, planes, and the transformations that map them to each other. It is the language of computer graphics, quantum mechanics, and machine learning.

**Why it matters:** Every data point can be viewed as a vector, every operation as a matrix, and every model as a linear transformation. Understanding linear algebra is essential for machine learning, graphics, and scientific computing.

**The key insight:** Eigenvalues and eigenvectors reveal the "natural directions" of a transformation — the directions that only get stretched, not rotated. This is why PCA (principal component analysis) finds the most important directions in data.

## Common Mistakes

**Forgetting that matrix multiplication is not commutative:** $AB \neq BA$ as a rule. The $(1,2)$ entry of $AB$ is computed from row 1 of $A$ and column 2 of $B$, which is entirely different from row 1 of $B$ and column 2 of $A$. Always compute products in the correct order and never assume $AB = BA$.

**Confusing linear independence with linear dependence criteria:** Having no vector as a scalar multiple of another is necessary but not sufficient for linear independence in dimensions $\geq 3$. For example, $(1,2,3)$, $(2,5,7)$, and $(1,3,4)$ have no pair of scalar multiples but are still linearly dependent because $v_3 = v_2 - v_1$. Always check whether one vector is a general linear combination of the others.

**Mixing up the rank-nullity theorem with dimension counting:** The rank-nullity theorem states $\text{rank}(T) + \text{nullity}(T) = \dim(\text{domain})$, not $\dim(\text{codomain})$. For $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ with rank 2, the nullity is $3 - 2 = 1$, but for $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^5$ with rank 2, the nullity is still $3 - 2 = 1$ because the domain determines the constraint.
