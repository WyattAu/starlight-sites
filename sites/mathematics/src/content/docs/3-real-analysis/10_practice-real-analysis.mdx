## Intuition

**Rigorous calculus:** Real analysis puts calculus on a rigorous foundation — it defines limits, continuity, and derivatives with epsilon-delta precision, removing the hand-waving of introductory calculus. It is where you learn to think like a mathematician.

**Why it matters:** Real analysis is the bedrock of all analysis — it teaches you to construct rigorous proofs, understand convergence, and reason about infinite processes. These skills transfer to every area of mathematics.

**The key insight:** Limits are about "arbitrarily close" — for any precision you demand, the function eventually gets that close. This simple idea, made rigorous, resolves centuries of confusion about infinitesimals and infinite series.

### Integration

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing pointwise and uniform convergence:** A sequence of continuous functions can converge pointwise to a discontinuous function (e.g., $f_n(x) = x^n$ on $[0,1]$). Uniform convergence preserves continuity, but pointwise convergence does not. Always check whether the convergence is uniform before concluding the limit function inherits properties like continuity or integrability.

**Assuming continuity implies differentiability:** The function $f(x) = |x|$ is continuous everywhere but not differentiable at $x = 0$. Continuity is necessary but not sufficient for differentiability. The intermediate value theorem applies to continuous functions, but the mean value theorem requires differentiability on the open interval.

**Confusing conditional and absolute convergence:** A series like $\sum (-1)^n / n$ converges conditionally but not absolutely. Rearranging the terms of a conditionally convergent series can change its sum (Riemann's rearrangement theorem). Always test $\sum |a_n|$ separately when determining convergence properties.
