## Intuition

**Equations that describe change:** ODEs describe how things change — the speed of a falling object, the growth of a population, the oscillation of a spring. They are the language of physics, engineering, and biology.

**Why it matters:** Almost every physical law is expressed as a differential equation — Newton second law, Maxwell equations, Schrodinger equation. Solving ODEs lets you predict the future behaviour of physical systems.

**The key insight:** Most ODEs cannot be solved analytically — numerical methods (Euler, Runge-Kutta) approximate solutions, and the art is balancing accuracy against computational cost.

## Common Mistakes

**Using undetermined coefficients when the guess overlaps the complementary solution:** When solving $y'' + y = 3\cos(x)$, the naive guess $A\cos(x) + B\sin(x)$ fails because $\cos(x)$ and $\sin(x)$ already appear in the complementary solution $y_c = C_1\cos(x) + C_2\sin(x)$. You must multiply the guess by $x$ (or $x^2$ for repeated roots) to obtain a linearly independent particular solution.

**Confusing the integrating factor formula for first-order linear ODEs:** For $y' + P(x)y = Q(x)$, the integrating factor is $\mu(x) = e^{\int P(x)\,dx}$, not $e^{\int Q(x)\,dx}$. The factor multiplies the entire equation to make the left side an exact derivative. Applying it to the wrong term gives an incorrect equation that cannot be integrated directly.

**Forgetting the domain of validity for Laplace transforms:** The Laplace transform $\mathcal{L}\{e^{at}\} = 1/(s-a)$ is only valid for $s > a$. When solving initial value problems, you must track the region of convergence to ensure the inverse transform is well-defined. Ignoring this can lead to incorrect solutions or missing terms.
