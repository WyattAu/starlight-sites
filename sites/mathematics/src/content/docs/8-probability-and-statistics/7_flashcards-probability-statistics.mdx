## Intuition

Probability theory provides the mathematical framework for quantifying uncertainty. The Kolmogorov axioms define probability as a measure on a sample space satisfying non-negativity, normalisation (total = 1), and additivity for disjoint events. Statistics applies these tools to real data — estimating unknown parameters (point estimation), quantifying uncertainty (confidence intervals), and testing hypotheses (p-values, likelihood ratios). The Central Limit Theorem is the bridge: sample means approach a normal distribution regardless of the underlying distribution.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Confidence interval misinterpretation**: A 95% CI does not mean there's a 95% probability the parameter lies in the interval — the parameter is fixed; the interval is random. 95% of such intervals (from repeated sampling) would contain the true value.
- **p-value misconception**: A p-value is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true — it's the probability of observing data as extreme as (or more extreme than) what was observed, assuming the null is true.
- **MLE bias**: Maximum likelihood estimators are not always unbiased — the MLE for variance in a normal distribution underestimates the true variance by a factor of (n-1)/n.
