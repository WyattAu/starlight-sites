# Diagnostics

A diagnostic is not a practice paper. It is a set of questions chosen to find
the *earliest* topic you have a gap in, because that is the one worth repairing
-- everything after it depends on it.

**How to use one.** Work through it in one sitting, without notes and without
looking anything up. Mark each question wrong rather than half-right: a
question you solve by recalling the worked example is not evidence you can
solve it cold. Then work *up* -- from the lowest-numbered wrong answer
downwards. The first topic you cannot do at all is your real starting point,
and the ones you got right below it are secure.

**What a result means.** Every question in a wrong region is expected to fail
if that region is sound, so a failure there means the gap is above it. A
pattern of failures spread evenly across the whole paper usually means the
material has been read but not practised.

## Why the earliest gap is the one to repair

Mathematics is cumulative in a way that is easy to underestimate. A gap in
eigenvalues does not stay a gap in eigenvalues: it becomes a gap in linear
maps, in differential equations, in the spectral theory on the graduate track,
and eventually in a proof you cannot follow for reasons you cannot name.

That is why the instruction above is to work *up* from the lowest wrong answer
rather than to fix whatever looks most important. A student who repairs the
eigenvalue gap in an afternoon has unblocked four later topics; a student who
spends the same afternoon re-reading material they already pass has not.

The reverse is just as common: a student who stops at the first hard question
often concludes they are not a mathematician, when in fact one specific topic
from two years ago was never understood. Diagnostics are useful partly because
they replace that conclusion with a specific one.

## What these do not measure

- **Speed.** Nothing here is timed. Speed is a consequence of fluency, not an
  input to it.
- **Insight.** Every question has a worked method. A question that required
  invention would tell you about research aptitude, which is not what this is
  for.
- **Recall of formulas.** The formulas are the easy part. The diagnostic
  deliberately spends most of its questions on setting up and justifying, since
  that is where the gaps actually are.

If you are preparing for a specific exam rather than auditing foundations, use
the board's own past papers. This section is for finding out whether the
groundwork is solid.
