## Why a course on this site at all

Because the encyclopaedia's strengths are the course's weaknesses. An
encyclopaedia lets you look up the mean value theorem; it cannot tell you
what you needed to read first. A course gives you the order and refuses
to let a chapter assume anything you have not met. The Core Course on
this site is the second kind of object: one sequence, dependency-ordered,
with every prerequisite machine-checked before anything ships. You do not
browse it. You walk it.

## The encyclopaedia: what you will find

The reference tracks cover the undergraduate canon and part of the
graduate shelves, proof-based throughout:

- **Abstract Algebra**: groups, rings, and fields, from cyclic groups to
  Galois theory.
- **Real Analysis**: limits, continuity, and the ε-δ framework.
- **Linear Algebra**: vector spaces, eigenvalues, inner product spaces.
- **Multivariable Calculus**: gradient, multiple integrals, and the
  theorems of Green, Stokes, and Gauss.
- **Complex Analysis**: analytic functions, contour integration, residues.
- **Differential Geometry**: manifolds, curvature, geometric structures.
- **Functional Analysis**: infinite-dimensional spaces and bounded
  operators; the mathematics behind quantum mechanics and PDE theory.
- **Measure Theory, Probability, Topology, Number Theory**: the measure-
  theoretic foundations, the distributions, the qualitative geometry, and
  the arithmetic of the integers.

Every track carries worked examples, common mistakes, and practice
problems with spaced-repetition review.

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing proof-based mathematics with computational mathematics:**
these notes emphasise rigorous proofs and definitions, not formulas and
techniques. A correct answer without a valid proof is insufficient.

**Skipping prerequisite topics:** mathematics is cumulative, and skipping
foundations compounds gaps. This is precisely why the course exists: its
order is checked so that skipping is a choice you cannot make by
accident.

**Treating definitions as interchangeable across fields:** the word
"normal" means different things in group theory, linear algebra, and
topology. Always check which definition applies in the current context.
