## Intuition

**The language of the universe:** Mathematics is the language of the universe — it describes everything from the orbit of planets to the behaviour of subatomic particles. Understanding mathematics gives you the tools to model, predict, and understand the world.

**Why it matters:** Mathematics is not just about numbers — it is about patterns, structures, and logical reasoning. These skills transfer to every field, from computer science to economics to biology.

**The key insight:** Mathematical proof is the ultimate form of certainty — unlike empirical science, which can always be overturned by new evidence, a mathematical proof is true forever. This is why mathematics is the foundation of all exact sciences.

## Why This Matters

Mathematics is the foundation of all quantitative disciplines. These notes provide rigorous, proof-based coverage of undergraduate mathematics — from algebraic structures to functional analysis. Each topic builds on the previous, creating a coherent framework for understanding the mathematical universe.

## What You Will Find

- **Abstract Algebra**: Groups, rings, and fields — the algebraic structures underlying symmetry and number theory. From cyclic groups to Galois theory, abstract algebra reveals the architecture of mathematical objects.
- **Real Analysis**: The rigorous foundations of calculus — limits, continuity, and integration. The ε-δ framework provides the logical basis for all of analysis.
- **Linear Algebra**: Vector spaces, linear transformations, eigenvalues, and inner product spaces. Linear algebra is the most widely applied area of mathematics.
- **Multivariable Calculus**: Partial derivatives, gradient, multiple integrals, and vector calculus. Green's, Stokes', and Gauss' theorems unify differential and integral calculus.
- **Complex Analysis**: Analytic functions, contour integration, and the residue theorem. Complex analysis is the most elegant branch of analysis — differentiability once implies infinite differentiability.
- **Differential Geometry**: Manifolds, curvature, and geometric structures. Differential geometry connects calculus to geometry on curved spaces.
- **Functional Analysis**: Infinite-dimensional vector spaces and bounded operators. Functional analysis extends linear algebra to function spaces, underpinning quantum mechanics and PDE theory.

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing "proof-based" mathematics with computational mathematics:** These notes emphasise rigorous proofs and definitions, not just formulas and techniques. A correct answer without a valid proof is insufficient — you must justify every step using axioms, theorems, or previously established results.

**Skipping prerequisite topics:** Mathematics is cumulative. Abstract algebra builds on set theory and logic; real analysis assumes comfort with sequences and limits; functional analysis requires both linear algebra and real analysis. Skipping foundational material leads to compounding gaps in understanding.

**Treating definitions as interchangeable across fields:** The word "normal" means different things in group theory (normal subgroups), linear algebra (normal matrices), and topology (normal spaces). Always check which definition applies in the current context — they are not the same concept wearing different hats.
