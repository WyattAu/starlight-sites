**The physics of everyday motion:** Classical mechanics describes everything you can see and touch — from thrown balls to orbiting planets. Newton laws are simple but universal: F=ma tells you exactly how forces produce motion, and conservation laws tell you what stays constant.

**Why it matters:** Classical mechanics is the foundation of engineering — bridges, buildings, rockets, and cars are all designed using Newton laws. It is also the starting point for all of physics.

### Additional Flashcard Topics

- **Newton's Three Laws**: (1) F=ma — force equals mass times acceleration, (2) action-reaction — forces come in equal and opposite pairs, (3) inertia — objects maintain their state of motion unless acted upon. These are the foundation of classical mechanics.

- **Lagrangian Mechanics**: L = T - V (kinetic minus potential energy). The Euler-Lagrange equation d/dt(∂L/∂q̇) - ∂L/∂q = 0 gives equations of motion in generalised coordinates. This formulation is powerful for constrained systems.

- **Hamiltonian Mechanics**: H = T + V (kinetic plus potential energy) expressed in terms of coordinates and momenta. Hamilton's equations q̇ = ∂H/∂p, ṗ = -∂H/∂q give first-order equations of motion. Phase space trajectories conserve H.

- **Noether's Theorem**: every continuous symmetry of the action corresponds to a conserved quantity. Time translation symmetry → energy conservation, spatial translation → momentum, rotational symmetry → angular momentum.

- **Central Force Motion**: for forces directed toward a fixed point, angular momentum is conserved. The Kepler problem (F ∝ 1/r²) gives elliptical orbits. The effective potential combines gravitational and centrifugal terms.

## Common Mistakes

**Confusing Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics:** L = T − V uses generalised coordinates and velocities; H = T + V uses coordinates and momenta. Using the wrong formalism gives incorrect equations of motion.

**Forgetting that constraint forces do no work:** In Lagrangian mechanics, constraint forces (tension, normal force) are eliminated by choosing appropriate generalised coordinates. Including them doubles-counts.

**Mixing up active and passive transformations:** Active transformations move the particle; passive transformations change the coordinate system. Confusing them flips the sign of rotation angles.

**Confusing conservative and non-conservative forces:** conservative forces (gravity, springs) have potential energy functions; non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance) do not. Energy conservation only applies to conservative systems.

**Forgetting that the Lagrangian is not unique:** adding a total time derivative dF(q,t)/dt to L does not change the equations of motion. Different Lagrangians can describe the same physics.
