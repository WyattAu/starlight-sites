## Intuition

**The language of motion:** Classical mechanics provides the mathematical framework for describing and predicting motion — from simple projectile motion to complex orbital mechanics. It is the physics you can see and touch, and it is the foundation for everything else.

**Why it matters:** Every engineering discipline uses classical mechanics — civil engineers design bridges, aerospace engineers design rockets, and mechanical engineers design engines. Understanding it is understanding how the physical world works.

**The key insight:** Free body diagrams are the most powerful tool in mechanics — drawing all forces on an object and applying F=ma in each direction reduces every mechanics problem to a system of equations.

<PracticeProblem client:only="solid-js" question={"A particle of mass m slides on a frictionless parabolic wire y = ½ax² under gravity. ## Common Mistakes

**Confusing static and kinetic friction:** Static friction matches the applied force up to μₛN. Kinetic friction is always μₖN. Using the wrong coefficient gives incorrect acceleration.

**Forgetting that Lagrangian L = T − V, not T + V:** The sign convention is critical. L = T − V, not T + V. Using the wrong sign flips the equations of motion.

**Neglecting the centrifugal term in central force problems:** The effective potential V_eff = L²/(2mr²) + V(r) includes the centrifugal barrier. Forgetting this term misses the minimum that gives stable circular orbits.
