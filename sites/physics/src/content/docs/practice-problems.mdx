## Physics Practice Problems

## Intuition

**Physics you can do:** Practice problems turn abstract equations into physical understanding — when you solve for the trajectory of a projectile, you are not just manipulating symbols, you are predicting where a ball will land.

**Why it matters:** Physics is learned by doing, not by reading. Working through problems builds the intuition that lets you approach new situations with confidence.

**The key insight:** The hardest part of physics is not the math — it is translating a physical situation into equations. Practice problems build this translation skill, which is the real payoff of physics education.

## Difficulty Levels

- **Easy:** Basic recall and simple calculations
- **Medium:** Application of concepts and formulas
- **Hard:** Multi-step problems and analysis

## Common Mistakes

**Rushing through problems without drawing free-body diagrams:** FBDs reveal all forces acting on a body. Skipping them leads to missing forces or wrong directions, especially with friction and normal forces.

**Forgetting to convert units before calculating:** Mixing cm and m, or g and kg, gives wrong numerical answers. Always convert to SI base units before plugging into formulas.

**Not checking whether the answer makes physical sense:** A negative kinetic energy, a speed greater than c, or a negative temperature (in Kelvin) indicates a calculation error. Always do a sanity check.
