## Intuition

The SAT Reading and Writing section tests your ability to understand, analyse, and edit texts. Reading questions require you to identify the main idea, find evidence, and make inferences — always return to the passage to verify your answer. Writing questions test grammar conventions, sentence structure, and rhetorical effectiveness — the best answer is the one that is clear, concise, and grammatically correct.

## How to Use These Flashcards

**First pass:** Go through all 20 cards without rating — just flip and read. This familiarises you with the content and identifies weak areas.

**Rating system:** 1 = could not recall, 2 = recalled with difficulty, 3 = recalled correctly, 4 = instant recall. The spaced repetition algorithm shows cards rated 1-2 more frequently.

**Time pressure:** SAT is timed. After mastering the content, practice flipping through cards quickly to build speed and accuracy under pressure.

**Common Pitfalls**

- **Overthinking inferences**: Choosing an answer that goes beyond what the passage supports — SAT inferences must be directly supported by textual evidence, not reasonable guesses.
- **Subject-verb agreement across clauses**: Forgetting that a verb must agree with its subject even when a prepositional phrase separates them — "The list of items IS long," not "are."
- **Redundancy and wordiness**: The SAT favours concise writing — if two choices say the same thing, the shorter one is usually correct. Eliminate unnecessary qualifiers and vague language.
