## General SAT Terms

**Scholastic Assessment Test (SAT)**: A standardized college admissions test developed by the College Board, measuring reading, writing, and math skills.

**College Board**: The non-profit organization that creates and administers the SAT, AP exams, and PSAT.

**Evidence-Based Reading and Writing (EBRW)**: The section of the SAT that tests reading comprehension and writing skills, scored on a 200-800 scale.

**Math Section**: The SAT section testing algebra, problem-solving, and data analysis, advanced math, and additional topics, scored on a 200-800 scale.

**Total Score**: The combined score from the Evidence-Based Reading/Writing and Math sections, ranging from 400-1600.

**Percentile**: The percentage of test-takers who scored at or below a particular score, indicating relative performance.

**Superscoring**: The practice of taking the highest section scores across multiple SAT test dates to create a new total score.

**Test Date**: The scheduled day when the SAT is administered, typically offered in March, May, June, August, October, November, and December.

**Registration**: The process of signing up for the SAT through the College Board website, including selecting a test center and date.

**Accommodations**: Special testing arrangements provided for students with documented disabilities, such as extended time or assistive technology.

## Math: Algebra and Functions

**Linear Equation**: An equation of the form y = mx + b, representing a straight line with slope m and y-intercept b.

**Quadratic Equation**: An equation of the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0, solvable by factoring, completing the square, or quadratic formula.

**Function**: A relation where each input (x-value) has exactly one output (y-value), written as f(x).

**Slope**: The rate of change of a line, calculated as rise over run (Δy/Δx).

**Y-intercept**: The point where a line crosses the y-axis, where x = 0.

**X-intercept**: The point where a line crosses the x-axis, where y = 0.

**Domain**: The set of all possible input values (x-values) for which a function is defined.

**Range**: The set of all possible output values (y-values) that a function can produce.

**Vertex**: The highest or lowest point on a parabola, located at (-b/2a, f(-b/2a)) for quadratic functions.

**Absolute Value**: The distance of a number from zero on a number line, denoted |x|, always non-negative.

**Exponential Function**: A function of the form f(x) = a·bˣ, where b > 0 and b ≠ 1, representing exponential growth or decay.

**Polynomial**: An expression consisting of variables and coefficients, involving only addition, subtraction, multiplication, and non-negative integer exponents.

**Factoring**: The process of breaking down an expression into a product of simpler expressions.

**Quadratic Formula**: x = (-b ± √(b²-4ac)) / 2a, used to solve quadratic equations.

**Discriminant**: The expression b²-4ac under the square root in the quadratic formula, determining the nature of the solutions.

## Math: Problem Solving and Data Analysis

**Mean**: The average of a set of numbers, calculated by summing all values and dividing by the count.

**Median**: The middle value in a sorted list of numbers; if there's an even number of values, it's the average of the two middle numbers.

**Mode**: The value(s) that appear most frequently in a data set.

**Range (Statistics)**: The difference between the largest and smallest values in a data set.

**Standard Deviation**: A measure of how spread out numbers are from the mean; larger standard deviation indicates more variability.

**Probability**: The likelihood of an event occurring, expressed as a number between 0 and 1.

**Conditional Probability**: The probability of an event given that another event has already occurred, written P(A|B).

**Permutation**: An arrangement of objects in a specific order, where order matters.

**Combination**: A selection of objects where order does not matter.

**Rate**: A ratio comparing two quantities with different units, such as miles per hour or cost per item.

**Ratio**: A comparison of two quantities by division, expressing how many times one value contains another.

**Proportion**: An equation stating that two ratios are equal.

**Percent Increase/Decrease**: The change in a quantity expressed as a percentage of the original amount.

**Scatterplot**: A graph displaying data points on a coordinate plane to show the relationship between two variables.

**Line of Best Fit**: A straight line that best represents the data on a scatterplot, used to predict values.

## Math: Additional Topics

**Geometry**: The branch of mathematics dealing with shapes, sizes, and properties of space.

**Area**: The amount of two-dimensional space enclosed within a boundary, measured in square units.

**Volume**: The amount of three-dimensional space occupied by an object, measured in cubic units.

**Pythagorean Theorem**: In a right triangle, a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse.

**Special Right Triangles**: Right triangles with specific angle measures: 45-45-90 (sides: x, x, x√2) and 30-60-90 (sides: x, x√3, 2x).

**Circle**: A set of all points equidistant from a center point, with area = πr² and circumference = 2πr.

**Similar Figures**: Figures with the same shape but different sizes, with corresponding angles equal and corresponding sides proportional.

**Congruent Figures**: Figures that are identical in shape and size, with all corresponding parts equal.

**Coordinate Geometry**: The study of geometric shapes and relationships using the coordinate plane.

**Complex Numbers**: Numbers of the form a + bi, where a is the real part and bi is the imaginary part.

**Imaginary Unit**: The number i, defined as √(-1), where i² = -1.

**System of Equations**: Two or more equations with the same variables, solved simultaneously.

**Inequality**: A mathematical statement comparing two expressions using <, >, ≤, or ≥.

**Absolute Value Equation**: An equation containing an absolute value expression, often having two solutions.

## Reading: Comprehension and Analysis

**Main Idea**: The central point or primary message of a passage.

**Supporting Details**: Facts, examples, or evidence that develop or explain the main idea.

**Inference**: A conclusion drawn from evidence and reasoning, not directly stated in the text.

**Author's Purpose**: The reason an author writes a text, such as to inform, persuade, entertain, or reflect.

**Tone**: The author's attitude toward the subject or audience, conveyed through word choice and style.

**Mood**: The emotional atmosphere created for the reader through the author's descriptions and language.

**Context Clues**: Hints within the text that help determine the meaning of unfamiliar words.

**Connotation**: The emotional or cultural association of a word beyond its literal definition.

**Denotation**: The literal or dictionary definition of a word.

**Figurative Language**: Language that uses words or expressions with a meaning different from the literal interpretation, such as metaphors or similes.

**Metaphor**: A direct comparison between two unlike things without using "like" or "as."

**Simile**: A comparison between two unlike things using "like" or "as."

**Personification**: Attributing human qualities or actions to non-human things.

**Imagery**: Descriptive language that appeals to the senses, creating vivid mental pictures.

**Symbolism**: The use of objects, characters, or colors to represent abstract ideas or concepts.

**Argument Structure**: The organization of a text's reasoning, including claims, evidence, and counterarguments.

**Counterargument**: An argument presented against the main thesis, often addressed and refuted by the author.

**Rhetorical Strategy**: The techniques authors use to convey their message and persuade readers.

**Persuasive Appeal**: Methods of convincing readers: ethos (credibility), pathos (emotion), and logos (logic).

**Evidence Types**: Factual data, expert opinions, statistics, examples, and anecdotes used to support claims.

## Writing and Language

**Subject-Verb Agreement**: The grammatical rule that subjects and verbs must agree in number (singular/plural).

**Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement**: Pronouns must agree with the nouns they refer to in number and gender.

**Parallel Structure**: Using the same pattern of words to show that ideas have equal importance.

**Modifier Placement**: Words or phrases should be placed next to what they modify to avoid confusion.

**Dangling Modifier**: A modifier that doesn't clearly refer to any word in the sentence, creating confusion.

**Active Voice**: A sentence structure where the subject performs the action, generally preferred for clarity.

**Passive Voice**: A sentence structure where the subject receives the action, sometimes used for emphasis or objectivity.

**Comma Splice**: A sentence error where two independent clauses are joined only by a comma (incorrect).

**Run-on Sentence**: Two independent clauses joined without proper punctuation or conjunction.

**Semicolon**: Punctuation used to connect closely related independent clauses or separate items in a complex list.

**Apostrophe**: Punctuation used to show possession or indicate contractions.

**Colon**: Punctuation used to introduce a list, explanation, or quotation after an independent clause.

**Dash**: Punctuation used to set off parenthetical information or for emphasis.

**Transition Words**: Words that connect ideas and show relationships, such as however, therefore, moreover, and consequently.

**Conciseness**: Expressing ideas clearly with no unnecessary words, a key principle in SAT writing questions.

**Precision**: Using specific, accurate language that conveys exact meaning.

**Relevance**: Including only information that directly supports the main point or purpose.

**Sentence Variety**: Using different sentence structures and lengths to improve writing quality and flow.

**Effective Language Use**: Choosing words and phrases that are appropriate for the purpose, audience, and context.

**Data Interpretation**: Reading and analyzing graphs, charts, and tables to draw conclusions and answer questions.
