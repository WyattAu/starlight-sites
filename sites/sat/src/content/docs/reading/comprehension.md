## Common Mistakes

**Choosing answers based on outside knowledge rather than the passage.** The SAT reading section tests what the passage says, not what you already know about the topic. An answer choice that is true in general but not supported by the passage is incorrect. Always find specific textual evidence for your answer before selecting it.

**Confusing inference with explicit statements.** Some questions ask what the passage "implies" or "suggests," which requires drawing a logical conclusion from the text. Other questions ask what is "stated" or "directly supported." Students often select explicit statements for inference questions or make unsupported leaps for explicit questions. Identify the question type before answering.

**Overlooking qualifying language.** Passages often include words like "may," "sometimes," "often," or "in most cases" that limit the scope of a claim. Answer choices that remove these qualifiers and make absolute statements (using "always," "never," "all," or "none") are typically incorrect. Match the strength of the answer to the strength of the passage's claim.
