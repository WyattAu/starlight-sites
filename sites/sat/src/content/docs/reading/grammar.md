## Intuition

Grammar is the infrastructure of communication — like the electrical wiring in a house, you only notice it when something goes wrong. Subject-verb agreement is the matching of the subject to the verb, like making sure the key fits the lock. Pronoun reference is ensuring every "he" and "she" points to the right person — like labelling files so you can find them later. Modifier placement is about keeping related words together — a misplaced modifier is like putting the key to the front door in the kitchen drawer. The SAT tests whether you can spot these structural problems and fix them.

## Common Mistakes

**Subject-verb agreement across intervening phrases.** The subject and verb must agree in number, even when prepositional phrases, relative clauses, or appositives separate them. For example, "The box of chocolates is on the table" uses "is" (singular) because "box" is the subject, not "chocolates." Ignore intervening phrases when identifying the subject.

**Comma splices joining independent clauses.** Two independent clauses cannot be joined by only a comma; this creates a comma splice. Fix it by using a semicolon, a comma plus a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS), or by making one clause a dependent clause. For example, "It was late, we went home" is a splice; "It was late, so we went home" is correct.

**Misplaced modifiers changing meaning.** A modifier should be placed next to the word it modifies. A misplaced modifier creates ambiguity or unintended meaning. For example, "Running quickly, the finish line was reached" implies the finish line was running. The corrected version is "Running quickly, the runner reached the finish line."
